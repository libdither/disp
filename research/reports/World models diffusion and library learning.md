# Denoisers, world models and libraries share one skeleton

Yes, the link is real and can be stated exactly, with one correction to the wording: a denoiser does not output a step. It outputs its best guess of the clean signal (a conditional mean), and the sampler turns that guess into a step. A JEPA (Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture, LeCun's family of models that predict the *embedding* of hidden or future parts instead of their pixels) trained with squared error converges to the same kind of object. So a JEPA world model is one slice of a denoiser: one corruption level, masking instead of Gaussian noise, and a learned target space. Because it has only one slice, it cannot be sampled from. Diffusion and Langevin sampling (noisy gradient ascent on log-probability) do draw a representative state from a model's joint distribution, but only when the model's energy matches how often things actually happen in the data. Denoising-trained models have that property. JEPA energies are built to be minimized, not sampled. The generalized world model you describe already exists in outline: Diffusion Forcing gives every token its own noise level and calls the result an interleaving of "the time axis of the sequence and the noise axis of diffusion" ([Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01392#page=3)), and one such network works as forecaster, joint sampler, policy or inverse model depending on which noise levels you pin. Library learning (automatically extracting reusable functions from a corpus of programs) is the other half of the same "wake-sleep" skeleton. A denoiser is an inverse model trained against a fixed, hand-chosen corruption. Library learning rewrites the prior itself, by compression. A world model compresses dynamics, a library compresses explanations, and the two coincide when an abstraction commutes with the dynamics. No paper found runs library learning over denoising traces. disp already has a hand-built version of every slot but no learned piece, and the cheapest first experiment is to mine its memo cache for reusable patterns and score them by `cold_equiv` (the evaluator's step count with cache hits priced at their original cost).

*How to read this:* a claim with a link is what the linked paper states. A claim tagged **[synthesis]** is the research team's own algebra or inference, which no source states in that form. Repo locations are `path:line` relative to the disp checkout.

## A JEPA predictor is one slice of a denoiser, missing the slices that make sampling possible

Start with what a denoiser actually computes. Train a network to map a noisy `x_t` back to the clean `x_0` under squared error, and the best it can do is the average clean signal consistent with what it sees, `E[x_0 | x_t]`. Under additive Gaussian noise that average has a second reading. Tweedie's formula, which dates to Robbins in 1956 ([Efron 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3325056/#P1)), and Miyasawa's 1961 result ([Kadkhodaie & Simoncelli 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.13640#page=1)) say that the denoiser's correction is proportional to the **score**, the gradient of the log-density of noisy data, i.e. the direction toward more likely data. EDM writes it as `∇_x log p(x; σ) = (D(x; σ) − x) / σ²` and glosses it: "the score function isolates the noise component from the signal" ([Karras et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364#page=3)). Training a denoising autoencoder is therefore the same as matching the score of a smoothed data density ([Vincent 2011](https://gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/diffusion/2011-vincent.pdf#page=1)).

The different things a diffusion model can be trained to predict (the noise ε, the clean `x_0`, the "velocity" v, or a flow-matching vector field) are all fixed linear re-expressions of that one conditional mean. They differ only in how the loss is weighted across noise levels, since every diffusion objective is a "weighted integral of ELBOs over different noise levels" ([Kingma & Gao 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00848#page=1)). The flow-matching field is the average of the per-example fields given the current point ([Lipman et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.02747#page=4)), and in stochastic interpolants the velocity is explicitly a conditional expectation ([Albergo et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.08797#page=13)). The step itself is assembled outside the network. DDIM's update is a weighted sum of the "predicted x0" and a "direction pointing to x_t" ([Song, Meng & Ermon 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02502#page=5)). So **"predicting the denoising direction" = predicting the velocity = predicting the conditional mean**: the same function under known coefficients.

The JEPA side lands in the same place. I-JEPA predicts the embeddings of target blocks from a context block, with targets produced by an EMA (a slowly updated moving-average copy) of the context encoder ([Assran et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08243#page=1)). Its conditioning variable is just "a set of (possibly learnable) mask and position tokens", not a random latent ([I-JEPA p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08243#page=3)). With an L2 loss and the encoders held fixed, the optimal predictor is therefore `E[s_y | s_x, positions]`, a conditional mean in a learned space **[synthesis]**. V-JEPA and V-JEPA 2 switch to L1, and V-JEPA's own analysis says the optimum is then the conditional *median* ([Bardes et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.08471#page=3); [Assran et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09985#page=4)).

Masking itself is a known diffusion corruption, and this is the tightest link in the whole comparison. D3PM introduced "absorbing state" corruption, where tokens turn into a `[MASK]` symbol, and tied it to BERT-style masked language models ([Austin et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03006#page=1)). MDLM shows that masked diffusion training "is a weighted average of masked language modeling (MLM) losses" across mask ratios ([Sahoo et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07524#page=2)), and MD4 gives the continuous-time version ([Shi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.04329#page=1)). RADD supplies the masking counterpart of Tweedie: the score equals the clean-data conditional times a known time-dependent scalar ([Ou et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.03736#page=1)), and masked diffusion models "are instead equivalent to masked models" ([Zheng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02908#page=1)). BERT trains at one fixed 15% mask ratio, while MaskGIT samples ratios during training and lowers them during generation ([Chang et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.04200#page=4)). MAE trains at 75% ([He et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377#page=1)), and DiffMAE says outright that "MAE can be viewed as a single-step patch-conditioned diffusion model" ([Wei et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03283#page=5)). D-JEPA classifies JEPA as masked image modeling ([Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.03755#page=1)).

One piece of algebra makes the hypothesis concrete **[synthesis, derived from Lipman's Eq. 8 with a straight-line path; not tested empirically anywhere found]**. A flow-matching Euler step of size `h` from time `t` moves the state a fraction `h/(1−t)` of the way toward the denoiser's guess. Take one step all the way to the end and the output is exactly `E[x_1 | x_t]`, a one-shot conditional-mean prediction, which is what a JEPA predictor does. Take many small steps, re-asking for the mean as uncertainty shrinks, and the result is a sample instead of an average. **A JEPA predictor is a single-step flow sampler from its corruption level; a diffusion sampler is a JEPA-like predictor queried repeatedly at falling corruption.** In this reading the JEPA's "next latent" corresponds to the denoiser's mean output `x̂_0`, not to the sampler's step.

The architecture matches too. Latent diffusion is literally encoder → denoiser in latent space → decoder, with a frozen pretrained autoencoder ([Rombach et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.10752#page=1)). The exact system you describe, with a self-supervised encoder as the latent, has been built several times: RCG diffuses in a MoCo v3 latent ([Li, Katabi & He 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.03701#page=2)), RAE "uses frozen pretrained representations as the encoder with a lightweight decoder" ([Zheng et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11690#page=1)), and SVG runs on frozen DINO features ([Shi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15301#page=1)). JEDI derives that "the conditional diffusion model is a multi-step stochastic JEPA predictor" ([JEDI 2026, p.21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13013#page=21)). Flow-JEPA swaps one-step latent regression for flow matching and reports **mean planning success rising from 86% to 92% on clean observations and from 67% to 86% on noisy ones** ([F-JEPA 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29029#page=1)).

The table puts the whole family in one template **[synthesis; DiffMAE and JEDI each state one row's link, no source states the table]**. Every method regresses a target `T(y)` from a corrupted view, so its optimum is the conditional mean (L2), median (L1), or full conditional distribution (cross-entropy on one-hot tokens).

| Method | Corruption | Levels trained | Target space | Loss | Sampleable alone? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian diffusion / flow matching | additive Gaussian | all | pixels or frozen VAE | L2 | yes |
| Masked diffusion (MDLM, MaskGIT) | absorbing mask | all ratios | one-hot tokens | cross-entropy | yes |
| MAE / BERT | absorbing mask | one ratio (75% / 15%) | pixels / tokens | L2 / CE | no |
| I-JEPA / V-JEPA | block mask | about one | learned (EMA encoder) | L2 / L1 | no, and needs anti-collapse |
| JEPA world model (DINO-WM, V-JEPA 2-AC) | hide the future, add the action | one horizon | frozen encoder | L2 / L1 | no; plans by search |
| Diffusion Forcing, JEDI, Flow-JEPA | noise on future latents | all levels × time | frozen or learned | L2 / flow | yes |

The analogy breaks in four places. The first is the corruption family: the "residual = σ² × score" identity needs Gaussian noise and an input and output in the same space, and a JEPA has neither, so it has no attached score field **[synthesis; no published theorem either way]**. The second is the target space. A fixed target space cannot collapse; a learned one can, which is why "the main challenge with JEAs is representation collapse" ([I-JEPA p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08243#page=2)) and why LeCun's taxonomy says deterministic generative architectures "cannot collapse" while joint-embedding ones can ([LeCun 2022, p.21](https://web.archive.org/web/20241231120746id_/https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf#page=21)). REPA-E is a natural experiment here: letting the diffusion loss train the VAE "causes the latent space to collapse" ([Leng et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10483#page=1)). That is the JEPA problem showing up inside diffusion. The third is the loss (median vs mean). The fourth, and the one that decides sampling, is **one level vs a family**. A diffusion model learns the conditional mean at *every* noise level, and because Gaussian corruptions nest, those means pin down the scores of every blurred density and hence the data distribution. One conditional moment at one level determines no distribution and offers no path from a simple prior to the data **[synthesis]**. One level is enough for *representation*: a single-noise-level denoising autoencoder reaches 61.5%, about 3% below the multi-level version ([Chen, Liu, Xie & He 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.14404#page=7)). So the noise schedule is a generative necessity, not a representational one. With a multimodal future, mean prediction gives the classic "inherently blurry predictions" ([Mathieu, Couprie & LeCun 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05440#page=1)). JEPA's defence is to train an encoder that drops unpredictable detail, "eschewing the need to predict every detail of y" ([LeCun 2022, p.24](https://web.archive.org/web/20241231120746id_/https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf#page=24)), which is why averaging works for planning but not for generation.

## Langevin draws a representative state only from an energy calibrated to data

Langevin dynamics turns any differentiable energy `E` into a sampler. Take gradient steps downhill on `E` and add Gaussian noise of the right size, and the chain settles into the distribution `p ∝ e^(−E)`. SGLD frames this as a "seamless transition between optimization and Bayesian posterior sampling" ([Welling & Teh 2011](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~teh/research/compstats/WelTeh2011a.pdf#page=1)), and energy-based models use it as their "implicit sample generation procedure" ([Du & Mordatch 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.08689#page=1)). Plain Langevin has a known defect: chains cannot cross low-density gaps, so Langevin "will not be able to correctly recover the relative weights of these two modes in reasonable time" ([Song & Ermon 2019, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.05600#page=4)). Annealing fixes it. Run Langevin on heavily blurred versions of the density first, then sharpen, and "annealed Langevin dynamics recover the relative weights faithfully" ([Song & Ermon 2019, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.05600#page=5)). In continuous time, both the reverse SDE and the deterministic "probability flow ODE" need only the score at each noise level ([Song et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.13456#page=1)). A diffusion model is therefore an amortized annealed-Langevin sampler. Statistical physics describes what the annealing does: the reverse process passes a "speciation" transition, "where the gross structure of data is unraveled", and only later a "collapse" onto individual points ([Biroli et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18491#page=1)). Coarse choices (which mode) are made first, with the right odds.

So yes, diffusion sampling draws a representative state from a world model's joint distribution, but three conditions matter. The first is **calibration**. A temperature knob puts all the existing methods on one dial **[synthesis]**: `p_β(y | x) ∝ exp(−β E(x, y))`. At β → ∞ you get the single best answer, which is what JEPA planners compute: V-JEPA 2-AC plans "by minimizing a goal-conditioned energy function" with the cross-entropy method, CEM, a population-based optimizer ([V-JEPA 2, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09985#page=10)), and LeCun's "Mode-2" planning is optimization against the world model ([LeCun 2022, p.12](https://web.archive.org/web/20241231120746id_/https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf#page=12)). At β = 1 with Langevin you get samples. Diffusion is the β = 1 case made practical, because denoising training calibrates its energies (`−log p_σ`) to the data. JEPA energies get no such guarantee. LeCun calls the JEPA energy "a zero-temperature free energy" and says turning it into a probability "is not at all a necessity" ([LeCun 2022, p.20](https://web.archive.org/web/20241231120746id_/https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf#page=20)). Energy-Based World Models found "a similar loss with and without Langevin dynamics" and dropped the noise ([Gladstone et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.08862#page=7)). Energy-Based Transformers predict by plain gradient descent on the energy ([Gladstone et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.02092#page=1)). A 2026 latent-action study reports that "sampling approaches start to break down when handling continuous latents" ([2026, p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.05230#page=19)). Non-contrastive JEPA training only has to push energy down on data and avoid collapse, so nothing forces `e^(−E)` to match data frequencies **[synthesis]**. Running Langevin on an off-the-shelf V-JEPA energy would produce *something*, but nobody has checked whether it is representative. That test is open.

The second condition is **joint vs conditional**. A one-step model `P(s′ | s, a)` defines a joint over trajectories only once you add a start-state distribution and a behaviour policy, `p(s_1) Π π(a_t | s_t) P(s_{t+1} | s_t, a_t)` **[synthesis]**. A diffusion model over whole trajectories learns the joint directly, under whatever policy collected the data. Diffuser makes this the selling point: "sampling from the model and planning with it become nearly identical" ([Janner et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.09991#page=1)), with planning as sampling from a reweighted joint `p̃(τ) ∝ p(τ) h(τ)` ([Janner et al. 2022, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.09991#page=3)). That is Levine's "control as inference", where conditioning on optimality variables `p(O_t = 1 | s_t, a_t) = exp(r)` gives the trajectory posterior ([Levine 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.00909#page=3)). The guided score is simply the prior score plus the gradient of `log h` **[synthesis]**. The joint model contains the one-step model (pin the past and denoise the next state), but not the other way round: building a joint from a one-step model needs rollouts, and errors compound. That is the stated motivation for DWM's "long-horizon predictions in a single forward pass" ([Ding et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03570#page=1)).

The third condition is **mode vs typical sample**. Minimizing gives the single likeliest prediction, which for a car that may turn left or right can be an atypical average or one arbitrary branch. Annealed sampling picks a branch with roughly the right probability **[synthesis]**. Both camps use the same object at different temperatures. Navigation World Models, co-authored by LeCun, uses a diffusion simulator but plans by minimizing an energy with CEM ([Bar et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.03572#page=5)). The real disagreement between "generative" and JEPA world models is two separable choices **[synthesis]**: which space (pixels or VAE latents vs abstract embeddings) and which inference (sample vs minimize). Diffusion world models pick (pixels, sample), JEPA picks (abstract, minimize), NWM picks (pixels, minimize), and JEDI-style latent diffusion aims at the fourth corner (abstract, sample).

## Time and noise are two axes of one generalized world model

The generalized world model you describe, one that does both physical prediction and denoising prediction, is already the organizing idea of the 2024–2026 "forcing" literature, though no paper names it in those words. Diffusion Forcing trains a model to "denoise a set of tokens with independent per-token noise levels" ([Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01392#page=1)). It describes the result as one that "interleaves the time axis of the sequence and the noise axis of diffusion", with Bayesian filtering along time and diffusion along an uncertainty axis ([p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01392#page=3)). The trick that lets one network be both predictor and denoiser is that "noising tokens is a form of partial masking" ([p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01392#page=2)). A clean past with a fully noised next token *is* next-token prediction. Unified World Models takes the same idea across modalities, where "independent diffusion timesteps govern each modality" ([Zhu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.02792#page=1)), so that "the model captures all combinations of marginal and conditional distributions" ([UWM p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.02792#page=2)). One network reads out as policy, forward dynamics, inverse dynamics or video generator.

Formally, give each element of a sequence its own noise level and learn the score for every noise vector. Then each familiar model is a point or path on that grid **[synthesis, consistent with Diffusion Forcing and UWM]**:

| Noise pattern over (past, next, future) | What you get |
|---|---|
| clean, being denoised, fully noised | one-step world model `P(x_{t+1} \| x_{≤t})` (GameNGen, Genie 2, Dreamer 4) |
| same level everywhere | full-sequence joint diffusion (Diffuser, DWM, video models) |
| noise rising with time | rolling / FIFO schedules: the far future stays vaguer ([Ruhe et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09470#page=1); [FIFO-Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.11473#page=1)) |
| observations clean, actions noised (or the reverse) | policy, forward dynamics, inverse dynamics (UWM) |
| any of the above plus guidance | planning |

Under this view, "physical prediction" moves along the time axis and "denoising prediction" moves along the noise axis. A JEPA world model is the zero-noise slice, read in an abstract embedding and run at zero temperature: it moves along time, never along noise **[synthesis]**.

Stochastic interpolants show that the two kinds of move are the same operation. Interpolants "bridge any two probability density functions exactly in finite time" ([Albergo, Boffi & Vanden-Eijnden 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.08797#page=1)). Denoising is transport from Gaussian noise to data. Probabilistic forecasting is transport that "maps a point mass centered at the current state onto a probabilistic ensemble of forecasts" ([Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.13724#page=1)). The same velocity regression handles both. Physical time picks *which two distributions* to connect, and an auxiliary "noise time" parameterizes the path between them **[synthesis]**. DYffusion is the case where the two clocks are the same: its diffusion steps are physical time steps ([Cachay et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.01984#page=1)).

One asymmetry explains why forecasters still carry a separate noise axis **[synthesis]**. Along the noise axis the forward process is known and trivial, and only the reverse is learned. Along physical time, the forward process *is* the unknown dynamics. The noise axis supplies a tractable dimension along which the unknown next state can be sampled. GenCast runs a conditional diffusion model at each 12-hour step for 15-day ensemble forecasts ([Price et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.15796#page=1)). PDE-Refiner runs a denoising loop inside each physical step to recover the high frequencies a one-shot predictor neglects ([Lippe et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.05732#page=1)). GameNGen stays stable only "via noise augmentation" of its context frames ([Valevski et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.14837#page=2)). Dreamer 4's world model "is based on the paradigm of diffusion models" ([Hafner, Yan & Lillicrap 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.24527#page=3)). Genie 2 is "an autoregressive latent diffusion model" ([DeepMind 2024](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-2-a-large-scale-foundation-world-model/#model-architecture)), and NVIDIA's diffusion world foundation models "are latent diffusion models" ([Cosmos 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03575#page=19)).

The noise axis also behaves like a *scale* axis, which ties it to coarse-graining. Wavelet score models factor generation into conditionals across scales ([Guth et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.05003#page=1)). For natural images, diffusion performs "approximate autoregression in the frequency domain" ([Dieleman 2024, a blog post](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html#main)). High-level features are fixed abruptly in the reverse process while details evolve smoothly ([Sclocchi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16991#page=1)). One conceptual paper reads the renormalization group (physics' coarse-graining flow) as a diffusion and its inverse as a generative model ([Sheshmani et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17090#page=1)); its empirical content is thin. The resulting picture **[synthesis]**: denoising predicts fine scales from coarse ones at fixed time, world modelling predicts the next time at some scale, a JEPA is a world model at one coarse scale chosen by its encoder, and LeCun's hierarchical JEPA ([LeCun 2022, p.2](https://web.archive.org/web/20241231120746id_/https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf#page=2)) is a world model at a few discrete scales. The limit is that noise ≈ scale holds for Gaussian noise on data whose spectrum decays, like natural images. In symbolic domains, noise should be read as "how much has been masked", which matters for disp below.

Two caveats are open. No theorem guarantees that the conditionals one network learns across all noise vectors fit together into a single joint distribution. And long-rollout drift is still handled by tricks such as noise augmentation and history guidance ([DFoT 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06764#page=1)), not by any proven optimal scheme. The strongest steelman for JEPA is cost, not principle: DINO-WM notes that "predicting in pixel space, however, is computationally expensive", including "the overhead of using diffusion models" ([Zhou, Pan, LeCun & Pinto 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.04983#page=2)). The two camps are converging. JEDI is "the first online end-to-end latent diffusion world model", learning future latents by denoising "rather than relying on reconstruction" ([JEDI 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13013#page=1)). Equilibrium Forcing keeps Diffusion Forcing's per-frame noise grid but drops noise conditioning in favour of one implicit energy landscape ([Lillemark, Rojas et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14706#page=1)), building on Equilibrium Matching's "samples are obtained by gradient descent on the learned landscape" ([EqM 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02300#page=1)), which is LeCun's energy language arrived at from the diffusion side.

## Library learning learns the prior that a denoiser takes as given

The common skeleton is wake-sleep. Hinton and colleagues train a generative model and a recognition model (an inverse that maps data back to its explanation). The recognition model learns on "fantasies" sampled from the generative model, which "provide an unbiased sample" of it ([Hinton et al. 1995, p.4](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~hinton/absps/ws.pdf#page=4)). DreamCoder, the reference library-learning system, inherits this "in the spirit of the Helmholtz machine" ([Ellis et al., p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.08381#page=5)). Its learned library "defines a generative model over programs and tasks", and a recognition network "learns to invert this generative model" ([DreamCoder p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.08381#page=4)). The abstraction-sleep phase, which invents new library functions, is "equivalent to minimizing the description length" of library plus refactored programs ([p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.08381#page=7)). That objective is MDL (minimum description length: pick the model that makes model-plus-data shortest). The dream phase samples programs from the library, executes them to make tasks, and trains the recognizer, on "a 50/50 mix of replays and fantasies" ([p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.08381#page=8)). There is one twist: DreamCoder re-solves each dreamed task with search and trains toward the best program found, "we train Q to perform MAP inference" ([p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.08381#page=7)). That makes it self-distillation of search rather than plain joint sampling.

Lay diffusion over this skeleton and the correspondence is direct **[synthesis; structural, no published equivalence proof]**. A fantasy corresponds to a synthetic noisy sample, the recognition model to the denoiser, and a replay to real data pushed through the noise kernel. Diffusion training is "replay only": every pair starts from a real `x_0`, and the generative side is just the fixed, hand-chosen corruption. That spares diffusion the fantasy-mismatch flaw Hinton names, "the sleep phase optimizes the recognition weights for fantasy data" ([p.4](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~hinton/absps/ws.pdf#page=4)), but diffusion never learns building blocks. **A denoiser is the recognition half trained against a fixed corruption; library learning is the step that rewrites the generative half.** Inference compilation is the same pattern for probabilistic programs, an inverse network "trained with an infinite stream of training data sampled from the generative model" ([Le, Baydin & Wood 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.09900#page=2)).

Why a library counts as a world model at all: prediction and compression are the same thing. "Predictive models can be transformed into lossless compressors and vice versa", and Chinchilla 70B, a text model, compresses ImageNet patches to 43.4% and LibriSpeech audio to 16.4% of raw size ([Delétang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.10668#page=1)). In MDL terms, "the more we are able to compress the data, the more" we have learned ([Grünwald 2004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0406077#page=5)). A library sets the code length of every program, so adding an abstraction that captures reused structure raises the prior probability of the world's typical structure. **Learning a library is learning a better generative model over explanations** **[synthesis]**. A second link runs through generalization. The exact empirical-optimal denoiser has a closed form, a weighted average of training images ([EDM p.23](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364#page=23)), and "diffusion models that learn the ideal score function can only memorize" ([Kamb & Ganguli 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.20292#page=2)). Real denoisers generalize because of architectural bias, a "geometry-adaptive harmonic" basis ([Kadkhodaie, Guth, Simoncelli & Mallat 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02557#page=1)). The same holds for any squared-error regressor, JEPA predictors included **[synthesis]**. The objective alone memorizes, and the prior does the generalizing. A library is that prior made explicit and inspectable.

So can the generalized world model be framed as library learning? Partly **[synthesis]**. Let the library be the prior over two kinds of explanation: "physical" programs that map `(s, a)` to `s′`, and "repair" edit sequences that map a corrupted `s` back to a clean one. Compress both corpora under one MDL objective, and shared sub-programs (object types, laws, canonical edits) become the abstractions. There is one trap. Physical regularities come from the world and are worth compressing. Repair regularities partly mirror the noise kernel the designer picked, so a library learned from repair moves picks up the kernel's design choices.

The pieces exist separately. Program world models write the transition function as code: WorldCoder "builds a Python program representing its knowledge of the world" ([Tang, Key & Ellis 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.12275#page=1)), and PoE-World uses an "exponentially-weighted product of programmatic experts" whose experts "generalize to new game levels" ([Piriyakulkij et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.10819#page=2)). TheoryCoder-2 is the only one that does library learning in the strict sense. It keeps "a library of abstract states and actions" as PDDL files (a planning language of predicates and operators) and grows it "through a curriculum" ([Ahmed et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00929#page=3)). The 2026 ARC-AGI-3 coding agents refactor toward simplicity within one game but "do not perform formal MDL optimization or cross-task library learning" ([Rodionov 2026b](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15439#page=4)), and the benchmark protocol isolates games, so cross-environment reuse cannot even be measured there. Tree diffusion is the closest thing to a denoiser over programs: "we randomly make small mutations to the syntax trees of programs" and "train a conditional neural model to invert these small mutations" ([Kapur, Jenner & Russell 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.20519#page=3)), on programs drawn from a fixed grammar. It handles "no variable binding, loops" ([p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.20519#page=9)) and has no library. **No paper found learns a library from denoising traces, or uses a learned library as a program-diffusion model's vocabulary.** One candidate design **[synthesis, untested]**: log the denoiser's edits and compress the edit corpus with Stitch, a fast top-down compressor where "a good abstraction is one which minimizes the size of the corpus" ([Bowers et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.16605#page=3)). Frequent multi-step repairs would become macro-rewrites, much as Ruler infers rewrite rules by enumeration ([Nandi et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.10436#page=1)).

The relationship has a real tension. MDL keeps whatever compresses the corpus. Reinforcement learning's abstractions keep only what matters for behaviour: bisimulation metrics (a distance under which states that act alike count as close) ([Ferns, Panangaden & Precup 2004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.4114#page=1)), value equivalence, where models are equal if they "yield the same Bellman updates" ([Grimm et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.03506#page=1)), and MuZero, which drops any "requirement for the hidden state to capture all information necessary to reconstruct the original observation" ([Schrittwieser et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.08265#page=2)). Tycho shows the gap on ARC-AGI-3: transition match shows whether a simulator reproduces dynamics, "not whether it has identified the objective or whether consulting it improves the next action" ([Tycho 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28287#page=1)). The library side has its own warning. In LLM theorem-proving agents, "function reuse is extremely infrequent", and the gains come from "self-correction and self-consistency" ([Berlot-Attwell, Rudzicz & Si 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.20274#page=1)). A LEGO-Prover case study finds "no evidence of the direct reuse of learned lemmas" ([2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.03048#page=1)). Human data argues the objective should look forward, since behaviour "cannot be captured by existing retrospective compression-based algorithms" ([Hernandez Cano et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09985#page=1)). The relationship, stated plainly: **a world model is the predictive half and a library is the generative prior, both are the "model" part of a two-part code, and they coincide exactly when the abstraction commutes with the dynamics.** TheoryCoder-2's predicates-plus-operators library is that coincidence built. Learned options (temporally extended actions) under an MDL penalty "on the description length of the skills" ([LOVE 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04590#page=1)) are the action-side version.

**The inverter demo is a worked JEPA-style coarse-graining.** Its table has a fine level (voltages plus shot noise), a lens (a wire reads as 1 above half the supply), a coarse step rule, a domain ("Gain above 1 ... noise well inside the margin") and an error bound with two numbers (`research/coarse-graining/inverter-chain.html:98-103`). "One step" is the commuting square: "read the fine state, take one coarse step, compare with reading after the fine step". "Run alone" is open-loop simulation from the input. These are exactly the two errors that matter for a latent world model: encode-then-predict vs predict-then-encode consistency, and rollout drift **[synthesis]**. The page observes that "State commutes almost exactly here, time does not", and that a register "resets" accumulated drift (`research/coarse-graining/inverter-chain.html:105`). A register is a denoise step interleaved with prediction, the same job noise augmentation does in GameNGen **[synthesis]**. Gain above 1 is a literal restoring force: bits exist only where the dynamics contract toward attractors. The soft-judge essay finds the same structure in its mean-field magnet `m = tanh(m/(4T))`, which has "two committed selves" only below `T = 1/4` (`research/NATURE_OF_AGENTS.md:173-185`). That symmetry breaking is the same kind of event as diffusion's "speciation" transition, where a discrete choice appears as noise falls **[synthesis]**.

**disp's denoisers are exact projections, which is unusual and useful.** Reduction to normal form with hash-consing gives the canonical member of a reduction class, but not of a behaviour class: `dbl_step` and `dbl_add` are different trees that `eq_by_induction` proves equal (`README.md:113-128`, synced from `lib/tests/by_example.test.disp:124`). Behaviour-level canonicalizers are user-chosen projections onto a representative. The confirmer (disp's symbolic checker over trees with holes, `lib/confirm.disp:2-4`) defines them as quotients:

```disp
Parity := QuotC(NatR, parity)
All := QuotC(NatR, always_zero)
```
(`lib/confirm.disp:63-64`)

`respects_nf` checks that a function agrees with its normalizer by pairing "the class representative with the member" (`lib/confirm.disp:630-640`), and the confirmer tests pin both a proved and a refuted case (`lib/tests/confirmer.test.disp:247-249`). A diffusion denoiser is the soft, learned version: it projects toward high-density representatives instead of a fixed `nf` **[synthesis]**. What disp offers that no ML denoiser has is a gate. A learned or searched normalizer could be accepted only when `respects_nf` proves it, which would make it a verified discrete denoiser.

**For a tree language, the natural noise axis is hole density, not Gaussian noise** **[synthesis]**. The confirmer's partly-known values are trees with named holes (`lib/confirm.disp:2-4`), and the optimizer design fills holes from candidates "ordered by a prior — size, library frequency, an LLM's logits" (`research/OPTIMIZER.typ:616-629`). That is absorbing-state corruption. MDLM's result says a hole-filler trained across *all* hole densities, with the right weighting, is already a sampleable generative model, and RADD says it needs no time input. Tree diffusion's random subtree mutations are the other option, but they are a hand-picked kernel. The repo's constrained-search survey already has the guidance term: masking is "a Doob transform with the wrong h", and Φ, "the probability that a random completion type-checks", is at once the sampling correction, the SMC twist and the value function (`research/CONSTRAINED_PROGRAM_SEARCH.md:45-51`). Its target `p(y | A = 1) ∝ p₀(y)·A(y)` (`research/CONSTRAINED_PROGRAM_SEARCH.md:187-191`) has the same shape as Diffuser's `p̃ ∝ p·h`, with the type checker as `h`, and Φ plays the role classifier guidance plays in diffusion **[synthesis]**. The tempered posterior `score = gate × exp(−β·cost)` (`archive/one-offs/SINGULAR_LEARNING_THEORY.md:294`) is the same β dial as the Langevin section: β → ∞ is the optimizer's argmin, β = 1 is sampling. The strand lattice's thermal mode, where "every move is proposed as often as its reverse" with a per-site heat bank and a forward drive (`evaluators/rust-ic-strands/README.md:1562-1594`), is a physical Metropolis-style sampler **[synthesis]**. Whether it satisfies detailed balance, or whether the drive deliberately breaks it, was not checked.

**The memo snapshot is a library before the abstraction step.** Its entries are ground facts, "`apply(f,x) = r` under the fixed rules", each storing its cold cost and its "FRESH-FOREST PARENT" (the call that first computed it) (`evaluators/rust-eager/crate/src/snapshot.rs:1-23`). Only facts costing at least `DISP_MEMO_MIN_COST` steps, 1000 by default, are kept (`test/disp.test.ts:147-149`). The stored cold cost is literally the "cached depth" the abstraction essay says a good library entry holds: "expensive-to-rediscover, cheap-to-reuse" (`research/THE_NATURE_OF_ABSTRACTION.typ:117`). What it lacks is DreamCoder's sleep-abstraction: no variables and no anti-unification (finding the most specific pattern that covers several terms). The essay already plans that step as corpus MDL modulo licensed relations with babble-style anti-unification (`research/THE_NATURE_OF_ABSTRACTION.typ:269-282`). One design tension is worth noting: the optimizer design calls memo the "degenerate, zero-intelligence corner" of rewriting and keeps it out of the optimizer's substrate so that cost stays attributable per candidate (`research/OPTIMIZER.typ:189-215`).

In order of how cheaply they can be tested, the concrete experiments are these (all unbuilt). First, **memo-as-library**: mine the fresh-forest parent tree for recurring `(f, x)` shapes, anti-unify them into schematic candidates, and score each by its `cold_equiv` delta on one fixed workload. Per `CLAUDE.md:19`, deltas against one cache are reliable even when absolute values carry a few percent of bias. Report reuse counts on held-out files as well, since accuracy gains without reuse are the "Library Learning Doesn't" failure, which the repo's notes already flag (`research/constrained-program-search/typed-enumerative-synthesis.md:212-213`). Second, port the inverter demo's "one step vs run alone" errors to `growth.disp` fits or to the lattice-to-net projection. Third, search for normalizers and gate them with `respects_nf`. Fourth, run SMC over the `Trees` stream with `honest` as the potential, where a refutation zeroes a particle and "Open" leaves its weight alone (`research/CONSTRAINED_PROGRAM_SEARCH.md:51`). Fifth, run the soft-judge temperature sweep and the size-vs-step price sweep (`research/NATURE_OF_AGENTS.md:234-235`) as disp's noise schedule, looking for the points where committed abstractions appear. Sixth, under the residue framing, where a stage that forgets becomes a bijection `A ≅ B × R` once it returns what it forgot (`research/REVERSIBLE_PIPELINE.md:10-17`), run reduction backward from an answer with a sampled residue. That would be a disp-native reverse process that generates programs in the answer's fibre, testable on the lattice's `resid=1` mode (`evaluators/rust-ic-strands/README.md:1681`).

## Conclusion

The three ideas sort cleanly by which piece is learned and which is fixed by hand. Diffusion learns the inverse model and fixes both the corruption and the target space. JEPA learns the target space but trains only one slice of the inverse. Library learning learns the prior and leaves the inverse to search. A generalized world model in the full sense learns all three: an encoder that picks the coarse scale, a predictor trained over the whole time × noise grid, and a structured prior that supplies the generalization squared-error regression cannot. The 2024–2026 literature has merged the first two. Nobody has attached the third, and that is the open slot. It also changes what "generalization" means here. If an ideal denoiser only memorizes and all transfer comes from inductive bias, then for disp the stdlib is not just convenience. It decides whether any learned denoiser over trees generalizes or memorizes, which gives concrete content to the essay's line that stdlib quality is "the optimizer's asymptotic search budget" (`research/THE_NATURE_OF_ABSTRACTION.typ:196`).

For disp, the transferable lesson is to pick the noise process native to trees. Holes are absorbing-state masking, and the masked-diffusion results say a hole-filler trained across every hole density is already a sampler, with the type checker's Φ as its guidance term. disp is also unusually well placed to measure its library before learning one. The memo snapshot already stores per-fact cold cost and provenance, and `cold_equiv` already prices reuse exactly. So the first experiment (mine the cache, anti-unify, score by `cold_equiv` delta, count held-out reuse) answers the field's main criticism of library learning with numbers rather than accuracy anecdotes.
