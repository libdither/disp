# Constrained decoding for language models: syntactic and semantic constraints applied token by token (state as of October 2026)

Scope note for the report writer: the system being compared is disp, a dependently typed language on Barry Jay's tree calculus where a type is an ordinary program that answers yes/no about a value, type checking runs as a stream of cheap steps under a step budget, and the end goal is an optimizer that treats "type checker returns 1" times a cost score as the objective of a combinatorial search over programs. The survey question is constraint-driven generation and program search under hard formal constraints (grammar, scope, types) plus soft learned guidance, when the exact constraint oracle is undecidable or expensive.

Every link below was fetch-verified on 2026-10-09 with `scripts/verify-source.sh -q '<quote>'`; the `#page=N` / `#anchor` fragments are the ones the script minted. Quotes are verbatim from the fetched text. Results older than 2025 are marked with their year. Two classic sources (Knuth 1965, Earley 1970) could not be verified and are listed at the end; nothing above links to them.

Vocabulary used throughout: "mask" = the per-step boolean vector over the LLM vocabulary saying which next tokens are allowed; "prefix oracle" = the procedure that decides whether a partial output can still be completed to something valid; "safe pruning" = the oracle never rejects a prefix that has a valid completion (soundness of rejection); "dead-end freedom" = every prefix the oracle accepts really has a completion (no stuck states).

## Q1. Grammar-constrained decoding engines: mechanism, per-token cost, tokenizer alignment, measured overhead

### Takeaway
All production engines compute a per-token mask from a character-level grammar by walking the vocabulary as a trie against an automaton or parser state. Three mechanism families exist: finite automata with a precomputed vocabulary index (Outlines, Koo et al.), LR(1)/LALR with an offline DFA-state-to-mask table (SynCode), and Earley or pushdown parsers with a context-independent-token cache (llguidance, XGrammar, XGrammar-2). Pure-grammar per-token cost is now a few to a few tens of microseconds (XGrammar under 40 µs for JSON; llguidance ~50 µs average, 1.5 ms uncached; Koo "a few microseconds"), i.e. below a GPU forward pass, so end-to-end overhead is "near zero" for grammars that fit the precomputation. The remaining costs are offline (seconds to minutes for precomputation) and the long tail (tens to hundreds of ms when an input doesn't fit the cache). Token/character misalignment is handled by subword-aligned grammars ("subterminals", FST composition), and getting it wrong measurably hurts accuracy (DOMINO: 41.5% to 30.8% on GSM8K with naive masking). A 2026 theory paper shows masks depend only on the language, not the grammar, and that a sound, parse-preserving online engine must spend Ω(t²) per token on some CFG families.

### Cited Findings

**Outlines / "Efficient Guided Generation" (Willard & Louf, 2023, arXiv 2307.09702)**
- Mechanism and cost: the regex is compiled to an FSM and the vocabulary is indexed once by FSM state; "The result is an algorithm that costs O(1) on average." — [arXiv PDF p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.09702#page=2). The abstract adds that the index "can also be extended to CFGs" (same PDF, p.2 region).
- Startup cost, as characterized by a competitor: "Outlines builds an automaton from constraints and then pre-computes token masks for all automaton states", which the llguidance README says introduces "significant startup cost and memory overhead" — [llguidance README, comparison section](https://github.com/guidance-ai/llguidance#comparison-and-performance).

**llguidance (Microsoft/guidance-ai; README, read 2026-10-09)**
- Mechanism: "The library implements a context-free grammar parser using Earley's algorithm on top of a lexer based on derivatives of regular expressions." Mask computation "is achieved by traversing the prefix tree (trie) of all possible tokens". — [README #technical-details](https://github.com/guidance-ai/llguidance#technical-details)
- Per-token cost: "Mask computation takes approximately 50μs of single-core CPU time for a tokenizer with 128k tokens." and "There is no significant startup cost." — [README #technical-details](https://github.com/guidance-ai/llguidance#technical-details)
- Uncached vs. average: "the full mask computation for a typical JSON schema takes about 1.5ms (for 128k tokenizer)", but with the "slicer" optimization the average over JSON Schema Bench (2.5M tokens, 10k schemas) "is under 50μs, with less than 1% of masks taking longer than 1ms, and 0.001% taking longer than 10ms (but still shorter than 30ms)". The README's throughput claim: "with 16 cores and a 10ms forward pass, llguidance can handle batch sizes up to 3200 without slowing down the model." — [README #comparison-and-performance](https://github.com/guidance-ai/llguidance#comparison-and-performance)
- On XGrammar's precomputation strategy (competitor's characterization): "The pre-computation often runs into seconds, and sometimes minutes"; when the precomputation fits, masks come "under 8μs in half of masks we tested", otherwise "the mask computation times can run to tens or hundreds of milliseconds." — [README #comparison-and-performance](https://github.com/guidance-ai/llguidance#comparison-and-performance). These are self-reported by one vendor about another.

**Guidance (guidance-ai/guidance README)**
- Guarantee is conditional on backend support: "It can ensure that the output conforms to any context free grammar (so long as the backend LLM has full support for Guidance)." — [README #guarantee-output-syntax-with-constrained-generation](https://github.com/guidance-ai/guidance#guarantee-output-syntax-with-constrained-generation). The README as fetched does not describe token healing (see Gaps).

**XGrammar (Dong et al., MLSys 2025, arXiv 2411.15100)**
- Cache split: "context-dependent tokens account for only a minor proportion, amounting to less than 1% (1134 out of 128k) for the Llama-3.1 model using JSON grammar." Context-independent tokens are precomputed into an adaptive token mask cache; context-dependent ones are checked at runtime with a persistent execution stack. — [arXiv PDF p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15100#page=4)
- Measured per-token cost and speedups: "under 40 µs per token for JSON Schema and CFG (JSON), and under 200 µs for XML and Python DSL. It delivers up to 3x speedup on JSON Schema and over 100x on CFG, compared to the best baseline in each case." — [arXiv PDF p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15100#page=9)
- End-to-end: "enabling XGrammar enhances output quality with nearly zero overhead in TPOT" (time per output token), attributed to overlapping mask generation with GPU inference. — [arXiv PDF p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15100#page=10)
- Tokenizer alignment: byte-level pushdown automaton where "each character edge" may "include one or more bytes", so tokens that split a UTF-8 character are handled (§3, same PDF).

**XGrammar-2 (Li, Dong, et al., Jan 2026, rev. Aug 2026, arXiv 2601.04426)**
- Targets agentic workloads where grammars "vary both across requests and within a request": "TagDispatch for dynamic structural dispatching", a "Cross-Grammar Cache", and "an Earley-based adaptive token mask cache, just-in-time compilation, and repetition state compression". Claims "over 6x faster compilation than prior structured generation engines" and "near-zero end-to-end overhead". — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04426#page=1)

**SynCode (Ugare et al., 2024; TMLR per Semantic Scholar, arXiv 2403.01632)**
- Parser: "we employ LR parsers in SynCode. Our evaluation indicates that LR(1) parsers suffice for eliminating most syntax errors" — [arXiv PDF p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01632#page=14). The grammar must be unambiguous LALR(1)/LR(1) (§4.7, same PDF).
- Mask mechanism: an offline "DFA mask store" maps lexer-DFA states (per terminal) to boolean masks; a "remainder" string tracks the suffix whose lexical type may still change. Soundness is bought by over-approximation: "Hence, we overapproximate the precise set of syntactically valid tokens. We make this choice to ensure that SynCode is sound for any length of accept sequences." — [arXiv PDF p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01632#page=11). The paper's own framing (HTML §4.4–4.5): Theorem 1 soundness (every valid token survives the mask); completeness (Theorem 2) only for long enough accept sequences; the shipped short-sequence mode is "sound but incomplete syntactical decoding".
- Results: "SynCode reduces 96.07% of the syntax errors for Python and Go on average." and JSON syntax errors go to zero. — [arXiv PDF p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01632#page=3)
- Offline cost: "The DFA mask store for Llama-2-7B-chat took 113.72 seconds to create and consumes 181 MB of memory." — [arXiv PDF p.21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01632#page=21). Per-completion wall time in Table 1: 3.07 s with SynCode vs 3.58 s standard for Llama-2-7B-chat (faster because it stops earlier) — same PDF, §6.1; no isolated per-token figure is given.

**DOMINO (Beurer-Kellner, Fischer, Vechev; ICML 2024 per Semantic Scholar; arXiv 2403.06988)**
- The misalignment cost of naive masking: "naive constraining can reduce accuracy from 41.5% in the unconstrained case to 30.8%" (GSM8K, JSON-encoded, Mistral 7B). — [arXiv PDF p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.06988#page=3)
- Formal target: "Definition 2.1 (Minimally invasive) We consider a constrained decoding method minimally invasive, if every valid output that can be generated by an unconstrained model (for any given prompt), is also generated by the constrained model, given the same prompt." — [arXiv PDF p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.06988#page=2)
- Mechanism (HTML §3.3–3.6): a "subterminal" is a piece of a terminal's NFA aligned to the vocabulary; a vocabulary-aligned subterminal tree is built offline ("1-5s", C ~20 s); at runtime the parser checks the model-proposed token first (opportunistic masking) and uses speculative decoding to skip forced tokens.
- Overhead: free-form JSON "incurring only a low 4.21% overhead" — [arXiv PDF p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.06988#page=9); schema-driven formats give up to 77% higher throughput than unconstrained, abstract claims "almost 2×" speedup (Table 2 shows up to 2.71×) — same PDF §4.

**Koo, Liu, He — "Automata-based constraints for language model decoding" (COLM 2024, arXiv 2407.08103)**
- Tokenizer lifting: "Our first contribution is a reformulation of detokenization (i.e., the process of converting token sequences back into text) as an FST"; the token-level constraint is the character-level automaton composed with this transducer, which handles the fact that "the same text can have many tokenizations". — [arXiv PDF p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08103#page=3)
- Pushdown extension is restricted to deterministic grammars: "our system detects non-deterministic grammars and signals an error, leaving it to the author of the grammar to rewrite the grammar in a deterministic manner." — [arXiv PDF p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08103#page=6)
- Cost: constraint compilation "~7,000x faster" than Outlines (abstract); "For per-step overhead, Outlines takes 10s of microseconds and our system takes a few microseconds. While our system has less per-step overhead, in practice both systems have negligible latency" — [arXiv PDF p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08103#page=10). Claims "provably correct" (Appendix B: composed automaton accepts exactly the token sequences whose detokenization matches).

**GCD, Geng et al. (EMNLP 2023, arXiv 2305.13971)**
- Mechanism: a character-level grammar is written by the user; "we use the incremental parser of Grammatical Framework (Angelov, 2009) as the completion engine." — [arXiv PDF p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13971#page=4). Introduces input-dependent grammars.
- Cost: "Incremental parsing imposes an additional overhead on top of pure vanilla decoding." — [arXiv PDF p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13971#page=8); per-token parser latency in Table 4 is of the same order as a LLaMA-33B forward pass (87 ms) for the heaviest grammar, and "the latency for the REBEL grammar (0.5s) is significantly higher than the latency of the LM" — [arXiv PDF p.18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13971#page=18). (2023 result; this is the slow end of the spectrum before the engines above.)

**Park, Zhou, D'Antoni — "Flexible and Efficient Grammar-Constrained Decoding" (2025, arXiv 2502.05111)**
- "existing GCD algorithms require tens of minutes to preprocess common grammars"; their algorithm "offers 17.71x faster offline preprocessing than existing approaches while preserving state-of-the-art efficiency in online mask computation." It precomputes how the subword tokenizer aligns with grammar terminals. — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.05111#page=1)

**Alpay & Senturk — "Attention Meets Reachability" (Mar 2026, arXiv 2603.05540)**
- Formalization: "We formalize GCD as a coupling between a Transformer-style next-token distribution and a reachability oracle over a pushdown system compiled from a CFG" — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05540#page=1). Abstract results (same page): an oracle-invariance theorem (language-equivalent grammars induce identical admissible next-token sets for every prefix); a "structural ambiguity cost" (packed-parse-forest growth per token) that is O(1) under right recursion but Θ(t²) per token / Θ(n³) cumulative under concatenation; and a lower bound that any sound, retrieval-efficient, parse-preserving online masking engine incurs Ω(t²) work per token on a constant-size CFG family. No benchmark numbers in the abstract.

### Inferences
- Per-token grammar checking is a solved cost problem for CFGs that fit a cache: microseconds per token, below the forward pass. The real budget items are offline compilation (seconds to minutes) and the worst-case tail (ambiguous grammars, Earley chart growth), which is exactly what Alpay & Senturk's Ω(t²) bound says cannot be engineered away for all grammars.
- Every engine chooses sound-but-incomplete masking when precision is expensive (SynCode's over-approximation, Koo's rejection of nondeterministic PDAs, DOMINO's bounded lookahead k). The common pattern is: never mask a token that could be valid; accept that some invalid tokens slip through and get caught later. This is the same contract the semantic systems in Q2 adopt.
- Tokenizer alignment is a correctness issue, not just speed: the measured 11-point GSM8K drop from naive masking is a distribution-distortion effect caused purely by cutting words at non-natural subword boundaries (see Q3).

### Gaps
- Guidance's "token healing" is not described in the README as fetched; I could not verify a primary-source definition, so it is not cited.
- No independent (non-vendor) head-to-head per-token benchmark across llguidance, XGrammar and SynCode was found; all overhead numbers above are self-reported.
- XGrammar-2's per-token numbers are not in its abstract; the full paper was not read.

## Q2. Semantic constrained decoding: what is enforced, how the prefix oracle is made sound, whether completability is guaranteed, measured effect

### Takeaway
Semantic systems all enforce the same contract, with increasing formality over time: reject a prefix only when no continuation can repair it (safe pruning); they do not, in general, guarantee that an accepted prefix is completable (dead-end freedom). Synchromesh (2022) abstracts the constraint as a "completion engine" with axioms and a decision procedure for the prefix-closure of the valid language; PICARD (2021) layers lexing, parsing, and scope "guards" that fail fast in a beam; Monitor-Guided Decoding (2023) calls static analysis at trigger points and is explicitly heuristic (imprecise and incomplete); Mündler et al. (PLDI 2025) build "prefix automata" whose prefix property is maintained by a sound type-inhabitation search, deliberately incomplete to guarantee termination; Kronlund-Drouault (Sep 2026) separates the two contracts formally, proves safe pruning for the implementation, proves dead-end freedom only under three grammar conditions, and validates differentially against ocamlc and cc (zero false prunes on 65 programs). Measured effects: compilation errors roughly halved (Mündler: 74.8% / 56.0% reduction on HumanEval / MBPP vs 9% for syntax-only), compilation rate +21.8–24.7% relative (MGD), Spider text-to-SQL to state of the art (PICARD), validity 43% to 72% on SQL (Synchromesh). Costs: Synchromesh +8%, PICARD 2.5 s to 3.1 s per sample, MGD +83% decode time, Mündler +39–52% wall time, Kronlund-Drouault's oracle up to ~130 ms per whole input at a few hundred tokens.

### Cited Findings

**Synchromesh / Constrained Semantic Decoding (Poesia et al., ICLR 2022, arXiv 2201.11227) — 2022 result**
- Abstraction: a completion engine CL is "a partial function from Σ*L to a set of tokens", represented as a regex; "The strings in the domain of CL are called completion points, and a CE satisfies the following axioms: (A1) The empty string and every p ∈ L must be completion points ... (A2) If s is a completion point and t fully matches CL(s), then their concatenation st must also be a completion point. (A3) The CE is exhaustive". — [arXiv PDF p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.11227#page=4)
- Soundness route: "A key component of our algorithm for constrained sampling is a decision procedure for membership in prefix-closure of the set L of all valid programs." The prefix-closure "contains all partial programs that can be completed to a valid program." The valid next tokens are V_M(s) = {t : st ∈ Lc}, computed "by using our decision procedure for each token in the vocabulary", and "Once we have a complete program, we are guaranteed that it will satisfy all constraints enforced by the CE." — [arXiv PDF p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.11227#page=5)
- What is enforced: "syntax, scope, typing rules, and contextual logic" (abstract); engines for SQL, Vega-Lite, SMCalFlow built with Brzozowski derivatives over the vocabulary (p.2–5).
- Cost: "In our experiments with GPT-3, CSD adds an average of 8% overhead to the sampling procedure" — [arXiv PDF p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.11227#page=6)
- Effect: "In SQL, it eliminates execution errors from 29% of the queries generated by GPT-3 13B (as validity improves from 43% to 72%)." — [arXiv PDF p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.11227#page=7)
- Note on dead ends: the completion-engine axioms make accepted prefixes completable only to the extent the engine itself is dead-end free; the paper does not prove this for its engines.

**PICARD (Scholak, Schucher, Bahdanau; EMNLP 2021, arXiv 2109.05093) — 2021 result**
- Mechanism: "PICARD helps to find valid output sequences by rejecting inadmissible tokens at each decoding step." — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.05093#page=1). Rejected top-k tokens get score −∞ inside beam search (Fig. 2).
- Four modes: "off (no checking), lexing, parsing without guards, and parsing with guards—the highest mode. A prediction that passes a higher mode will always pass a lower mode" — [arXiv PDF p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.05093#page=2)
- Scope guards: when a column is referenced via `tid.cid` or `alias.cid` the table/alias must "eventually" be brought into scope; "These guards are enforced eagerly in order to fail fast and to eject invalid hypotheses from the beam at the earliest possible time." — [arXiv PDF p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.05093#page=3). "Eventually" obligations are the dead-end risk: a prefix may pass now and only fail at finalization.
- Cost and effect: "the decoding speed with beam size 4 on an NVIDIA A100-SXM4-40GB GPU was, on average, 2.5 seconds per sample without PICARD and 3.1 seconds per sample with PICARD." T5-3B+PICARD reaches 75.5/71.9 exact-match and 79.3/75.1 execution accuracy on Spider dev/test. — [arXiv PDF p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.05093#page=4)

**Monitor-Guided Decoding (Agrawal et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2306.10763) — 2023 result**
- Mechanism (abstract/§1): a monitor "uses static analysis to guide the decoding"; it "observes the code generated by an LM and queries static analysis at pre-defined trigger points" (partial object dereferences), implemented over the Language Server Protocol; masks to type-consistent identifiers.
- Effect: "All the models with MGD outperform their respective base models on Compilation Rate, by a relative improvement ranging between 21.77%–24.69%." — [arXiv PDF p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.10763#page=7). Abstract: SantaCoder-1.1B with MGD beats text-davinci-003 on compilation rate and next-identifier match. (Correction to the task brief: the small model is SantaCoder-1.1B, not a 350M model.)
- Soundness is not claimed: static analysis of partial programs uses heuristics that "can be both imprecise (they can give incorrect suggestions) and incomplete (they can leave out correct suggestions)." — [arXiv PDF p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.10763#page=10)
- Cost: "We observe a mean slowdown of 83.16% in decoding time for CodeGen-6B with MGD." — [arXiv PDF p.21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.10763#page=21)

**Type-Constrained Code Generation (Mündler, He, Wang, Sen, Song, Vechev; PLDI 2025, arXiv 2504.09246)**
- Claim: "we develop novel prefix automata and a search over inhabitable types, forming a sound approach to enforce well-typedness on LLM-generated code." — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09246#page=1)
- Motivation: "on average 94% of compilation errors result from failing type checks" (syntax is ~6%). — [arXiv PDF p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09246#page=2). The intro states the design goal as maintaining "a prefix property, ensuring that parsing a program prefix only results in a non-empty set of states when it can be completed into a well-typed program" (same region, p.2–3).
- Prefix automata (§3.2, p.10): an automaton "satisfies the prefix property or is a prefix automaton, if there is a path from every" reachable state to an accepting state; Lemma 1: for a prefix automaton the language of reachable prefixes equals the prefix language. Unions/concatenations/Kleene-star preserve the property under stated side conditions. — [arXiv PDF p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09246#page=10)
- Deliberate incompleteness: "Our final automaton will cover a significant but incomplete subset of L_B. Incompleteness exists because to ensure that our algorithms terminate, we do not cover high-order types that are less likely to occur in practice." — [arXiv PDF p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09246#page=10). So the oracle is sound (never admits an untypable prefix as completable) and dead-end free by construction of the prefix property, at the price of rejecting some well-typed programs.
- Effect: "Type constraining reduces compiler errors by 74.8% and 56.0% in the synthesis of HumanEval and MBPP problems respectively, compared to only 9.0% and 4.8% ideal improvement" from syntax-only constraining. — [arXiv PDF p.17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09246#page=17). Abstract: functional correctness up 3.5–5.5% relative for synthesis/translation, repair up 37% relative; models 2B–34B; TypeScript subset.
- Cost: "we observe a relative increase of 39.1% and 52.1% in HumanEval and MBPP respectively" in median wall time per instance, on "an unoptimized, Python-based implementation". 99.4% of steps need one sample-and-check iteration. — [arXiv PDF p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09246#page=19)

**Semantic Prefix Oracles for LLM Decoding: Contracts and Differential Validation (Paul Kronlund-Drouault, submitted 28 Sep 2026, arXiv 2609.35425; Semantic Scholar lists it in the Proceedings of the 2nd ACM SIGPLAN International Workshop on Language Models and Programming Languages, doi 10.1145/3843750.3843841)**
- Setup: "semantic grammar specifications" attach context-dependent constraints (scope, typing) to a CFG as typing-style ascriptions over shared holes, and the implementation ("Aufbau") builds and solves the constraint graph "during Earley descent rather than after parsing"; a stable contradiction removes an Earley item immediately, unresolved evidence stays in the chart. Verdicts are Satisfied, Lost, or Live (Definition 2.3). — [arXiv HTML §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35425v1#S2.SS3.p2.1)
- The two contracts: "A semantic prefix oracle owes the decoder two contracts, safe pruning and dead-end freedom" (§2.3); the implementation "only relies on the safe-pruning contract".
- Safe pruning proven: "Theorem 2.4 (Safe pruning). Aufbau never rejects a prefix that has a witness. Proof. Every rejection is justified by a stable contradiction on every compatible branch." — [arXiv HTML #S2.Thmtheorem4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35425v1#S2.Thmtheorem4.p1.1.1)
- How missing/undecided information is handled: "prediction-time checks use only constraints already determined by the consumed prefix. Missing information can therefore delay a rejection, but it cannot create one. This matters for dead-end freedom, not for safe pruning." — [arXiv HTML #S2.SS3.p2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35425v1#S2.SS3.p2.1)
- Dead-end freedom is NOT guaranteed in general: "Dead-end freedom is a property of a grammar in addition to the oracle. A Live prefix has not contradicted the grammar, but it may still have no completion." Example given: in plain STLC with distinct atomic types A, B, the prefix `λf:A→B. f(` is Live but has no witness if the language has no closed term of type A. — [arXiv HTML #S2.SS3.p3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35425v1#S2.SS3.p3.1)
- Dead-end freedom under three sufficient grammar conditions, Definition 2.5: productivity (P) "Every reachable nonterminal has a finite completion, and every reachable regular residual is nonempty"; type coverage (T) "Every reachable type demand has a finite closed realizer"; left-to-right flow (F) "A child's semantic obligations are determined by the parent demand, the fixed context, and children to its left". "Theorem 2.6 (Dead-end freedom under coverage). A grammar satisfying (P), (T), and (F) is dead-end free: every Live prefix has a witness." — [arXiv HTML #S2.Thmtheorem6](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35425v1#S2.Thmtheorem6.p1.1.1). The paper does not claim these conditions for its tool DSLs or the STLC instance.
- Decidability of the oracle itself (Proposition 2.2): for finite grammars with regular terminals, an acyclic zero-consumption left-corner relation, and productive nonterminals, "evaluation of any finite prefix and masking over any finite candidate set are decidable. Proof (sketch). Earley parsing over a finite input has a finite chart of item cores." — [arXiv HTML #S2.Thmtheorem2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35425v1#S2.Thmtheorem2.p1.m6). Normalization rewrites are excluded from this result; inference-mode recursion is excluded from the ML fragment.
- Differential validation: "Across every prefix of 65 compiler-valid programs we observe zero false prunes. The semantic oracle localizes 25/30 invalid programs mid-stream, against 0/30 for a syntax-only oracle" (ocamlc, cc; 40 ML + 25 C programs). — [arXiv HTML #abstract](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35425v1#abstract1.2.2). The author's own caveat: "These are finite, hand-selected test cohorts, so the results validate rather than certify." — [arXiv HTML #S4.SS1.p1.1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35425v1#S4.SS1.p1.1.2)
- Generation study (§4.2, from the fetched text): 12 models, 3,693 graded attempts; semantic-minus-syntactic point estimates nonnegative on every model-language pair; maxima +15.2 points STLC task correctness and +14.3 points ML validity; pooled ML +7.1 points (32.5% vs 25.4%), STLC +2.0 (40.4% vs 38.4%), C 0 (a direct mask lifts C validity 53% to 80% but syntax-only reaches the same 80%, reported as a negative result).
- Cost: no per-token figure; "Median whole-input try_feed time on four synthetic stress families grows smoothly and mildly superlinearly with expression size, at log–log slopes of roughly 1.1–1.4"; the largest inputs (450-token C declaration sequences ~125 ms, 65-token STLC chains ~131 ms) stay under ~130 ms on a six-core laptop CPU without a model. — [arXiv HTML #S4.SS1.p6.1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35425v1#S4.SS1.p6.1.2)
- Languages: STLC (monomorphic, mandatory binder annotations), a finite lambda language with let/Int/Bool/Float, a strict OCaml subset ("core ML"), a C-like fragment graded by `cc -fsyntax-only`, and an agentic tool DSL in three dialects (§3.2). Positioning (§5): ChopChop is read as "the general setting our work instantiates"; Mündler et al. is a stronger per-language result for TypeScript; Synchromesh/IterGen do not analyze the two contracts separately.

**ChopChop (Nagy, Zhou, Polikarpova, D'Antoni; arXiv 2509.00360, Aug 2025 rev. Dec 2025; reported as POPL 2026 in secondary sources, not on the arXiv page)**
- "ChopChop introduces a principled way to construct constrained decoders based on analyzing the space of programs a prefix represents. It formulates this analysis as a realizability problem which is solved via coinduction"; demonstrated on "(1) equivalence to a reference program and (2) type safety", improving success rates "while maintaining practical decoding latency". — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.00360#page=1)

**Correctness-Guaranteed Code Generation via Constrained Decoding (Li, Rahili, Zhao; COLM 2025, arXiv 2508.15866)**
- A dynamic tree of parsers, each "a modular context-free grammar enriched with contextual information such as variable scopes and type constraints", emitting a regex per step; demonstrated on "sLua, a strongly typed variant of Lua"; claims semantic guarantees extend to runtime correctness in a game-scripting application. — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15866#page=1), [p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15866#page=7)

**Decode-Time Grammars (Zhang et al., Jul 2026, arXiv 2607.18357)**
- Scope as a grammar refinement: "environment-indexed grammars ordered by refinement, prove No-Ghost soundness for Γ-slotted fragments, show that refinement preserves this support-set" guarantee; a tightening operator replaces open reference positions with slots whose candidates are "exactly the names, fields, APIs, or options available at that point", eliminating references to undefined symbols by construction; 0.6B–236B models on TileLang, SQL, P4 "at moderate overhead over standard constrained decoding". — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18357#page=1)

**The Alignment Problem in Constrained Code Generation (Biagiola et al., Jun 2026, arXiv 2606.21619)**
- An incomplete constrainer (one that rejects valid target programs) is harmful: "unconstrained decoding significantly outperforms constrained decoding in terms of functional correctness. Incompleteness pushes the model into low-probability regions of the program space, causing the generation to frequently time out, and reducing functional correctness by up to 97%." Seven models, two target languages, two constrainers (types and syntax for TypeScript), three benchmarks. — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21619#page=1)

**Decoding Time Verification / meta-step decoding (Zhou et al., May 2026, arXiv 2605.17626)**
- Runs full verifiers at structural boundaries instead of per token: "a framework that treats structural boundaries as meta steps for verifier-guided decoding. DTV interleaves generation with verifier calls under a state-machine" with rollback; C-to-Rust pass rate 72.3% to 82.0%, JS-to-TS 33.3% to 46.0% (Qwen3-4B). — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17626#page=1)

**Projectional Decoding (Chen, Hernández López, Babikian; FSE 2026 IVR track, arXiv 2605.30054)**
- Keeps "a partial graph model as the primary artifact representation throughout generation" for incremental semantic validation with explicit uncertainty; preliminary results only. — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30054#page=1)

**SEM-CTRL (Albinhassan, Madhyastha, Russo; TMLR 2026, arXiv 2503.01804)**
- Token-level MCTS guided by constraints "expressed using Answer Set Grammars, which is a logic-based formalism that generalizes context sensitive grammars"; claims guaranteed valid completions for off-the-shelf LLMs and small models beating o4-mini on synthetic grammar synthesis, combinatorial reasoning, JSON parsing, planning. — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01804#page=1)

**TreeCoder (Princis, Sharma, David; Nov 2025 rev. Apr 2026, arXiv 2511.22277)**
- "TreeCoder represents decoding as a tree search over candidate programs, where both decoding strategies and constraint functions–such as style, syntax, execution–are treated as first-class"; MBPP and SQL-Spider; no formal guarantee. — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.22277#page=1)

**AdapTrack (Li, Li, Li, Jin; ICSE 2026, arXiv 2510.17376)**
- "By incorporating backtracking into the generation process, AdapTrack avoids distorting the output intent of the language model"; claims a proof that its output distribution aligns with the model's; up to 360.87% improvement on synthetic API completion, 7.84% HumanEval, 6.42% MBPP over plain constrained decoding. — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17376#page=1)

### Inferences
- The field converged on exactly the contract disp needs: safe pruning (reject only on a stable contradiction) is provable and cheap; dead-end freedom is a property of the language/grammar (productivity, type inhabitation coverage, left-to-right flow of obligations), and nobody guarantees it for a real language. For disp, a step-budgeted type checker that returns "undecided" must map to Live, never Lost ("missing information can delay a rejection but cannot create one"), or it breaks safe pruning and triggers the Biagiola et al. failure mode (incomplete constrainer → up to 97% loss of functional correctness).
- PICARD's "eventually brought into scope" guards and Kronlund-Drouault's "Pending" bindings are the same device: carry forward obligations instead of deciding early. The chart or constraint graph is the data structure that makes this per-token cheap.
- Costs scale with how much semantics is checked per token: grammar µs; scope/type checks on a language subset tens of percent of decode time (Synchromesh 8%, PICARD 24%, Mündler 39–52%, MGD 83%); whole-input re-evaluation ~100 ms at hundreds of tokens (Kronlund-Drouault). DTV shows the alternative when a verifier cannot be made incremental: run it at structural boundaries and roll back.
- Nothing here handles undecidable semantics other than by (a) restricting the language (monomorphic, no inference recursion, no higher-order types), (b) over-approximating (sound but incomplete), or (c) deferring to a later check. There is no published system that runs an arbitrary program as the type oracle.

### Gaps
- Mündler et al.'s PSPACE-completeness remark on type inhabitation (quoted in secondary summaries) was not found in the PDF text by grep; treat it as unverified.
- Kronlund-Drouault reports no per-token cost and no model-in-the-loop latency; its 12-model study is reported at the point-estimate level only (confidence intervals not extracted).
- ChopChop's venue (POPL 2026) and SEM-CTRL's exact guarantee statement were only seen in abstracts/secondary sources.

## Q3. The distribution-distortion problem and its fixes (GAD, SMC, Ctrl-G/GeLaTo, 2025–2026 follow-ups)

### Takeaway
Masking the next-token distribution to grammatical tokens and renormalizing samples a different distribution from "the LM conditioned on the output being in the grammar". The missing factor at each step is the expected future grammaticality (EFG) of the prefix, i.e. the probability that a random continuation ends up valid; hard masking sets it to 1 for every live prefix, so mass flows toward prefixes with few valid completions. Three families of fixes: (1) GAD/ASAp (NeurIPS 2024) learns EFG over-approximations from repeated samples and provably converges but is slow (thousands of iterations); (2) SMC (Lew et al. 2023, Loula et al. ICLR 2025) uses the masked LM as a proposal and corrects with importance weights so the particle population is asymptotically exact, letting small models beat 8× larger ones, with expensive semantic checks folded into the weights; (3) tractable models (GeLaTo 2023, Ctrl-G 2024) distill the LM into an HMM where the conditional on a DFA constraint is computable exactly. 2025–2026 work reframes the target as a Doob h-transform with h = future validity, gives sharp KL/TV bounds for hard masking, adds exact rejection-sampling variants (CARS, AWRS), offline-learned corrections conditioned on parser state (SHIM), and extends the impossibility to speculative decoding. Format restrictions measurably degrade reasoning even before any of this.

### Cited Findings

**Grammar-Aligned Decoding (Park, Wang, Berg-Kirkpatrick, Polikarpova, D'Antoni; NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2405.21047)**
- The exact problem: the target is "the distribution Q^{P,G} that is proportional to P but restricted to sentences in G" (§2); GCD "can be viewed as approximating the exact left-to-right conditionals" (HTML §2). The missing term is "c(w_{1:i}) = E_{P(w_{i+1:n}|w_{1:i})}[1[w ∈ L(G)]] ... We refer to c(w_{1:i}) as expected future grammaticality (EFG), i.e. the probability that a continuation of w_{1:i} sampled from P lands in L(G)." — [arXiv PDF p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.21047#page=3)
- Why masking is biased: GCD "makes no attempt to integrate the model's likelihood to estimate expected future grammaticality, which can lead to substantial bias in the sampling distribution—i.e., every EFG such that c(w_{1:i}) > 0 is approximated by 1". — [arXiv PDF p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.21047#page=4)
- ASAp: starts from the GCD over-approximation (EFG = 1 if live) and after each sample lowers the EFG estimates along the sampled prefix using the recorded next-token likelihoods; Theorem 2: converges to exact EFG in the limit (assumes almost-sure termination of ancestral sampling); outputs are always grammatical. Abstract: "provably producing outputs that match the conditional probability of the LLM's distribution".
- Cost: "The key limitation of our work is the current slow convergence of the ASAp algorithm. In some benchmarks, even after 2,000 iterations the KL divergence barely improves" — [arXiv PDF p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.21047#page=10); exact GAD is intractable in general (§1–2).

**SMC steering with probabilistic programs (Lew, Zhi-Xuan, Grand, Mansinghka; 2023, arXiv 2306.03081) — 2023 result**
- Frames tasks "as posterior inference problems in a class of discrete probabilistic sequence models, and replace standard decoding with sequential Monte Carlo inference. For a computational cost similar to that of beam search, SMC can steer LLMs to solve diverse tasks, including infilling, generation under syntactic constraints, and prompt intersection". — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.03081#page=1)

**Syntactic and Semantic Control via SMC (Loula et al.; ICLR 2025, arXiv 2504.13139)**
- Venue verified: [ICLR 2025 proceedings page](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/a2d537e69a6c6638a3630eef835f07de-Abstract-Conference.html).
- Critique of locally constrained decoding: it "often introduces greedy approximations that badly distort the distribution" — [arXiv PDF p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13139#page=2); "for most practical potential functions, the local and global product of experts do not define the same distribution" — [arXiv PDF p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13139#page=4). It "can lead to myopic sampling down paths that lead to globally poor solutions" (§2).
- Fix: the locally constrained LM is the SMC proposal; importance weights include a factor that "corrects for the greediness of" the local proposal and converge to the global posterior as particles grow (§2, footnote 6); adaptive resampling refocuses compute on promising partial sequences. Expensive potentials (running partial Python, checking SQL column names "modulo aliasing") enter the weights because they "cannot be efficiently encoded in logit masks" (§3.1–3.2).
- Cost and effect: expensive potentials' "cost rarely rises above ∼30ms per token" — [arXiv PDF p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13139#page=9). Abstract: "small open-source language models to outperform models over 8× larger"; "with 5–10× fewer particles, SMC outperforms approaches that only incorporate constraints at the end of generation" (§1). Table 2 (from fetched text): full SMC vs base LM 0.419 vs 0.063 (goal inference), 0.577 vs 0.132 (molecular synthesis), 0.407 vs 0.213 (data science), 0.620 vs 0.531 (text-to-SQL). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13139)

**Adaptive Weighted Rejection Sampling (Lipkin et al.; COLM 2025, arXiv 2504.05410)**
- Two problems named: "Evaluating the constraint on every token can be prohibitively expensive—LM vocabularies often exceed 100,000 tokens. (ii) LCD can distort the global distribution over strings, sampling tokens based only on local information, even if they lead down dead-end paths." Fix: "an adaptive rejection sampling algorithm that typically requires orders of magnitude fewer constraint evaluations", extended to give "low-variance, unbiased estimates of importance weights" usable inside SMC. — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05410#page=1)

**Constrained Adaptive Rejection Sampling (Parys, Vaidya, Berg-Kirkpatrick, D'Antoni; Oct 2025 rev. Jun 2026, arXiv 2510.01902)**
- "greedy constrained decoding methods enforce validity during decoding but distort the LM's distribution"; CARS samples unconstrained and rules out violating continuations "by recording them in a trie and subtracting their probability mass from future draws", so "the resulting samples exactly follow the constrained distribution"; efficiency measured in LM forward passes per valid sample. — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01902#page=1)

**SHIM — "The Parser Already Knows" (Özgü, Wu, Van den Broeck, Kim; Aug 2026 rev. Oct 2026, arXiv 2608.10137)**
- "the parser and lexer states that GCD tools already maintain carry a strong signal about future grammatical validity. We introduce SHIM, a lightweight, offline-trained correction of the LM's next-token probabilities" conditioned on parser state; runs at nearly masking speed and narrows the gap to the grammar-conditioned distribution without online resampling; no formal guarantee. — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10137#page=1)

**Future Validity is the Missing Statistic (Nie et al., May 2026, arXiv 2605.07698)**
- Extends the impossibility to speculative decoding: a speculative decoder with local mask access samples the locally projected distribution, not the grammar-conditional. "We identify the future-validity function Φ_t(y) = Pr_p[valid completion | y] as the missing correction statistic. The target distribution is a Doob transform of the base model with h = Φ, while local masking" sets Φ ≡ 1. Numbers: TV gap on Dyck grammars with Qwen3-8B "can reach 0.996"; a one-step correction cuts Dyck TV 14% at <1% throughput cost; exact dynamic programming cuts it 97%; "finite-language correction closes JSON gaps to numerical precision. All fidelity claims are scoped to enumerable grammars and token tries." — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07698#page=1)

**Alpay & Senturk 2026 (arXiv 2603.05540)** — also derives "sharp one-step KL and total-variation distortion bounds for hard-masked decoding" via a Doob h-transform (abstract). — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05540#page=1)

**GeLaTo (Zhang, Dang, Peng, Van den Broeck; ICML 2023, arXiv 2304.07438) — 2023 result**
- Sampling from the conditional is "intractable for even the simplest lexical constraints"; "we propose to use tractable probabilistic models (TPMs) to impose lexical constraints in autoregressive text generation models" — distilled HMMs in which Pr(text | constraint) is computed exactly and used to reweight the LM. — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07438#page=1)

**Ctrl-G (Zhang, Kung, Yoshida, Van den Broeck, Peng; NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2406.13892) — 2024 result**
- "Ctrl-G combines any production-ready LLM with a Hidden Markov Model, enabling LLM outputs to adhere to logical constraints represented as deterministic finite automata"; TULU2-7B + Ctrl-G beats GPT-3.5/GPT-4 on interactive text editing with >30% higher human-rated satisfaction. — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13892#page=1)

**Format restrictions and reasoning (Tam et al., 2024, arXiv 2408.02442) — 2024 result**
- "we observe a significant decline in LLMs' reasoning abilities under format restrictions. Furthermore, we find that stricter format constraints generally lead to greater performance degradation in reasoning tasks." — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.02442#page=1)

**Related distortion evidence from Q1/Q2:** DOMINO's 41.5% to 30.8% GSM8K drop from token-misaligned masking ([p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.06988#page=3)); Biagiola et al.'s up-to-97% functional-correctness loss from an incomplete constrainer ([p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21619#page=1)); AdapTrack's backtracking with a claimed distribution-alignment proof ([p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17376#page=1)).

### Inferences
- The clean statement: hard masking samples the Doob transform with h ≡ 1 on live prefixes; the correct h is Φ(prefix) = Pr[a random completion is valid]. For disp, Φ(prefix) = "probability a random completion type-checks", so the distortion question and the search-heuristic question are the same object: an estimate of Φ is simultaneously the sampling correction (GAD/SMC/SHIM) and the value function for program search.
- Exact fixes cost either many samples (ASAp, CARS) or particles (SMC); approximate fixes learn Φ offline from parser state (SHIM) or restrict to tractable model classes (HMM + DFA). Only the HMM route gives exactness, and only for regular constraints; type-checking constraints are far outside that class.
- The SMC framing is the one that already accommodates an expensive, possibly non-terminating oracle: cheap checks (grammar) go in the proposal mask, expensive checks (run the type program under a step budget) go in the particle weight. Loula et al.'s ≤30 ms/token for such potentials is a realistic budget.

### Gaps
- No paper quantifies distortion for type-level (non-regular, non-CF) constraints; all KL/TV measurements are for grammars (Dyck, JSON, small CFGs).
- Ctrl-G's "100% constraint satisfaction" claim appears in secondary summaries; the abstract reports human-evaluation satisfaction rates instead, so no exactness claim is attributed here.
- SHIM's quantitative KL reduction is not in the abstract; the full paper was not read.

## Q4. Prefix liveness theory: viable prefixes, Earley charts as liveness certificates, PEG limits, incremental editor parsers

### Takeaway
For context-free grammars, "can this prefix be completed?" is decidable and cheap: an LR(1) parser detects an error at the first token that cannot extend a viable prefix, and an Earley chart over a finite prefix is a finite object whose non-empty item set certifies syntactic liveness (Kronlund-Drouault Prop. 2.2 relies on exactly this); completability of an infill (prefix and suffix fixed) reduces to emptiness of CFL ∩ regular, which is decidable (Mündler et al. 2025 for diffusion LLMs). The catch is that syntactic liveness is not completability once semantics are added: a Live prefix "may still have no completion" unless the grammar is productive and type demands are realizable. PEGs break the picture entirely: emptiness and completeness of an arbitrary PEG are undecidable (Ford 2004), so a PEG-based prefix oracle cannot in general be made dead-end free; only well-formed PEGs are guaranteed complete. Editor technology (Wagner & Graham 1998 incremental LR(k) sentential-form parsing, tree-sitter's keystroke-rate GLR with error recovery) is the cheap route to per-token re-checking, and llguidance/XGrammar-2 already use Earley with persistent state for the same reason.

### Cited Findings
- **LR(1) as the viable-prefix oracle (SynCode):** "we employ LR parsers in SynCode. Our evaluation indicates that LR(1) parsers suffice for eliminating most syntax errors" — [arXiv PDF p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01632#page=14); LR(1) is chosen for immediate error detection (HTML §4.5), i.e. the parser state set is non-empty exactly when the consumed string is a viable prefix. Grammars must be LALR(1)/LR(1) and unambiguous (§4.7).
- **Deterministic pushdown only (Koo et al.):** "as LL(k) and LR(k) parsers do, our system detects non-deterministic grammars and signals an error" — [arXiv PDF p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08103#page=6). Nondeterministic CFGs are pushed to Earley-style engines.
- **Earley as the engine and chart as certificate:** llguidance "implements a context-free grammar parser using Earley's algorithm on top of a lexer based on derivatives of regular expressions" and computes the mask by trie traversal — [README #technical-details](https://github.com/guidance-ai/llguidance#technical-details); XGrammar-2 uses "an Earley-based adaptive token mask cache" — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04426#page=1). Kronlund-Drouault's decidability proof: "Earley parsing over a finite input has a finite chart of item cores" — [arXiv HTML #S2.Thmtheorem2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35425v1#S2.Thmtheorem2.p1.m6).
- **Liveness ≠ completability once semantics are attached:** "A Live prefix has not contradicted the grammar, but it may still have no completion." — [arXiv HTML #S2.SS3.p3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35425v1#S2.SS3.p3.1); dead-end freedom needs productivity (P), type coverage (T), left-to-right flow (F) — [arXiv HTML #S2.Thmtheorem6](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35425v1#S2.Thmtheorem6.p1.1.1).
- **Prefix property as an automaton invariant (Mündler et al. 2025):** an automaton "satisfies the prefix property or is a prefix automaton, if there is a path from every" reachable state to acceptance; Lemma 1 equates reachable-prefix language with the prefix language; closure under union/concatenation/star needs L(A_Y) ≠ ∅ for concatenation — [arXiv PDF p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09246#page=10). Synchromesh's version: a "decision procedure for membership in prefix-closure of the set L of all valid programs" — [arXiv PDF p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.11227#page=5).
- **Infill completability as emptiness of an intersection (Mündler, Dekoninck, Vechev; 2025, arXiv 2508.10111; ICLR 2026 per search listings):** constrained decoding for diffusion LLMs is reduced to "the task of deciding whether the intersection of the target language and a regular language is empty and present an efficient algorithm to solve it for context-free languages." — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.10111#page=1)
- **Cost lower bound for sound online masking (Alpay & Senturk 2026):** structural ambiguity cost is O(1) per token under right recursion but Θ(t²) per token under concatenation, and any sound, retrieval-efficient, parse-preserving online engine must do Ω(t²) work per token on a specific constant-size CFG family (abstract) — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05540#page=1).
- **PEG limits (Ford, POPL 2004):** "Theorem: It is undecidable in general whether the language L(G) of an arbitrary parsing expression grammar G is empty." (proof via undecidability of PEG intersection emptiness, since PELs are closed under intersection) — [peg.pdf p.6](https://bford.info/pub/lang/peg.pdf#page=6); "Theorem: It is undecidable whether an arbitrary grammar is complete: that is, whether it either succeeds or fails on all input strings." — [peg.pdf p.6](https://bford.info/pub/lang/peg.pdf#page=6); the positive side: "Theorem: A well-formed grammar G is complete." (well-formed = no direct or indirect left recursion, checked by a conservative syntactic analysis) — [peg.pdf p.7](https://bford.info/pub/lang/peg.pdf#page=7). The same page notes PELs include non-context-free languages (aⁿbⁿcⁿ), so the PEG prefix problem is not reducible to the CFG one.
- **Incremental parsing for editors (Wagner & Graham, TOPLAS 1998 per the Harmonia project list) — 1998 result:** "We present a simple algorithm based on parsing LR(k) sentential forms that can incrementally parse an arbitrary number of textual and/or structural modifications in optimal time and with no storage overhead." — [Harmonia PDF p.1](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~harmonia/papers/twagner-parsing.pdf#page=1)
- **tree-sitter:** aims to be "General enough to parse any programming language", "Fast enough to parse on every keystroke in a text editor", "Robust enough to provide useful results even in the presence of syntax errors", with a dependency-free C11 runtime; its research list cites GLR and LR error-recovery papers. — [tree-sitter docs #introduction](https://tree-sitter.github.io/tree-sitter/#introduction)

### Inferences
- For disp's own grammar the syntactic prefix oracle is a non-issue if the grammar is a CFG parsed by Earley or LR(1); the chart/state-set is both the liveness certificate and the reusable incremental state. If disp's surface syntax is PEG-defined (prioritized choice, lookahead predicates), then dead-end freedom of the syntactic oracle is undecidable in general and must be argued per grammar (e.g., by restricting to well-formed, left-recursion-free PEGs and checking productivity by hand).
- The deeper lesson from Kronlund-Drouault is that liveness must be stratified: syntactic Live (chart non-empty), semantic Live (no stable contradiction), and completable (a witness exists). Only the first two are decidable per token for a dependent type system; the third is the type-inhabitation question, which is where the step budget bites.
- Editor-style incremental parsers (keystroke-rate reparse with error recovery) are a cheaper engineering route to the per-token syntactic check than LLM-specific engines when the grammar is large and already has a tree-sitter definition, but they do not produce token masks; they answer "is the current prefix still parseable" for one candidate at a time, which fits a sample-then-check loop (Mündler: 99.4% of steps need one check) better than full-vocabulary masking.

### Gaps
- Knuth 1965 (LR(k), viable prefixes, earliest error detection) and Earley 1970 could not be fetch-verified (host timeout; publisher 403 with no open copy), so the classical statements above are attributed through SynCode, Koo et al., llguidance and Kronlund-Drouault rather than the originals.
- No source was found that states and proves "Earley chart non-empty at position i ⇔ prefix is viable" for grammars with useless symbols removed as a standalone theorem; Kronlund-Drouault's Prop. 2.2 assumes productive nonterminals explicitly, which is the needed hypothesis.

## Q5. 2025–2026 work combining constrained decoding with proof assistants or dependent types (Lean, Rocq, Agda, Idris, Dafny, F*), including typed-hole-driven decoding

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 no verified paper puts a dependent type checker or a proof assistant's elaborator inside a token-level masking loop. The closest verified items are: type-constrained decoding for simply typed languages (Mündler et al. PLDI 2025 for TypeScript; Kronlund-Drouault Sep 2026 for STLC/core ML/C with Earley-integrated typing), semantic realizability frameworks (ChopChop, type safety), scope-refinement grammars (Decode-Time Grammars), verifier calls at structural boundaries with rollback (DTV for Rust/TypeScript), and infill-completability for diffusion models (Mündler et al. 2025). On the proof-assistant side the only 2026 decoding-time intervention found is structural: forcing Lean tactic skeletons as fixed prefixes (Burton, ICML AI4Math 2026 workshop), which is prompt-level, not a type-checked mask. Typed-hole work (Blinn et al., OOPSLA 2024) uses the hole's type and context to build prompts, not to constrain decoding. The strongest cautionary result is Biagiola et al. 2026: an incomplete constrainer (which any step-budgeted checker is, if timeouts are treated as rejections) can cut functional correctness by up to 97%.

### Cited Findings
- **Simply typed, not dependent:** Mündler et al. formalize "on a generic, simply-typed core calculus" and instantiate on TypeScript ([p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09246#page=1)); they exclude "high-order types that are less likely to occur in practice" to guarantee termination ([p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09246#page=10)). Kronlund-Drouault's STLC and ML instances are monomorphic with mandatory binder annotations, and inference-mode recursion is excluded from the ML fragment (§3.2) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35425v1#S2.SS3.p3.1)).
- **Type safety as a decoding constraint via realizability:** ChopChop enforces "(1) equivalence to a reference program and (2) type safety" by solving a realizability problem "via coinduction" over the programs a prefix can become — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.00360#page=1).
- **Scope constraints as grammar refinement:** Decode-Time Grammars prove "No-Ghost soundness for Γ-slotted fragments" so generated references are "exactly the names, fields, APIs, or options available at that point" — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18357#page=1).
- **Whole-checker verification at boundaries:** DTV "treats structural boundaries as meta steps for verifier-guided decoding", with rollback; C-to-Rust 72.3% to 82.0% — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17626#page=1). This is the pattern for a verifier (such as a borrow checker or an elaborator) that cannot be run per token.
- **Hole filling as a language-emptiness problem:** Mündler, Dekoninck, Vechev reduce (multi-region) infilling under a CFG to "deciding whether the intersection of the target language and a regular language is empty" — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.10111#page=1). This is the syntactic half of typed-hole-driven decoding; the typed half has no published decoding-time counterpart.
- **Typed holes as context, not constraint (2024):** "The Hazel Language Server is able to identify the type and typing context of the hole that the programmer is filling, with Hazel's total syntax and type error correction ensuring that a" meaningful sketch is always available; completions are "iteratively refined via further dialog with the language server" (prompting) — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.00921#page=1).
- **Lean, decoding-time structure without type checking (2026):** for DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL on miniF2F-test, "doubling the sampling budget from k=32 to k=64 produces zero additional solved theorems (42/244 in both cases). A fixed schedule of 15 tactic skeletons breaks this plateau and recovers a +45% relative improvement at k=16 (mean Δ = +12.3 ± 4.2 theorems across n=3 seeds". The skeletons are forced prefixes; the abstract does not describe token masking or Lean-in-the-loop checking. The effect reverses for the SFT-trained Goedel-Prover (−10.0 ± 4.4). — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.16172#page=1)
- **Why an incomplete oracle is dangerous:** "Incompleteness pushes the model into low-probability regions of the program space, causing the generation to frequently time out, and reducing functional correctness by up to 97%." — [arXiv PDF p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21619#page=1)
- **Semantic checks that cannot be masks go in weights (Loula et al.):** potentials "making use of information that cannot be efficiently encoded in logit masks" (running partial programs, schema checks) enter SMC weights at "∼30ms per token" — [arXiv PDF p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13139#page=9).

### Inferences
- The gap the disp survey targets is real and open: every verified type-aware decoder restricts itself to a decidable, terminating fragment (simply typed, monomorphic, no higher-order types, no inference recursion) precisely so that the oracle can answer per token. A dependent type checker that is itself a program under a step budget cannot promise termination, so it cannot be a masking oracle in the published sense; it fits the SMC-weight or boundary-verifier (DTV) slot instead, with "budget exhausted" mapped to Live/unknown.
- The three grammar conditions for dead-end freedom (productive nonterminals, every type demand has a finite closed realizer, obligations flow left to right) translate directly into requirements on disp's syntax and standard library: the type-inhabitation coverage condition (T) is the one a dependent system cannot meet in general, which is exactly why hole-driven generation should expect dead ends and plan for backtracking (AdapTrack) or particle resampling (SMC) rather than a straight-line mask.
- Lean-side evidence (tactic skeletons) suggests structural priors at decode time matter even without checking; combining them with a cheap syntactic Earley oracle and a budgeted elaborator in the weights is unexplored.

### Gaps
- A secondary aggregator described a 2025 "Lai et al." Lean data-generation pipeline using constrained decoding over a 60-tactic set; the primary paper was not found and nothing here relies on it.
- No Rocq, Agda, Idris, Dafny or F* constrained-decoding paper (2025–2026) was found by any query; LLM work on those systems is sample-then-check or agentic repair, outside this survey's scope.
- Lean Atlas (arXiv 2604.16347) and other 2026 Lean IDE papers were seen only in search listings and are not cited.
