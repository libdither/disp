interaction nets as strands on a lattice

No addresses and no routing. A wire is a path of strands laid along the links between neighbouring sites; strand i of a link is the same wire from both sides. Each site holds a bead or two (agents) and a little switchboard joining the strand ends that meet there. Every move is a random local proposal, accepted by an energy: wires pull like springs, crowding pushes, heat jiggles.

how it works

Step. An agent moves to a neighbouring site. The strand it walks along is eaten; its other wires are dragged one strand longer. Wanted readers mostly step along their principal wire, toward what they need.

Fold and flip. A wire that goes out and straight back snaps shut; a corner can flip to the other side of its square. Together they let wires straighten like strings under tension.

Exchange. In a full site, an agent may trade places with a resident, so walkers get through crowds.

Rewrite. When a wanted reader and the value it reads meet (same site, or one strand apart), the rule fires inside a 2×2 block: fresh agents take the free seats and are wired with fresh strands.

Demand. Nothing reacts until wanted. A wanted reader sends a pulse (white dot) along its principal wire, one strand per clock; when it reaches another computation's output, that computation is wanted too. A pulse whose strand gets moved is dropped, and the reader sends another.

Tension. At temperature 2 a loose wire gains more from wiggling than it loses to its tension, so over long runs it swells until no lane is free. A link carrying n strands costs n²/2 more, so wire stays loose in open space but cannot crowd every lane around a reaction.

Density. Wires pull matter together until, left alone, three quarters of occupied sites are full, and wanted readers and rewrites stall against full sites. Two idle agents (anything but a wanted reader) sharing a site cost energy, so idle matter spreads out to about one agent per site and keeps a seat free for traffic, while wanted readers pass through.

Garbage. Agents the answer no longer depends on are drawn dimmed. An eraser that touches a computation's output collects it: a duplicator becomes a plain wire from its input to its other output; a dead apply becomes erasers on its inputs; an unpair with both parts erased becomes one eraser on the pair. Once the answer is in, the machine keeps running until only the answer is left.

Clocks. With random turns, a clock is one turn for the busiest site. With 2×2×2 blocks, the lattice is cut into disjoint blocks at a random offset every clock and each block makes one move: what a synchronous chip would do, with no two moves ever touching the same site.

Views. Layers stacked draws every layer of the 3D lattice offset over the others: compact, but layers overlap. Layers side by side gives each layer its own panel: exact, and every site is easy to click. 3D shows the lattice as it is: drag to orbit, right-drag to pan, spread the layers apart or show one at a time.
