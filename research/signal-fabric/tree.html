The rules

Every change to the tree is a rule: a little tree whose loose ends (a, b, c) are parts passed along unread, rewired; gold rings a new node. Rules light up as they fire; on the lattice the parts they moved get the same letters.

what is this?

The value is a tree laid out on the lattice: each node sits in a cell (△ a leaf, a triangle with one hole a stem, with two a fork) and each edge is a wire, a path of cells. The program is not laid out anywhere: it comes in at the root as a signal and moves a cell a tick.

Where a piece of the program reaches a node, it rewrites there by rules. The first run of a piece goes by the machine's own rules, the moves, and is then kept as one rule. In a later run, on the same bits or other ones, the pieces whose rules fit fire in 1 tick: the circuit that the program amounts to builds itself from the bits up, and what is left is mostly signals travelling.

matter moves: each tick a node may step to a free cell where its wires are shorter, and a small value that is read climbs toward what reads it.

layout: rows lays the value out as a tidy tree; packed gives each part its own share of a square, halves side by side and their halves one above the other (an H-tree), so a signal from the root reaches any bit in about √n cells instead of n.

physics: what the machine pays for. Honest: a signal takes its wire's length plus its size in symbols, one at a time on a wire; a rewrite waits until its writes reach the farthest node it changes; wires are paths of cells, a node that steps drags its end of each wire a cell, a rewrite splices wires along the old ones, and kinks straighten a cell at a time. Learned rules ride down with the program's signals (a wire carries each once, the node at its end keeps it) or stay in the cell they were learned in. Cheap takes every shortcut the first version took: one rule book every node reads at once, free sizes and writes, wires drawn fresh from their ends. A library name is one symbol: every cell holds the library.

flip runs the program backward on the answer, with its own rules, giving the input back.
