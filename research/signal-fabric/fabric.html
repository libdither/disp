what is this?

A grid of identical cells. Each cell has one wire to and one wire from each neighbour, and a few dozen configuration bits that make it a gate or a little switch box. Several wires can cross one cell on different links.

The program is compiled once: run with its bits unknown, it becomes a fixed circuit (each gate reads one input bit and the one or two gates under it). That circuit is placed level by level, left to right, and routed.

Load: every cell tests itself, then the configuration spreads cell to cell from the left edge. Cells that failed the test are left out and the wires go around them. Lower the supply to see more fail.

Run, static clock: each node switches as soon as its inputs arrive, paying ½CV² as heat. Adiabatic clock: the power supply itself ramps up one level at a time, slowly, so little is lost.

Flip, adiabatic: the levels ramp back down, last first, and the charge goes back to the supply. Each node is compared with its driver on the way down, which catches noise flips (⚡). Static: the nodes just drain, and their charge is heat.

Not a field: data rides on wires. What spreads like a field is set-up (the test and configuration wave) and the power clock's phase.
