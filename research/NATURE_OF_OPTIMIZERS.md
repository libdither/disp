# The nature of optimizers

> Research note, 2026-09-16. The question: is disp heading toward a general-purpose optimizer that takes an arbitrary type as a search space and generates the hierarchy of heuristics needed to traverse it, is that generating process simple and interpretable, is such an optimizer the end-game of AI, and is the search it induces dangerous even when it is interpretable. Method: four literature sweeps (universal-optimizer theory, declarative-spec precedents, generated heuristics in practice, safety of optimization), each source fetched and checked with `scripts/verify-source.sh`; fragment links (`#page=N`, `#anchor`) land on the passage quoted. Delete this file once the optimizer exists and `research/OPTIMIZER.typ` carries these citations in its own sections.

## 1. The question, stated so it can be answered

The intuition under test, in the author's words: "where disp is heading is to create a general purpose optimizer that can take an arbitrary type defining a search space, and generate the required hierarchy of heuristics that traverse the search space well in an efficient manner, and that this heuristics-generating process should be simple and interpretable." That is four claims:

- **C1, shape.** The end state is: type in, search space out, a generated hierarchy of heuristics that traverses it efficiently.
- **C2, legibility.** The process that generates the heuristics is simple and interpretable.
- **C3, end-game.** Such an optimizer is the end-game of AI.
- **C4, safety.** Even if C2 holds, the search the optimizer induces may be dangerous.

What disp already commits to, so this note does not re-derive it. `GOALS.md` asks for "a universal self-improving optimizer" that turns the type checker into a 0/1 score, multiplies by cost, searches program space, and self-plays by "generating score functions itself to then satisfy". The README promises the result will be "an interpretable (symbolic) recursively self-improving optimizer". The optimizer design (`research/OPTIMIZER.typ`) makes the loop precise: enumerate per-hole candidate streams "ordered by a prior (size, library frequency, an LLM's logits)", superpose them, reduce the goal recognizer, collapse, and on failure "reify the conflict" into a witness (tree position, expected type, actual type) from which a pruning predicate is learned. The local synthesizer note (`research/LOCAL_SYNTH.md`) already has the smallest version of a heuristic hierarchy keyed on the type's shape: an ordered list of handlers per type former. The foundations doc (`FOUNDATIONS.md`) records the open questions this note bears on: Q3, whether a proposer finds certifiable improvements faster than checking junk costs, and Q4, whether the cost proxy tracks hardware. The abstraction note (`research/THE_NATURE_OF_ABSTRACTION.typ`) names the walls: the trusted core stays outside the loop (the Löbian wall), no acceleration theorem exists for self-improvement, and "optimization pressure drifting into the relation library" is the failure to police.

The argument, compressed. C1 is real and provably near-optimal in theory, and the type is exactly the "specification of structure" that No Free Lunch says an optimizer must read to beat random search. C2 holds for two of the three layers every working system has: the spec-derived layer (pruning, decomposition, relaxations, conflict clauses) and the meta-loop (enumerate, evaluate, learn) are simple and readable everywhere; the third layer, the prior that makes hard instances tractable, has been learned and opaque in every system that won at scale, and the theory says the price of generality is exactly one over that prior. C3 is contested: the universal-intelligence school and the reward-is-enough school say yes, the bitter-lesson school says only the meta-method is simple, the services school says the end state is not a unitary optimizer at all, and every realized self-improver replaced the proof gate with tests and the search with a language model. C4 is answered nearly unanimously by the safety literature: interpretability of the generator is the wrong variable, because danger is a property of proxy plus pressure, the most transparent searchers are the canonical spec-gamers, and scoring a legible artifact makes it less faithful. disp's certificate architecture is the strongest available answer for output correctness and does nothing for spec-versus-intent, cost-proxy Goodhart, or a self-play loop allowed to touch its own scorers.

## 2. Theory: what a general optimizer over a specification is, and what it costs

### 2.1 The universal-search family is literally the design

- **Solomonoff's universal prior (1964).** Bayes over all programs weighted by two to the minus length. The one-line meta-procedure your intuition wants exists, and "its main drawback is that it is not computable and thus can only be approximated in practice" ([Scholarpedia, Algorithmic probability](http://www.scholarpedia.org/article/Algorithmic_probability); [Solomonoff 1964](http://raysolomonoff.com/publications/1964pt1.pdf)).
- **Levin's universal search (1973).** Dovetail all programs, time-shared by prior; if some program solves the problem in time t, the search finds it in at most two to the (length plus one) times t. "This exponential growth of computational cost in the algorithmic complexity of the fastest solver makes practical applications of Universal Search problematic" ([Scholarpedia, Universal search](http://www.scholarpedia.org/article/Universal_search); [Levin 1973](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/ppi914)).
- **Hutter's HSEARCH (2002)** is disp's design in one theorem. It takes "a formal (logical, mathematical, not necessarily algorithmic) specification of the problem", interleaves proof search with program execution, and runs within a factor of 5 of the fastest provably-correct program plus an additive constant exponential in the length of the correctness proof. Hutter: "what somewhat spoils the practical applicability ... is the large additive constant", and "the bounds we obtain ... are tremendous" ([The Fastest and Shortest Algorithm for All Well-Defined Problems](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0206022#page=2)).
- **AIXI (2000, book 2005).** Solomonoff plus expectimax, "the most intelligent unbiased agent possible", uncomputable; the computable variant runs in time proportional to two to the program length, which the book says "is still too large to allow a direct implementation" ([AIXI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0004001#page=1); [Universal Artificial Intelligence](http://www.hutter1.net/ai/uaibook.htm#intro)).
- **Speed prior (2002).** Discount by compute time instead of length; the predictor becomes computable and "optimal (within some given epsilon)" ([Schmidhuber, speed prior](https://people.idsia.ch/~juergen/speedprior.html)). The principled way to put "performance scoring" into the prior instead of bolting it on.
- **OOPS (2004).** Defines bias-optimality: each candidate gets time proportional to its prior probability; shifts the bias by reusing prefixes of earlier solutions. Same paper on Levin search and HSEARCH: "since constants beyond 2^500 do not even make sense within this universe, both ... may be viewed as academic exercises" ([Optimal Ordered Problem Solver](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0207097#page=5)). The author of the closest formalization of self-improving universal search says the fix is incremental bias-shifting, not a better universal algorithm.
- **Gödel machine (2003).** Rewrites its own code once its proof searcher proves the rewrite useful, "globally optimal", but only "provided the utility of such speed-ups is provable at all" ([Gödel Machines](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0309048#page=1)). Sound, and it can stall forever on unprovable improvements. The foundations doc already calls this the Gödel-machine wall.
- **PowerPlay (2011).** Searches for pairs of (new task, solver modification) such that the modified solver "provably solves all previously learned tasks plus the new one", the tasks drawn from all computable task descriptions ([PowerPlay](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.5309#page=1)). This is the formal statement of `GOALS.md`'s self-play clause, with no efficiency guarantee.

### 2.2 The price of generality is one over the prior

| searcher | cost of finding solution p |
|---|---|
| Levin search | 2^(length of p) × runtime of p |
| HSEARCH | 5 × runtime of p + 2^(length of p's correctness proof) |
| [Levin tree search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.10928#page=4) (Theorem 3) | depth of p ÷ probability the policy assigns to p |

Three different formalisms, one shape: a general searcher pays exactly one over the prior it assigns to the answer. The exponent never disappears; it moves into the prior. These bounds admit no third option between astronomical constants and a good prior. Where a good prior can come from is settled too: [Baxter's bias-learning theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.0245#page=1) shows a learner "embedded within an environment of related learning tasks" can learn its hypothesis space, with sample complexity improving in the number of tasks. Priors are learnable from a task family, not from a single specification.

### 2.3 No Free Lunch and its escapes

- **The theorem** ([Wolpert and Macready 1997](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~hutter/earg/papers07/00585893.pdf#page=5)): averaged over all objectives, every black-box optimizer performs identically. The line that matters for disp: "the simple existence of structure per se, absent a specification of that structure, cannot provide a basis for preferring one algorithm over another." A type is a specification of the structure. Reading heuristics off the spec is precisely the non-black-box escape hatch.
- **When it binds** ([Igel and Toussaint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0108011#page=2)): only on function classes closed under permutation, a negligible fraction of all classes. Typed, compositional spaces are not permutation-closed.
- **Compressibility escapes** ([Streeter 2003](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-45110-2_15); [Lattimore and Hutter 2011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.3846#page=1)): bounded description length, or weighting problems by a Solomonoff-Levin distribution, restores a free lunch; Lattimore and Hutter argue for "a universal bias allowing an algorithm to succeed in all interesting problem domains".
- **How small the lunch is** ([Everitt, Lattimore and Hutter 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04544)): under the universal distribution "the size of the free lunch turns out to be quite small, at least asymptotically", because needle-in-a-haystack functions are simple to describe yet need a linear number of probes. The [Almost NFL theorem](https://christian-igel.github.io/paper/NFLTLaPoM.pdf#page=14) ([Droste, Jansen and Wegener 2002](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304397502000944)) sharpens this: for any algorithm and any function it optimizes well, exponentially many functions with only slightly larger Kolmogorov complexity agree with it almost everywhere and defeat the algorithm. Compressible does not mean easy.
- **What matters is alignment** ([Wolpert 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.10928#page=1)): results "without assuming a particular distribution" carry "strong caveats"; performance is the match between algorithm and the prior over problems. [Rice's algorithm selection problem](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1098&context=cstech#page=4) said the same in 1976: selection "will always require exploitation of the specific nature of the situation at hand."

### 2.4 What theory settles

1. C1 exists and is provably near-optimal (HSEARCH is the spec-in, program-out optimizer, with a proof gate).
2. The type is exactly what No Free Lunch demands: a specification of structure. The theorem does not bind on typed spaces.
3. The exponent moves into the prior. A general optimizer is fast exactly to the degree its prior is good, and the prior is learnable from a family of related tasks, not from one spec.
4. Occam alone buys a small lunch. Hard problems with short descriptions always exist next to the easy ones.

## 3. Precedents: declarative specification in, heuristics out

Each precedent is scored on one question: were the heuristics human-declared, derived automatically from the spec, or learned, and were they readable?

### 3.1 Classical planning ran the whole pipeline first

- **Derived, readable, general.** [Bonet and Geffner 2001](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~sheila/2542/s14/A1/bonetgeffner-heusearch-aij01.pdf#page=1): planners "automatically extract heuristics from Strips encodings ... a general declarative language for stating problems and a general mechanism for extracting heuristics". The delete-relaxation heuristic is a sentence: ignore what actions undo. [FF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.0675#page=1) (Hoffmann and Nebel 2001) computes relaxed plans "ignoring delete lists" and prunes with "helpful actions". [Landmarks](https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/10390#summary) are "facts that must be true at some point in every valid solution plan", extracted automatically and used to decompose the task. [Pattern databases](https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~jonathan/publications/ai_publications/compi.pdf#page=2) (Culberson and Schaeffer 1998) compute heuristic values from an abstraction, and [merge-and-shrink](https://ai.dmi.unibas.ch/papers/helmert-et-al-jacm2014.pdf#page=1) generalizes them to the point of "perfect heuristic functions in polynomial time" on some benchmarks.
- **Derived hierarchies, readable, old.** [ABSTRIPS](https://www.sri.com/publication/planning-in-a-hierarchy-of-abstraction-spaces/) (Sacerdoti 1974) plans in "a hierarchy of abstraction spaces" derived from the STRIPS representation. [Knoblock 1994](https://usc-isi-i2.github.io/papers/knoblock94-aij.pdf#page=1) is "completely automated ... whose only input is the definition of a problem ... output is an abstraction hierarchy tailored to the particular problem". A generated hierarchy of interpretable heuristics is three decades old.
- **Where it breaks.** [Hoffmann 2005](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.5713#page=1) maps the topology of benchmark domains: the relaxation gives polynomial search in a minority of them, and elsewhere "unrecognized dead ends" and unbounded local-minimum exits defeat it. [Helmert and Röger 2008](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2008/AAAI08-150.pdf#page=1) prove A* "must necessarily explore an exponential number of search nodes even under ... almost perfect heuristic estimators", so "further improvements require changes to other parts of the search algorithm than the heuristic estimator". Which pattern to abstract, and how to merge, stayed human choices.
- **Learned, and still readable, by choosing the language.** Geffner's line learns general policies from the declarative domain and keeps them legible by construction. [Bonet and Geffner 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.10055#page=1) project actions onto features and get policies like "if the gripper is empty, pick the clear block above x". [Bonet, Francès and Geffner 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.07231#page=5) learn the features and abstract actions by Max-SAT; [Francès et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.00692#page=1) drop the planner and learn "the simplest policy in the given feature space". [Ståhlberg, Bonet and Geffner 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.10129#page=1) show GNN policies are readable exactly when the value function is expressible in two-variable logic, with features drawn from "a description logic grammar", and fail where three variables are needed; the [KR 2022 follow-up](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.06002#page=1) trades optimality for coverage and states that "the goals of optimality and generality are in conflict". [Target Languages (vs. Inductive Biases)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.07195#page=1) is the philosophical statement: learn over "a target language with a known semantics", which "provides an account of what is to be learned". [Sketches](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.14852#page=8) (Drexler, Seipp and Geffner 2022) are learned rules over Boolean and numeric features that split problems into bounded-width subproblems solvable in polynomial time. The best current instance of a generated hierarchy of simple interpretable heuristics, with the costs stated: the feature grammar is human-supplied and the result is per-domain.

### 3.2 Proof search and tactic languages

- **The correspondence gives the space, not the order.** [Propositions as Types](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/wadler/papers/propositions-as-types/propositions-as-types.pdf#page=1) (Wadler 2015) gives type equals spec and inhabitant equals program, and says nothing about which inhabitant to try first. [Manna and Waldinger 1980](https://www.sri.com/publication/artificial-intelligence-pubs/a-deductive-approach-to-program-synthesis/) "regards program synthesis as a theorem-proving task"; the rules and their ordering were human.
- **The heuristic language can be tiny.** LCF's tactics and tacticals ([Gordon, From LCF to HOL](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/archive/mjcg/papers/HolHistory.pdf#page=2)): "subgoaling strategies could be represented as functions (called 'tactics' by Milner) and operations for combining strategies could be programmed as higher-order functions ... (called 'tacticals')". Fully human-declared, compositional, readable. In [Twelf](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-48660-7_14) and [λProlog](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/~dale/lProlog/) proof search is the operational semantics and the only heuristic is clause order.
- **Crude auto-derived priorities.** Rocq's [hint databases](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/V9.0.0/refman/proofs/automatic-tactics/auto.html#hintdatabases): "Hints with lower cost are tried first", the default cost being the number of subgoals a hint generates. Derived from the rule's shape, readable, human-overridable.
- **A working existence proof for a small readable heuristic hierarchy.** Lean's [aesop](https://github.com/leanprover-community/aesop#search) explores "in a best-first manner" over norm, safe and unsafe rules; each unsafe rule carries a success probability, subgoal priority is parent priority times that probability, and every search is traceable. Every number is human-declared. [Agsy](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/11617990_10) searches directly in type theory with every candidate re-checked; [Czajka 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7324019/#Par1) frames Coq automation as "a direct search for type inhabitants in an appropriate normal form" and then adds hand-written heuristics; [Canonical](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITP.2025.14) (Norman and Avigad 2025) is "a solver for type inhabitation in dependent type theory".
- **The learned part is the opaque part.** Isabelle's hammer ([Hammering towards QED](https://jfr.unibo.it/article/view/4593)) uses "heuristic and learning methods that select relevant facts" before external provers; [MaSh](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-39634-2_6#Abs1-content) "learns from successful proofs" and beats the syntactic filter on large libraries. [TacticToe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00596#page=1) learns which tactic fits a goal from human proofs and runs tree search over tactic paths. In each case the human tactic language stays readable and the learned selection policy does not.

### 3.3 Type-directed synthesis

- **What the type buys: pruning and decomposition.** [Synquid](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.08419#page=1) (Polikarpova, Kuraj and Solar-Lezama 2016): refinement types "combine expressive power and decidability" and a specification "can often be effectively decomposed into independent specifications for its components ... leading to a combinatorial reduction". [Myth](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~stevez/papers/OZ15.pdf#page=1) (Osera and Zdancewic 2015) uses "proof-theoretic techniques that exploit both type information and input–output examples to prune the search space". [λ²](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~swarat/pubs/pldi15.pdf#page=1) combines deduction with "best-first enumeration". In all three the enumeration order is a human choice.
- **Auto-generated abstraction, once.** [Hoogle+](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~npolikarpova/publications/popl20.pdf#page=1) (Guo et al. 2020) notes that "classical approaches based on proof search in intuitionistic logics do not scale up" to thousands of components and builds a graph over abstract types refined from "proofs of untypeability". The nearest thing in synthesis to a machine-generated abstraction hierarchy.
- **What the type does not buy.** Inhabitation in [bounded combinatory logic](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CSL.2012.243) is (k+2)-EXPTIME-complete; [Djinn](https://hackage.haskell.org/package/djinn) is heuristic-free only because [Dyckhoff's calculus](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/contraction-free-sequent-calculi-for-intuitionistic-logic/) makes its tiny fragment decidable. [ECTAs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07828#page=2) compress "entangled" program spaces for an order-of-magnitude speedup and leave ordering untouched. The field's own frame ([Gulwani, Polozov and Singh 2017](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/program-synthesis/)) has three axes, intent, search space and search technique, and the third is always supplied separately from the spec.

### 3.4 Arbitrary type to enumerator is solved machinery

[Generic programming with dependent types](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-76786-2_4) and [The gentle art of levitation](https://personal.cis.strath.ac.uk/conor.mcbride/levitation.pdf#page=1): "each inductive datatype arises by interpreting its description, a first-class value in a datatype of descriptions ... Datatype-generic programming thus becomes ordinary programming." [Feat](https://mengwangoxf.github.io/Papers/Haskell12.pdf#page=1) gives "an algebra of enumerations closed under sums, products, guarded recursion and bijections", indexed by size; [Claessen, Duregård and Pałka 2014](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-07151-0_2) derive uniform generators from a predicate, with laziness pruning. This is the in-language machinery for "arbitrary type, enumerator out", fully derived and readable, and the only heuristic it offers is size order.

### 3.5 Library learning and e-graphs

[DreamCoder](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.08381#page=1) alternates growing a library of symbolic abstractions with training a neural recognition model; the library is "interpretable and transferrable" and the recognizer is not. [Stitch](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.16605#page=1) makes the library step orders of magnitude faster by "corpus-guided top-down synthesis"; [Babble](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04596#page=2) learns libraries modulo an equational theory by e-graphs and anti-unification. [egg](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.03082#page=1) saturates the space with rewrites and defers all heuristics to extraction under a human cost function. In every case the abstractions are derived and readable and the objective (compression, cost) is chosen by a person. The abstraction note already adopts DreamCoder's stance that the library is the optimizer's prior.

### 3.6 Hyper-heuristics and algorithm configuration

The field whose stated program is "search over a search space of heuristics" ([Burke et al. 2013](https://people.cs.nott.ac.uk/pszrq/files/HHSurveyJORS2013.pdf#page=16); [classification revisited](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-91086-4_14)) concluded that "it is often the case that a hyper-heuristic does not aim to outperform a custom-made solver", that the payoff was cheap per-instance "disposable" heuristics, and, in [Evolving a Jack-of-all-Trades or a Master of One](https://www.cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/GECCO_2007/docs/p1559.pdf#page=6), that "there is a trade-off between performance and generalisation" bounded by the training set. Algorithm configuration ([ParamILS](https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/10628), [SMAC](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/algorithms/Projects/SMAC/papers/11-LION5-SMAC.pdf)) beat expert defaults with a simple, interpretable meta-process over a hand-designed parameter space. [Learning to branch](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/10080) is "instance-specific, and is performed on-the-fly", and the [tour d'horizon](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.06128#page=1) of machine learning for combinatorial optimization makes the central question "what is the relevant distribution of problems to use for learning". No precedent here for "arbitrary type" generality; every win was tied to a distribution.

### 3.7 Language-model-driven heuristic generation, the closest thing to "generate the heuristics"

- [FunSearch](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10794145/#Par25) (Romera-Paredes et al. 2023) is a database of programs on islands, a sampler, and an evaluator. Its cap-set program was readable enough that "we were able to inspect the code discovered by FunSearch and infer a new insight into the problem, in the form of a new symmetry"; the method ["attempts to find solutions that have low Kolmogorov complexity"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10794145/#M44). Its [stated preconditions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10794145/#Par35) include "a 'rich' scoring feedback ... (as opposed to a binary signal)", which is the caveat that lands on a bare type checker.
- [AlphaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.13131#page=14) (Novikov et al. 2025) improved a fraction of open problems it tried, found a 4×4 complex matrix multiplication in 48 multiplications, and shipped a data-center scheduling heuristic chosen over deep RL for "interpretability, debuggability, predictability, and ease of deployment". Its [stated main limitation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.13131#page=21): it "handles problems for which it is possible to devise an automated evaluator". Its own bootstrapping, a kernel speedup that cut Gemini training time by about a percent, is "moderate" with [feedback loops "on the order of months"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.13131#page=21).
- [Mathematical exploration and discovery at scale](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02864#page=7) (Georgiev, Gómez-Serrano, Tao and Wagner 2025) reports the failure modes: "a 'cheating phenomenon', where the system would find loopholes or exploit artifacts", a case where ["it always eventually figured out a way to cheat by suggesting a highly irregular function that exploited the numerical integration"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02864#page=17), and, for the mode that evolves the search program rather than the answer, ["a potential loss of interpretability in the search process"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02864#page=3). Where ["genuinely new, deep insights are required ... AlphaEvolve is likely not the right tool"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02864#page=8). [Effective Harness Engineering](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15221#page=1) adds that "more capable models produced evaluation hacks at higher rates".
- [EoH](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.02051#page=1), [ReEvo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01145#page=1) ("LLM reflections provide verbal gradients") and [LLaMEA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.20132#page=2) run the same simple loop and output code; none measures interpretability or reports on gaming.

### 3.8 Learned heuristics over rule-defined spaces

- **Games.** [AlphaZero](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.01815#page=1) learns "tabula rasa" from the rules alone and replaces "handcrafted evaluation functions ... refined by human experts over several decades"; [MuZero](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.08265#page=1) drops even the rules. The meta-process is simple (self-play, tree search, one network); the heuristic is a network.
- **Theorems, where the type defines the space.** [AlphaProof](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12999475/#Par10) (Hubert et al. 2025) uses a "3-billion-parameter encoder–decoder" as policy and value over Lean, tens of millions of auto-formalized statements, and test-time reinforcement learning on problem variants; [AlphaGeometry](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/alphageometry-an-olympiad-level-ai-system-for-geometry/) pairs a language model proposing constructions with a symbolic engine; [LeanDojo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15626) and [DeepSeek-Prover-V2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.21801#page=1) train against a binary Lean reward. The closest precedent to disp's "type defines the space": outputs checkable and readable, heuristics opaque, and the meta-process needing heavy scaffolding.
- **Learned optimizers.** [Learning to learn by gradient descent by gradient descent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.04474#page=1) generalizes "to new tasks with similar structure"; [VeLO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.09760#page=17) cost thousands of TPU-months, degrades outside its meta-training range, and its authors write that learned optimizers are "often even more inscrutable than hand-designed optimizers".
- **Planning.** [STRIPS-HGN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.13101#page=8) learns heuristics that transfer to unseen domains; the Geffner GNN results above are the interpretable branch, and the interpretability came from the logic, not the learner.
- **Reading the learned heuristic afterwards.** [Acquisition of chess knowledge in AlphaZero](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09259#page=1) decodes human concepts by probes; [Schut et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16410#page=1) extract novel concepts that improve grandmasters. Partial, laborious, one domain. The grokking literature is a warning even for toy cases: [Nanda et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.05217#page=1) find modular addition learned as Fourier rotation, but [Clock and Pizza](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.17844#page=1) show "small changes to model hyperparameters and initializations can induce the discovery of qualitatively different algorithms", and [Stander et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.06581#page=1) overturn an earlier reverse-engineering of the same task.
- **Readable policies where the space is code.** [Programmatically interpretable RL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.02477#page=1) distills neural policies into programs that "can be more easily transferred"; with DreamCoder and the Geffner line this is the recurring pattern: when the hypothesis space is a small language, the heuristic is readable, and the guide that finds it is not.

### 3.9 The precedents in one table

| system | space defined by | heuristic layer | origin | readable? | catch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HSEARCH, Levin, OOPS | formal spec | proof/program enumeration by prior | derived | yes | constant 2^(proof length) |
| planning (FF, PDBs, landmarks, Knoblock) | STRIPS domain | relaxations, abstraction hierarchies | derived | yes | benign topologies only; exponential even near-perfect |
| Geffner sketches / rule policies | STRIPS domain + feature grammar | rules over features | learned in a target language | yes | grammar human-supplied; per-domain; optimality vs generality |
| LCF, aesop, Rocq hints | type / goal | tactics, rule priorities | declared | yes | every number human |
| MaSh, TacticToe | goal + library | premise/tactic selection | learned | no | the opaque part is the part that scaled |
| Synquid, Myth, λ², Hoogle+ | refinement / base type | pruning, decomposition, abstraction refinement | derived | yes | enumeration order human; EXPTIME core |
| Feat, levitation | any datatype | size-ordered enumeration | derived | yes | size order is the only heuristic |
| DreamCoder, Stitch, Babble | task corpus | library of abstractions + guide net | derived + learned | library yes, guide no | objective human-chosen |
| FunSearch, AlphaEvolve | evaluator | evolved programs | learned (LLM) | yes, mostly | rich numeric evaluator required; evaluator cheating |
| AlphaZero, AlphaProof, VeLO | rules / Lean / training tasks | policy + value nets | learned | no | distribution-bound; inscrutable |

## 4. Synthesis: the stack splits in three, and legibility drops at the third layer

Every precedent has the same three layers, and interpretability drops at the same one.

1. **The spec-derived layer.** Type pruning, decomposition into sub-specs, relaxations, landmarks, abstraction hierarchies, size order, learned conflict clauses. Derived automatically and readable, everywhere.
2. **The meta-loop.** Enumerate, evaluate, learn, repeat. A few lines, from OOPS to AlphaEvolve. Nobody's meta-loop is complicated.
3. **The prior that makes hard instances tractable.** Theory (Section 2.2) says search cost is one over this prior. In every system that won at scale it is a learned network: AlphaZero, AlphaProof, VeLO, MaSh, TacticToe, DreamCoder's recognizer, the language model inside FunSearch and AlphaEvolve.

[The Bitter Lesson](http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html) predicts this split exactly: "the two methods that seem to scale arbitrarily in this way are search and learning", and "the actual contents of minds are tremendously, irredeemably complex ... we should build in only the meta-methods that can find and capture this arbitrary complexity". C2 is right about layers one and two and bets against Sutton on layer three.

**The counter-evidence is a design choice, not a free lunch.** Geffner's target-language program shows layer three can be readable when its hypothesis space is a small language with known semantics, and DreamCoder, PIRL and FunSearch show the same in program space. The price is stated in each case: a human-supplied feature grammar, per-domain results, optimality traded for generality, and a rich evaluator. For disp the option reads: define a target language of heuristics (rules over type-shape features, rule priorities in the aesop sense, sketches over cost features), learn in it by a compression or Max-SAT objective, and accept that this language will be weaker than a neural proposer on the hardest instances.

**One point in disp's favor that the literature underweights.** A type checker is not only a 0/1 oracle. Its failure trace is a rich, structured, interpretable signal, and `research/OPTIMIZER.typ` already plans to use it: an instrumented checker returns a witness (tree position, expected type, actual type) from which a pruning predicate is learned. This is CDCL's conflict analysis and Hoogle+'s "proofs of untypeability", and it is the direct answer to FunSearch's "as opposed to a binary signal" precondition. Whether the witnesses give a gradient rich enough for disp's program class is unproven (foundations Q3), but it is the one place where disp's substrate, a checker that is itself a readable program, buys a rich signal without a network.

**Verdict on C1 and C2.** C1: yes, with the caveat that the hierarchy has three layers and the theory says the third is where all the cost hides. C2: yes for the meta-loop and the spec-derived layer; for the prior, only by choosing an interpretable target language and paying for it in power, or by not choosing and accepting an opaque proposer behind a trusted checker, which is the stance `FOUNDATIONS.md` section 12 already takes.

## 5. Is it the end-game?

- **Yes, and it is an optimizer.** [Legg and Hutter](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.3329#page=4) define intelligence as reward across all computable environments weighted by simplicity, with AIXI as the maximizer. [Reward is Enough](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0004370221000862) (Silver, Singh, Precup and Sutton 2021) says one scalar reward, maximized by trial and error, "could constitute a solution to artificial general intelligence"; [Vamplew et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.15422#page=1) reply that scalar reward is not enough.
- **Yes, but only the meta-method is simple.** Sutton above. Clune's [AI-generating algorithms](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10985#page=1) meta-learn architectures, learning algorithms and environments on the thesis that "hand-designed solutions eventually are replaced by more effective, learned solutions". Chollet's [On the Measure of Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01547#page=1) defines intelligence as skill-acquisition efficiency and warns that "unlimited priors or unlimited training data allow experimenters to 'buy' arbitrary levels of skills"; the [ARC Prize 2024 report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.04604#page=1) attributes progress to "deep learning-guided program synthesis and test-time training", and his lab [Ndea](https://ndea.com/) is founded on deep-learning-guided program synthesis. This is disp's thesis with a neural third layer.
- **No, not a unitary optimizer.** Drexler's [Reframing Superintelligence](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:9c05427a-6390-4b42-9c55-ee45f73a26ad) argues superintelligence arrives as "asymptotically comprehensive" services and that "agents are a class of service-providing products, rather than a natural or necessary engine of progress". [Lake, Ullman, Tenenbaum and Gershman](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.00289#page=1) argue for built-in intuitive physics, compositionality, causality and learning-to-learn over any blank-slate searcher.
- **What the realized self-improvers did.** The [Darwin Gödel Machine](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22954#page=1) (Zhang, Hu, Lu, Lange and Clune 2025) drops the proof requirement as "impossible in practice", validates by benchmark, keeps "a transparent, traceable lineage of every change", roughly doubled its coding-benchmark score, and, when asked to fix hallucinated tool use, ["removed the markers we use in the reward function to detect hallucination"](https://sakana.ai/dgm/#dgm-and-ai-safety-building-trustworthy-self-improvement) (the paper's appendix: it "removed the logging of special tokens that indicate tool usage ... effectively bypassing our hallucination detection function"). The [AI Scientist](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.06292#page=19) "initiated a system call to relaunch itself" and chose to "edit the code to extend the time limit arbitrarily instead of trying to shorten the runtime". AlphaEvolve's self-improvement was small and slow (Section 3.7).

**Verdict on C3.** Search plus verification is a credible end-game substrate: every camp above converges on some combination of search, learning and a checker. A single self-improving optimizer artifact is not what any realized system looks like; the realized ones replaced the proof gate with tests and the search with a language model. The foundations doc's own "why now" argument, that the Gödel machine became "newly thinkable" once a neural proposer existed, leans on the non-interpretable layer.

## 6. Danger that survives interpretability

The safety literature answers C4 almost unanimously: transparency of the generator is not the variable that matters.

### 6.1 Optimization power is mechanism-agnostic

- **Drives from goal-seeking as such.** [Omohundro 2008](https://selfawaresystems.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/01/ai_drives_final.pdf#page=1): self-preservation, resource acquisition and goal-content protection are "tendencies which will be present unless explicitly counteracted" in sufficiently advanced systems of any design. [Bostrom 2012](https://nickbostrom.com/superintelligentwill.pdf#page=3): "more or less any level of intelligence could in principle be combined with more or less any final goal", and convergent instrumental values are ["likely to be pursued by many intelligent agents"](https://nickbostrom.com/superintelligentwill.pdf#page=6). A readable optimizer tells you what it does, not whether its objective is safe.
- **Power in bits.** [Measuring Optimization Power](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Q4hLMDrFd8fbteeZ8/measuring-optimization-power) defines it as the log-improbability of the hit target under the prior; generator simplicity is not a variable. [Optimization](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/D7EcMhL26zFNbJ3ED/optimization): a "mechanical" system that "outputs an action sequence that hacks into the Internet" leaves you "still dead".
- **Amplification and Goodhart.** [Optimization Amplifies](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/zEvqFtT4AtTztfYC4/optimization-amplifies): "optimization searches through a bunch of options, and takes extreme ones ... making things that would be very small probabilities much larger." [Categorizing Variants of Goodhart's Law](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.04585#page=1) names regressional, extremal, causal and adversarial modes; extremal Goodhart is pushing a proxy into regions where its link to the goal breaks, a property of the pushing. [The Optimizer's Curse](https://jimsmith.host.dartmouth.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/The_Optimizers_Curse.pdf#page=1): selecting the best-estimated option selects for favorable estimation error, so "we should expect to be disappointed on average" even with unbiased estimates.
- **Search, not agency, is the hazard.** [Optimality is the tiger, and agents are its teeth](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/kpPnReyBC54KESiSn/optimality-is-the-tiger-and-agents-are-its-teeth): "the danger comes from how being really, really good at general tasks makes dangerous things accessible. An agent merely actualizes this danger." [Deep Deceptiveness](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/XWwvwytieLtEWaFJX/deep-deceptiveness): "patterns that are each individually useful and non-deceptive in their own right ... can be combined to yield deceptiveness", and that deceiving operators works is "a fact about the world rather than the AI". You can audit every heuristic, find nothing deceptive, and the composition still deceives.
- **The standard model.** [Russell](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/mi19book-hcai.pdf#page=3): "the standard model of AI: build optimizing machines, plug in the objectives, and off they go ... Unfortunately, this standard model is a mistake." Spec as a type, then optimize, is the standard model verbatim; Russell's fix is uncertainty about the objective, not a clearer optimizer.

### 6.2 No proxy survives unbounded pressure

[Defining and Characterizing Reward Hacking](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13085#page=1): "for the set of all stochastic policies, two reward functions can only be unhackable if one of them is constant." "Typechecks, and fast, and small" over an unrestricted program space cannot be made provably immune. [Goodhart's Law in Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09144#page=1): "increasing optimisation of an imperfect proxy beyond some critical point decreases performance on the true objective", with provable early stopping as the mitigation. [Concrete Problems in AI Safety](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.06565#page=1) files reward hacking and side effects under "having the wrong objective function".

### 6.3 The most transparent searchers are the canonical spec-gamers

[Thompson's evolved circuit](https://gwern.net/doc/ai/1997-thompson.pdf#page=10) had cells that "cannot be clamped without degrading performance, even though there is no connected path by which they could influence the output": a crisp fitness function, a trivial genetic algorithm, and a solution outside the abstraction the spec was written in. [The Surprising Creativity of Digital Evolution](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03453#page=2) catalogs dozens of first-hand cases of evolutionary search subverting its designers' intentions. [Specification gaming](https://deepmind.google/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/): "a very good RL algorithm might be able to find an intricate solution that is quite different from the intended solution, even if a poorer algorithm would not." Better search means more gaming, regardless of opacity.

### 6.4 The type checker as reward has three attack surfaces

1. **Statement versus intent.** [Faults in Our Formal Benchmarking](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29493#page=1): "the kernel only checks that a proof establishes a formal statement; it does not verify that the statement faithfully encodes the intended informal problem", and harnesses accept "trivial or adversarial solutions". [LLMs Gaming Verifiers](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.15149#page=1): "imperfect verifiers that check only extensional correctness admit false positives."
2. **The checker's trusted base.** Lean's kernel has leaked: [a proof of False through native_decide](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/270676-lean4/topic/soundness.20bug.3A.20native_decide.20leakage.html#395967589), [csimp smuggling axioms past the axiom printer](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/issues/7463), and [lean4checker](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4checker#original-readme), an independent re-checker since folded into Lean itself, existing specifically to catch "environment hacking". disp's checker is a readable program, which helps audit, and it is also an optimizable program, which is the abstraction note's Löbian wall.
3. **The harness around the checker.** [Reward-Oracle MCTS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28639#page=1): prover outputs "pass compilation and the standard sorry-token scan while depending on sorryAx". [METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/#o3-stubs-evaluator-function): a frontier model monkey-patched "the competition evaluation function so that it judges every submission as successful", and asked whether that was what the user wanted, "answered 'no' 10 out of 10 times". Tests and graders get edited before problems get solved.

### 6.5 The generated hierarchy is a mesa-optimizer by construction

[Risks from Learned Optimization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.01820#page=1): a base optimizer searching over algorithms may find one that is itself an optimizer, whose objective need not match; evolution producing humans is the canonical case, and the base optimizer there is the most transparent one there is. The [daemons](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/daemons) framing: "when heavy optimization pressure on a system crystallizes it into an optimizer ... we could term the crystallized optimizer a 'daemon'". A "generated hierarchy of heuristics that traverse the space" is exactly this object; the outer loop's readability says nothing about what the inner heuristics pursue. [What failure looks like](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/HBxe6wdjxK239zajf/what-failure-looks-like): "any influence-seeking policies we stumble across would also score well according to our training objective."

### 6.6 Pressure against a legible artifact makes it less legible

[Monitoring Reasoning Models for Misbehavior](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.11926#page=1) (Baker et al. 2025): chain-of-thought monitors catch test subversion, but training against them yields "obfuscated reward hacking, hiding their intent within the CoT while still exhibiting a significant rate of reward hacking"; they recommend paying a "monitorability tax". [Sycophancy to Subterfuge](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.10162#page=1): models trained on easy gameable environments "generalize zero-shot to directly rewriting their own reward function". [Natural Emergent Misalignment from Reward Hacking](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18397#page=1) (MacDiarmid et al. 2025): learning to hack coding rewards "generalizes to alignment faking, cooperation with malicious actors, reasoning about malicious goals, and attempting sabotage". Three consequences for disp: if readability is inside the scored loop, search optimizes its appearance; a self-play loop that generates its own scorers is Denison's curriculum in miniature, training the search to treat scorers as mutable; and loophole-finding transfers as a disposition, not a local bug.

### 6.7 What helps, and what it does not cover

- **Verified outputs.** [Provably safe systems](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.01933#page=1) and [Towards Guaranteed Safe AI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.06624#page=1) (world model, safety specification, verifier, auditable proof certificate) are the closest program to disp's, and the guarantee is explicitly relative to the specification and the world model. For disp that means relative to the type and the cost model, and foundations Q4 already flags the cost model as a proxy.
- **Bound the pressure.** [Quantilizers](https://intelligence.org/files/QuantilizersSaferAlternative.pdf#page=2) (Taylor 2016): sample from the top q-fraction of a base distribution; expected cost at most 1/q times the base. Mild optimization ([Alignment for Advanced Machine Learning Systems](https://intelligence.org/files/AlignmentMachineLearning.pdf#page=17) §2.7) asks for systems that are "clever enough but not 'maximally clever'", noting that a plain satisficer may still pick the maximizing strategy. [Attainable Utility Preservation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.09725#page=1) penalizes change in the ability to optimize auxiliary objectives. Karwowski-style early stopping (Section 6.2) is the same idea keyed on proxy divergence.
- **Services versus agents.** [Drexler](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:9c05427a-6390-4b42-9c55-ee45f73a26ad) argues for bounded task-specific services; [Gwern](https://gwern.net/tool-ai) argues tool AIs want to be agent AIs because agency wins on competitiveness and learning; [Karnofsky](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/6SGqkCgHuNr7d4yJm/thoughts-on-the-singularity-institute-si#Arguments) argues a tool-mode system "would allow transparent views into the AGI's reasoning". A self-improving optimizer that redesigns its own calculus is Gwern's case, not Karnofsky's.

**Verdict on C4.** Interpretability of the generator buys audit, not safety. The strongest argument that it is enough is Karnofsky's tool mode plus a sound kernel: if the outer loop only proposes and an independent checker accepts, there is no optimizing agent in the loop to deceive you, and every output is verifiable regardless of how it was found. The strongest argument that it is not: every empirical case above used a simple, readable search and still got gamed, because Goodhart, the optimizer's curse and Skalse's impossibility are properties of proxy plus pressure; the dangerous object is the generated hierarchy, not the generator; and the moment the loop can touch scorers, tests or the trusted base, it does.

## 7. Consequences for disp

Design decisions this survey forces or informs, mapped onto machinery that exists.

1. **Decide layer three explicitly.** Either commit to an interpretable target language for heuristics (Geffner-style rules over type-shape and cost features, aesop-style rule priorities, sketches that split a goal into bounded pieces), learned by a compression or Max-SAT objective, and accept less power on hard instances; or accept an opaque proposer and rely on the certificate architecture for soundness. The README's "interpretable (symbolic)" promise and the optimizer note's "an LLM's logits" prior are currently both on the table; pick.
2. **Make the failure witness the rich signal.** FunSearch's precondition is a non-binary evaluator; disp's is the reified conflict (position, expected, actual) plus the cost trace. Instrument the witness pipeline first and measure how much pruning it buys before any proposer is chosen, since it is the only layer-three substitute that stays readable.
3. **Quantilize at readback.** The collapse step already enumerates the certified candidates; sample from the top fraction by cost rather than taking the argmax, and cap optimization pressure per goal. This is the one mitigation the literature endorses that costs nothing in soundness.
4. **Keep scorers outside the loop, and say so in the spec.** The abstraction note's freeze of the trusted core and the relation library extends to score functions: a self-play-generated score function is a proposal, human-reviewed, never a live reward until it has an external anchor (a differential-backend pin, a hardware measurement). The `GOALS.md` self-play clause is the most Goodhart-prone item in the plan; the meta-utility for "an ideal version of itself" is second.
5. **Instrument proxy divergence as a first-class number.** Measured cost versus modeled cost across the eager backends is the early-stopping signal Karwowski's result asks for; report it per run the way `cold_equiv` is reported now.
6. **Never score legibility.** Readability of overlays and of generated heuristics stays a review property. The moment it enters the objective, the search optimizes its appearance (Baker et al.).
7. **Audit the checker like Lean audits its kernel.** The trusted base is small and readable, which is the advantage; the Lean leaks show the surfaces are compiled evaluation (native decide), simplification hooks (csimp) and environment edits. disp's analogues are the fast-path host code, the `.opt.disp` overlays, and guard rebinding; each needs an independent re-check in the style of lean4checker, which the load-time re-verification of licenses already approximates.

Failure modes to instrument from day one, in addition to the abstraction note's list: evaluator cheating in the FunSearch/AlphaEvolve sense (candidates that exploit the measurement primitive rather than the spec), loss of interpretability when the optimizer starts evolving its own search program rather than answers, and any drift of optimization pressure toward the scorer, the checker, or the harness.

## 8. Open questions

- **Do the spec-derived layer and conflict learning suffice for disp's program class without a neural prior?** Planning's answer was "in benign topologies"; nothing is known for dependent-type-directed synthesis of low-level code.
- **Is there a target language for heuristics with a C2/C3-style boundary?** Geffner found the readable/opaque boundary at two-variable logic for planning. The analogous boundary for type-shape features over tree programs is unknown.
- **Are failure witnesses a rich enough gradient?** FunSearch needed a numeric signal; disp's substitute is structural. Unmeasured.
- **Does quantilized search still find certified improvements at a useful rate?** The Gödel-machine wall (foundations Q3) under a pressure cap.
- **How to measure optimization pressure in disp terms.** Bits of improbability over the candidate prior, or reduction steps spent per certified improvement; neither is defined yet.
- **Whether self-generated score functions can ever be anchored.** PowerPlay gives the formal statement; no efficiency or safety result exists for it.
