# Diffusion denoisers vs JEPA-style latent predictors and latent world models

Hypothesis under test: "World models predict the next latent; diffusion models predict a denoising direction. If you think of the denoiser as an encoder-predictor-decoder system, it is fundamentally predicting a denoising step on a latent and then outputting that step. Is this actually related?"

Short verdict, unpacked below. Yes, closely, and the link can be stated exactly. Both are regressions whose optimum is a conditional expectation of a clean target given a corrupted view. Latent diffusion really is encoder → predictor in latent space → decoder. The analogy breaks in four places: (1) the corruption family (Gaussian at every level vs masking at one level), (2) whether the target space is fixed or learned (learned targets are why JEPA needs anti-collapse machinery), (3) the loss (V-JEPA's L1 gives a median, not a mean), and (4) a single conditional mean vs a whole family of them over noise levels. Only the family can be sampled from. One correction to the wording: the denoiser does not output a step. It outputs a conditional mean (equivalently a score, or a velocity, under known affine maps), and the sampler turns that into a step.

Notation used throughout. Gaussian corruption: `x_t = α_t x_0 + σ_t ε`, `ε ~ N(0, I)`. Masking corruption: each token is independently kept with probability `α_t`, otherwise replaced by `[MASK]`. JEPA: context encoder `f_θ`, EMA target encoder `f_θ̄`, predictor `g_φ`, target `s_y = f_θ̄(y)`.

## Q1. Tweedie / Miyasawa: what does the optimal L2 denoiser compute, and is an L2-trained JEPA a one-noise-level denoiser in a learned space?

### Takeaway
The optimal L2 denoiser outputs `E[x_0 | x_t]`. Under additive Gaussian noise its residual equals the scaled score of the noise-blurred density (Tweedie/Miyasawa). An L2-trained JEPA predictor (I-JEPA) also converges to a conditional mean, `E[s_y | s_x, positions]`, so it is a "one-corruption-level conditional-mean estimator in a learned space". It is *not* a denoiser in the Tweedie sense, for three reasons. Its corruption is masking rather than additive Gaussian noise. Its input and output live in different spaces, so there is no residual to read a score from. And V-JEPA / V-JEPA 2 use L1, whose optimum is a conditional *median*.

### Cited Findings
- **Tweedie's formula** dates to Robbins (1956). For `z ~ N(μ, σ²)` it gives the posterior mean `E[μ | z] = z + σ² d/dz log p(z)` from the marginal density alone (empirical Bayes). Verified quote: "Tweedie's formula, first reported by Robbins in 1956" — [Efron 2011, JASA (PMC author manuscript)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3325056/#P1)
- **Miyasawa (1961)**, as restated by Kadkhodaie & Simoncelli: the least-squares (MMSE) solution for removing additive Gaussian noise can be written in terms of the gradient of the log of the noisy-observation density. Verified quote: "who showed that the least-squares solution for removing additive Gaussian" — [Kadkhodaie & Simoncelli 2021, arXiv 2007.13640, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.13640#page=1)
- **The denoiser residual is proportional to the score of the noisy density**, `f(y) = x̂(y) − y ∝ ∇_y log p(y)`. They use this residual as a gradient in a coarse-to-fine stochastic ascent that samples from the prior implicit in a denoiser. Verified quote: "residual of the denoising function, which is proportional to the gradient of" — [Kadkhodaie & Simoncelli 2021, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.13640#page=4)
- **Modern diffusion form (EDM).** If `D(x; σ)` minimizes `E‖D(y + n; σ) − y‖²` with `n ~ N(0, σ²I)`, then `∇_x log p(x; σ) = (D(x; σ) − x) / σ²` (Eqs. 2–3). Karras et al. gloss this: "the score function isolates the noise component from the signal". Verified quote: "the score function isolates the noise component from the signal" — [Karras et al. 2022 (EDM), arXiv 2206.00364, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364#page=3)
- **Denoising = score matching.** A denoising autoencoder trained with Gaussian corruption is equivalent to matching the score of a Parzen-smoothed data density. Verified quote: "simple denoising autoencoder training criterion is equivalent to matching the score" — [Vincent 2011, Neural Computation (mirror PDF)](https://gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/diffusion/2011-vincent.pdf#page=1)
- **Score matching itself** minimizes the expected squared distance between the model's and the data's log-density gradients. Verified quote: "expected squared distance between the gradient of the log-density" — [Hyvärinen 2005, JMLR, p.1](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume6/hyvarinen05a/hyvarinen05a.pdf#page=1)
- **Generalization beyond the Gaussian case.** Raphan & Simoncelli write least-squares estimators "in terms of the measurement density, with no explicit reference to the unknown (prior) density", and "study the conditions under which such estimators exist". This makes the Tweedie/Miyasawa identity one instance of a wider prior-free empirical-Bayes family. Verified quote: "in terms of the measurement density, with no explicit reference to the unknown (prior) density" — [Raphan & Simoncelli 2011, Neural Computation (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3609403/#P3)
- **Recent (2026) Tweedie extensions** to non-Gaussian diffusion processes (e.g., geometric Brownian motion). None of these covers masking. Verified quote: "we extend Tweedie's formula to important non-Gaussian processes" — [arXiv 2605.19391, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19391#page=1)
- **I-JEPA's z is not a latent variable.** I-JEPA's predictor is conditioned on mask and position tokens, not on a stochastic latent. So with an L2 loss it regresses a single point per (context, target position). Verified quote: "The conditioning variable z then corresponds to a set of (possibly learnable) mask and position tokens" — [Assran et al. 2023 (I-JEPA), arXiv 2301.08243, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08243#page=3)
- **V-JEPA uses L1**, and its own analysis says the optimal predictor is the conditional median. The gradient then reduces to that of the conditional median absolute deviation: `P*(E_θ(x)) = median(Y | E_θ(x))`. Verified quotes: "regression, which we found to be more stable"; "median absolute deviation of a random variable conditioned on" — [Bardes et al. 2024 (V-JEPA), arXiv 2404.08471, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.08471#page=3)
- **V-JEPA 2 also uses L1** (and an EMA encoder for targets). Verified quote: "regressed to the prediction targets using an L1 loss" — [Assran et al. 2025 (V-JEPA 2), arXiv 2506.09985, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09985#page=4)

### Inferences
- **The precise statement for an L2 JEPA.** Fix the encoders (ignore that `θ̄` drifts). Then
  `g_φ* (s_x, pos) = argmin_g E ‖g(s_x, pos) − s_y‖² = E[ s_y | s_x, pos ]`.
  This has the same form as `D*(x_t, t) = E[x_0 | x_t]`. In both cases the network is a Bayes-optimal point estimator of a clean quantity given a corrupted view, at the corruption level(s) it was trained on. That much of the hypothesis is exactly right.
- **What the JEPA lacks: a score.** Tweedie's "residual = σ² × score" needs (a) additive Gaussian corruption and (b) input and output in the same space, so that `D(x) − x` makes sense. JEPA has neither. Its context is a masked view passed through `f_θ`, and its target lives in the space of `f_θ̄`. So the JEPA predictor's optimum is a conditional mean with no attached score field on the target space. The masking analogue of Tweedie exists only for categorical targets (see Q2, the RADD result).
- **One-hot targets recover the full conditional.** If targets are one-hot vectors, `E[onehot(X) | C]` *is* the full conditional probability vector. So L2 regression onto one-hot targets recovers the whole conditional distribution, which is why masked cross-entropy models (BERT, masked diffusion) are distributional. For continuous learned embeddings, L2 recovers only the first moment. This is the root of the "JEPA can't sample" gap in Q6.
- **Precision on "single noise level".** Calling I-JEPA a one-noise-level denoiser is accurate for the corruption axis (roughly one masking regime). It is inaccurate for the noise family (masking, not Gaussian) and inaccurate for V-JEPA (median, not mean).

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the original Miyasawa (1961) paper. It is cited here through Kadkhodaie & Simoncelli's restatement.
- I found no primary source that formally derives a Tweedie-like identity for a JEPA (masking corruption into a learned continuous embedding). The closest results are RADD for categorical masking (Q2) and the JEPA density result (Q3). The claim that "JEPA has no score" is my inference, not a published theorem.

## Q2. Is JEPA/MAE masking a diffusion corruption? Is a masked autoencoder one step of an absorbing-state diffusion?

### Takeaway
Yes, and this is the cleanest exact link in the whole comparison. Masking is the forward process of absorbing-state ("masked") discrete diffusion. That model's training objective is an integral over mask ratios of masked-prediction cross-entropy losses. BERT, MAE and I-JEPA each train on one slice (one mask ratio) of that integral. MaskGIT trains on the full range of ratios and samples iteratively, which makes it a masked diffusion model in all but name. DiffMAE states outright that "MAE can be viewed as a single-step patch-conditioned diffusion model". Two caveats: MAE and I-JEPA regress continuous values with L2/L1 rather than cross-entropy, and I-JEPA's targets live in a learned space.

### Cited Findings
- **D3PM introduces absorbing-state transition matrices** and uses them to connect diffusion with masked language models (BERT-style). Verified quote: "matrices that introduce absorbing states" — [Austin et al. 2021 (D3PM), arXiv 2107.03006, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03006#page=1)
- **MDLM's objective is a weighted average of masked-language-modeling losses.** Masked diffusion training is MLM training averaged over mask levels with a specific weighting. Verified quote: "it is a weighted average of masked language modeling (MLM) losses" — [Sahoo et al. 2024 (MDLM), arXiv 2406.07524, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07524#page=2)
- **The same result in continuous time** (MD4): the ELBO of masked diffusion is a weighted integral over time of cross-entropy losses. Verified quote: "objective of masked diffusion models is a simple weighted integral of cross-entropy losses" — [Shi et al. 2024, arXiv 2406.04329, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.04329#page=1)
- **The "Tweedie formula" for absorbing diffusion (RADD).** The concrete score, i.e. the ratio form of the score for discrete diffusion, equals the time-independent conditional probability of clean data times an analytic scalar that depends only on time. So the optimal masked denoiser is time-agnostic up to a known factor. Verified quote: "conditional probabilities of clean data, multiplied by a time-dependent scalar in an analytic form" — [Ou et al. 2024 (RADD), arXiv 2406.03736, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.03736#page=1)
- **Masked diffusion models do not need the time variable at all.** They are equivalent to masked models. Verified quote: "are instead equivalent to masked models" — [Zheng et al. 2024, arXiv 2409.02908, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02908#page=1)
- **BERT and MaskGIT differ in mask ratio.** BERT trains at one fixed mask ratio. MaskGIT samples the ratio from a schedule at training time and decreases it over sampling iterations, i.e. it runs a reverse absorbing process. Verified quotes: "BERT uses a fixed mask ratio of 15%" — [Chang et al. 2022 (MaskGIT), arXiv 2202.04200, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.04200#page=4); "The mask ratio is decreased until all tokens are generated" — [MaskGIT, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.04200#page=2)
- **MAE trains at one high mask ratio** and regresses pixels. Verified quote: "masking a high proportion of the input image, e.g., 75%" — [He et al. 2021 (MAE), arXiv 2111.06377, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377#page=1)
- **DiffMAE states the MAE-diffusion link directly.** Verified quotes: "MAE can be viewed as a single-step patch[-conditioned diffusion model]"; "diffusion models, the first step of whom effectively performs MAE" — [Wei et al. 2023 (DiffMAE), arXiv 2304.03283, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03283#page=5)
- **D-JEPA classifies JEPA as masked image modeling**, then turns it into a generator with a per-token diffusion loss. Verified quote: "By recognizing JEPA as a form of masked image modeling" — [Chen et al. 2024 (D-JEPA), arXiv 2410.03755, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.03755#page=1)

### Inferences
- **Formula.** With `α_t` = keep-probability, the masked diffusion ELBO is
  `L = ∫_0^1 w(t) E_{x_t ~ q_t(·|x_0)} [ Σ_{i : x_t^i = MASK} −log p_θ(x_0^i | x_t) ] dt`, with `w(t) = −α_t' / (1 − α_t)` (MDLM / MD4).
  BERT is this with the integral replaced by the single point `1 − α_t = 0.15`. MAE uses `0.75`, with L2 on pixels. I-JEPA uses roughly its multi-block mask ratio, with L2 on EMA embeddings. MaskGIT keeps the full integral and also has the iterative reverse sampler.
- **So "a masked autoencoder is one step of an absorbing diffusion" is literally true in a specific sense.** It is one time slice of the training objective (and, per DiffMAE, one reverse step). It is not true that you can sample from it. Sampling needs the denoiser at every mask ratio from 1 down to 0, plus a distributional output (cross-entropy, or a diffusion head on continuous tokens).
- **I-JEPA = absorbing-corruption, single-slice, continuous-target, learned-target-space denoiser** with an L2 point output. Each of those four qualifiers is one place where it departs from MDLM.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that trains a JEPA with mask ratios drawn across `[0,1]` and the MDLM weighting `w(t)`, i.e. a "masked-diffusion JEPA", and evaluates it as a generator in the learned space. D-JEPA and JEPA-T (Q3) come closest but add a separate diffusion head.

## Q3. Diffusion models as representation learners, and the reverse: JEPA/SSL spaces as diffusion latents (2023–2026)

### Takeaway
Representations flow in both directions. Denoising diffusion models learn linearly separable features, and stripping one down yields a classical denoising autoencoder (l-DAE) that still learns well even at a single noise level. In the other direction, diffusion training is accelerated by aligning to SSL features (REPA). Diffusing directly in a *frozen* SSL latent works (RCG, RAE, SVG). Making the diffusion latent trainable end to end makes it collapse (REPA-E), which is the JEPA problem appearing inside diffusion. JEPA-plus-diffusion hybrids (D-JEPA, JEPA-T, JEDI, Flow-JEPA, LeWAM, RoboJEPA) put a diffusion or flow head on a JEPA-style predictor to turn its point prediction into a distribution. LeJEPA's SIGReg Gaussianizes the embedding *marginal* (an anti-collapse / max-entropy constraint). That is a different thing from a diffusion model's Gaussian endpoint.

### Cited Findings
- **l-DAE: deconstructing a diffusion model down to a latent denoising autoencoder** (patch-wise PCA → add noise → predict clean image). Few modern components matter. Verified quote: "only a very few modern components are critical for learning good representations" — [Chen, Liu, Xie, He 2024 (l-DAE), arXiv 2401.14404, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.14404#page=1)
- **A single noise level is enough for representation learning.** l-DAE with a fixed noise level σ = √(1/3) loses only about 3% versus multi-level noise. Multiple noise levels matter for generation, not much for representation. Verified quote: "Using this single-level noise achieves decent accuracy of 61.5%" — [l-DAE, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.14404#page=7)
- **Unconditional diffusion networks learn linearly separable features** in their intermediate layers. Verified quote: "learned strongly linear-separable representations within its intermediate layers" — [Xiang et al. 2023 (DDAE), arXiv 2303.09769, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.09769#page=1)
- **REPA aligns diffusion hidden states to SSL features.** It aligns noisy-input hidden states of the diffusion transformer to clean features from a pretrained encoder (e.g., DINOv2). Fig. 1 reports about 17.5× faster training to the same FID. Verified quote: "aligns the projections of noisy input hidden states in denoising networks with clean" — [Yu et al. 2024 (REPA), arXiv 2410.06940, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.06940#page=1)
- **REPA-E: a trainable diffusion latent collapses.** Backpropagating the diffusion loss into the VAE collapses the latent space. The REPA alignment loss is what makes end-to-end training work. Verified quote: "causes the latent space to collapse" — [Leng et al. 2025 (REPA-E), arXiv 2504.10483, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10483#page=1)
- **RAE: diffusion in a frozen representation encoder's latent.** RAE uses frozen pretrained representation encoders (DINOv2, SigLIP, MAE) as the diffusion latent, with a trained decoder. Verified quote: "uses frozen pretrained representations as the encoder with a lightweight decoder" — [Zheng et al. 2025 (RAE), arXiv 2510.11690, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11690#page=1)
- **SVG: latent diffusion without a VAE**, on frozen DINO features plus a residual branch for detail. Verified quote: "leveraging frozen DINO features" — [Shi et al. 2025 (SVG), arXiv 2510.15301, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15301#page=1)
- **RCG: a diffusion "representation generator" in the latent space of a self-supervised encoder** (MoCo v3), followed by a pixel generator conditioned on the sampled representation. Verified quote: "we use a pre-trained self-supervised encoder (e.g., MoCo" — [Li, Katabi, He 2023 (RCG), arXiv 2312.03701, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.03701#page=2)
- **MAR: a small diffusion head turns a per-token point into a per-token distribution.** An autoregressive or masked transformer outputs a conditioning vector per token, and a small diffusion head models the distribution of that token. This is the generic recipe for turning a point predictor into a sampler. Verified quote: "model the per-token probability distribution using a diffusion procedure" — [Li et al. 2024 (MAR), arXiv 2406.11838, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11838#page=1)
- **D-JEPA: JEPA as next-token prediction plus a per-token diffusion (or flow-matching) loss**, as a generative model. Verified quote: "we incorporate diffusion loss to model the [per-token probability distribution]" — [D-JEPA, arXiv 2410.03755, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.03755#page=1)
- **JEPA-T (2025): text-to-image with a JEPA backbone** that denoises visual tokens iteratively. Verified quote: "iteratively denoising visual tokens conditioned on text" — [JEPA-T, arXiv 2510.00974, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.00974#page=1)
- **LeJEPA / SIGReg (2025): an isotropic Gaussian is the optimal embedding distribution.** It proves the isotropic Gaussian is the embedding distribution that minimizes downstream risk, enforces it with SIGReg, and drops the EMA / stop-gradient heuristics. Verified quotes: "identify the isotropic Gaussian as the optimal distribution that JEPAs' embeddings should follow"; "no stop-gradient, no teacher-student" — [Balestriero & LeCun 2025 (LeJEPA), arXiv 2511.08544, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08544#page=1)
- **JEPAs' anti-collapse term estimates data density.** The density can be read off the encoder's Jacobian ("JEPA-SCORE"), with Gaussianity motivated as the maximum-entropy choice. Verified quotes: "provably estimates the data density" — [Balestriero, Ballas, Rabbat, LeCun 2025, arXiv 2510.05949, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05949#page=1); "the Gaussian distribution is the one of maximum differential Entropy" — [same, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05949#page=2)
- **JEDI (2026): conditional diffusion in latent space is a multi-step stochastic JEPA predictor.** It derives this explicitly (appendix) and tests it on stochastic targets. Verified quotes: "the conditional diffusion model is a multi-step stochastic JEPA predictor" — [JEDI, arXiv 2605.13013, p.21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13013#page=21); "To test whether diffusion helps model stochastic targets" — [p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13013#page=6)
- **Flow-JEPA (2026): flow matching replaces one-step latent regression.** It swaps pointwise latent-transition regression in a JEPA world model for conditional flow matching over the future latent trajectory. Its page-1 abstract reports mean planning success rising from 86% to 92% (clean observations) and from 67% to 86% (noisy). Verified quotes: "switching from pointwise transition regression to stochastic trajectory-level prediction" — [F-JEPA, arXiv 2608.29029, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29029#page=1); "uses a conditional flow matching (CFM) objective to learn its evolution conditioned on the current latent state" — [p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29029#page=2)
- **LeWAM (2026): a JEPA world-action model that plans by steering a flow-matching policy's noise.** Verified quote: "steering the generative process by sampling within the policy's noise space" — [LeWAM, arXiv 2610.12407, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12407#page=2)
- **RoboJEPA (2026; authors include Ballas and Assran): the diffusion decoder is for visualization only.** It plans with CEM in V-JEPA feature space, and its pixel-space diffusion decoder is used only to visualize rollouts. Verified quote: "The decoder serves purely as a visualization tool" — [RoboJEPA, arXiv 2610.10515, p.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10515#page=13)

### Inferences
- **REPA-E is a natural experiment for the fixed-vs-learned target-space point (Q4).** Give a diffusion model a learnable target space and it collapses, exactly as a JEPA would. The fix is to anchor that space to an SSL representation (REPA-E), or to freeze it (RAE, SVG, RCG). That is the same structural role the EMA / stop-gradient / SIGReg machinery plays in JEPA.
- **RAE/RCG built around a JEPA-style encoder is the hypothesized system, built.** Freeze a JEPA-style encoder, train a diffusion model in its embedding space, train a decoder. That *is* "encoder → predictor (denoiser) in latent → decoder", with the JEPA encoder as the latent. The diffusion model then plays the role of a stochastic JEPA predictor whose context is "noise level + conditioning" instead of "visible patches".
- **SIGReg vs a diffusion prior.** LeJEPA forces the pushforward `f_θ#p_data` to be `N(0, I)`, a constraint on the encoder (like a normalizing flow's base-distribution matching). Diffusion forces `x_1` to be `N(0, I)` by *destroying* information with noise, and learns to undo it. Both use the isotropic Gaussian as a reference measure, but LeJEPA's is information-preserving. A SIGReg embedding is plausibly a well-conditioned space to run latent diffusion in, but I found no paper testing that.

### Gaps
- No primary source found that runs latent diffusion in a LeJEPA/SIGReg embedding, or that relates SIGReg formally to a diffusion terminal distribution.
- The search surfaced further 2026 titles that I did not verify and so do not rely on: UniJEPA (arXiv 2608.07409), Delta-JEPA (2606.31232), Med-D-JEPA (2608.28787), "JEPA Guided Diffusion" traffic forecasting (2609.21379), Qantara (2607.04978). Treat them as UNVERIFIED leads.

## Q4. Architecture: latent diffusion (encoder → denoiser → decoder) vs JEPA (context encoder → predictor → EMA target, no decoder); fixed vs learned target space and collapse

### Takeaway
Latent diffusion is literally encoder → latent predictor → decoder, with a *frozen* encoder that fixes the target space. JEPA is context encoder → predictor → *learned* target encoder (EMA), with no decoder. A fixed target space cannot collapse. A learned one can, so JEPAs need EMA / stop-gradient / variance regularizers / SIGReg. LeCun's position paper states the same classification: deterministic generative architectures cannot collapse; joint-embedding architectures can. JEPA's reason for skipping the decoder is to avoid predicting unpredictable detail. That is also exactly what makes it non-generative.

### Cited Findings
- **LDM runs diffusion in a pretrained autoencoder's latent space.** The encoder and decoder are frozen around a latent denoiser. Verified quote: "latent space of powerful pretrained autoencoders" — [Rombach et al. 2022 (LDM), arXiv 2112.10752, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.10752#page=1)
- **I-JEPA's recipe.** Predict target-block embeddings from a context block. Targets come from an EMA of the context encoder. Verified quotes: "from a single context block, predict the representations of various target blocks" — [I-JEPA, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08243#page=1); "exponential moving average of the context-encoder parameters" — [I-JEPA, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08243#page=4)
- **I-JEPA on collapse**: it is the core risk for joint-embedding methods, and it is not a concern for generative (reconstruction) architectures as long as the conditioning variable has low information. Verified quotes: "The main challenge with JEAs is representation collapse" — [I-JEPA, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08243#page=2); "Representation collapse is not a concern with these architectures as long as the informational capacity of z is low" — [I-JEPA, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08243#page=3)
- **V-JEPA on the trivial solution.** Naive latent regression admits a trivial solution (a constant encoder). Verified quote: "would admit a trivial solution, where the encoder outputs a constant" — [V-JEPA, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.08471#page=3)
- **LeCun 2022: JEPA is a non-generative world-model architecture**, by design. Verified quote: "a non-generative architecture for predictive world models" — [LeCun 2022, "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" (OpenReview PDF via Wayback), p.2](https://web.archive.org/web/20241231120746id_/https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf#page=2)
- **LeCun 2022's collapse taxonomy (Fig. 10)**: deterministic generative architectures cannot collapse; joint-embedding architectures can. Verified quotes: "Deterministic generative architecture: cannot collapse because it can only produce a single" — [p.21](https://web.archive.org/web/20241231120746id_/https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf#page=21); "can collapse when the information carried by sx and/or sy are insufficient" — [p.21](https://web.archive.org/web/20241231120746id_/https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf#page=21)
- **LeCun 2022: why JEPA predicts in representation space.** It avoids predicting every detail of y. Verified quote: "eschewing the need to predict every detail of y" — [p.24](https://web.archive.org/web/20241231120746id_/https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf#page=24). I-JEPA echoes this: "unnecessary pixel-level details are potentially eliminated" — [I-JEPA, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08243#page=2)
- **Latent world models on frozen features.** DINO-WM plans by predicting future frozen DINOv2 patch features. V-JEPA 2-AC post-trains an action-conditioned predictor on a frozen V-JEPA 2 encoder. Verified quotes: "by predicting future patch features" — [Zhou et al. 2024 (DINO-WM), arXiv 2411.04983, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.04983#page=1); "post-training a latent action-conditioned world model, V-JEPA 2-AC" — [V-JEPA 2, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09985#page=1)
- **DIAMOND argues the opposite**: a diffusion world model in pixel space, because compressed (discrete) latents drop details that matter for control. Verified quote: "compression into a compact discrete representation may ignore visual details" — [Alonso et al. 2024 (DIAMOND), arXiv 2405.12399, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.12399#page=1)
- **JEPA vs MAE bias.** In deep linear analysis, JEPA's latent-prediction objective is biased toward "high-influence" features (high regression coefficients) relative to MAE's reconstruction objective. Verified quote: "JEPAs are biased to learn high-influence features" — [Littwin et al. 2024, arXiv 2407.03475, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03475#page=1)

### Inferences
- **Side-by-side layout.**

  | | encoder | predictor input | predictor target | decoder | target space |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Latent diffusion | VAE enc `E` (frozen) | `z_t = α_t E(x) + σ_t ε`, `t` | `E(x)` (or ε / v) | VAE dec (frozen) | fixed → no collapse |
  | I-JEPA / V-JEPA | `f_θ` on visible patches | `f_θ(x_ctx)`, target positions | `f_θ̄(x_tgt)` (EMA) | none | learned → collapse risk |
  | JEPA world model (DINO-WM, V-JEPA 2-AC) | frozen `f` | `f(o_t)`, `a_t` | `f(o_{t+1})` | none (or vis-only) | fixed (frozen) → no collapse |
  | RAE / RCG | frozen SSL encoder | noisy SSL latent, `t` | clean SSL latent | trained decoder | fixed |

  The frozen-encoder JEPA world models (DINO-WM, V-JEPA 2-AC, RoboJEPA) are structurally the same as latent diffusion minus the noise axis. They regress the next frozen latent in one shot, where latent diffusion regresses the clean frozen latent from a noised one across all noise levels.
- **The real difference is the axis.** "Encoder → predictor in latent → decoder" fits both. What differs is what the predictor's input–target pair spans: (noisy, clean) at the same time step for diffusion, versus (now, later) or (visible, hidden) for JEPA.

### Gaps
- No source found that compares, at matched compute, a one-step L2 latent regressor and a latent diffusion predictor on the *same* frozen encoder for world modeling. JEDI and Flow-JEPA report gains for the stochastic version, but on their own setups.

## Q5. Prediction parameterizations: ε-, x0-, v-prediction and flow-matching velocity; is "predicting the denoising direction" the same as predicting a velocity, and how does it compare to predicting the next latent?

### Takeaway
Yes. Given `x_t` and `t`, ε-, x0-, v- and flow-velocity prediction are affine re-expressions of one conditional expectation, `E[x_0 | x_t]`. They differ only in loss weighting, and in which target is easy for a network in high dimensions. The flow-matching velocity is exactly `(denoiser output − current state) / (time remaining)`. So one Euler step from the current state all the way to the end returns `E[x_0 | x_t]`, which is precisely a JEPA-style one-shot point prediction. Many small steps, re-querying the mean as uncertainty shrinks, produce a sample instead.

### Cited Findings
- **DDPM's ε-prediction** came from the link to denoising score matching. Verified quote: "novel connection between diffusion probabilistic models and denoising score matching" — [Ho et al. 2020 (DDPM), arXiv 2006.11239, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11239#page=1)
- **v-prediction.** `v ≡ α_t ε − σ_t x`, with `x̂ = α_t z_t − σ_t v̂`. This interpolates between ε- and x-prediction and is the most stable choice for distillation. Verified quotes: "smoothly interpolating between predicting x directly and predicting via" — [Salimans & Ho 2022, arXiv 2202.00512, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.00512#page=5); "predicting v is the most stable option" — [p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.00512#page=7)
- **All diffusion objectives are one objective with different weightings**: a weighted integral of ELBOs over noise levels. Verified quote: "weighted integral of ELBOs over different noise levels" — [Kingma & Gao 2023, arXiv 2303.00848, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00848#page=1)
- **DDIM's update** is `x_{t−1} = √ᾱ_{t−1}·x̂_0 + √(1−ᾱ_{t−1})·ε̂` (+ noise). The paper labels these terms "predicted x0" and "direction pointing to x_t". So the step is assembled from the denoiser's mean estimate plus a direction. Verified quote: "direction pointing to" — [Song, Meng, Ermon 2020 (DDIM), arXiv 2010.02502, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02502#page=5)
- **Flow matching** regresses vector fields of fixed conditional paths. The marginal field is the posterior average of conditional fields (Eq. 8), and the conditional loss has the same gradients as the marginal one. Verified quotes: "regressing vector fields of fixed conditional probability paths" — [Lipman et al. 2022, arXiv 2210.02747, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.02747#page=1); "objectives have identical gradients" — [p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.02747#page=4)
- **Rectified flow** learns ODEs that follow straight paths between paired samples. Verified quote: "straight paths connecting the points drawn from" — [Liu et al. 2022, arXiv 2209.03003, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.03003#page=1)
- **Stochastic interpolants.** The velocity is a conditional expectation, `b(t,x) = E[ẋ_t | x_t = x]`, and is the unique minimizer of a quadratic objective. Verified quote: "This velocity is explicitly available only in special cases" — [Albergo, Boffi, Vanden-Eijnden 2023, arXiv 2303.08797, p.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.08797#page=13)
- **Diffusion and Gaussian flow matching are the same model family** under different output parameterizations (secondary commentary). Verified quote: "diffusion models and Gaussian flow matching are the same" — [Gao et al., "Diffusion Meets Flow Matching" blog](https://diffusionflow.github.io/#light-toggle)
- **x-prediction behaves differently in high dimensions (JiT, 2025).** Under the manifold hypothesis, clean data lies on a low-dimensional manifold and noise does not, so predicting the clean target can succeed where ε/v-prediction fails. Verified quotes: "predicting clean data and predicting noised quantities are fundamentally different"; "natural data should lie on a low-dimensional manifold, whereas noised quantities do not" — [Li & He 2025 (JiT), arXiv 2511.13720, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.13720#page=1)

### Inferences
- **All targets are affine maps of the denoiser.** Write `D(x_t, t) = E[x_0 | x_t]` and `s = ∇ log p_t`. Then
  - ε-target: `E[ε | x_t] = (x_t − α_t D) / σ_t = −σ_t s`
  - x0-target: `D = (x_t + σ_t² s) / α_t` (Tweedie)
  - v-target (α²+σ²=1): `E[v | x_t] = α_t E[ε|x_t] − σ_t D`
  - Flow matching, Lipman convention (noise at t=0, data at t=1, CondOT path, σ_min→0): `u_t(x) = (E[x_1 | x_t = x] − x) / (1 − t)`.

  So yes: "predicting the denoising direction" = predicting the velocity field = predicting the conditional mean. They are the same function under known coefficients. The numerical difference is the effective per-level weighting (Kingma & Gao) and whether the network output lies on a low-dimensional manifold (JiT).
- **One Euler step to the end is a JEPA-style prediction.** An Euler step of size `h` gives `x_{t+h} = (1 − h/(1−t))·x + (h/(1−t))·E[x_1|x_t]`, a convex move *toward* the denoiser's guess. With `h = 1 − t` (one step to the end) the output is exactly `E[x_1 | x_t]`, a JEPA-style one-shot conditional-mean prediction. So **a JEPA predictor is a single-step flow sampler from its corruption level**, and a diffusion sampler is a JEPA-like predictor queried repeatedly at decreasing corruption, each query conditioned on the previous partially resolved state.
- **"Next latent" vs "denoising step".** A JEPA world model's "next latent" `ŝ_{t+1} ≈ E[s_{t+1} | s_t, a_t]` corresponds to the diffusion denoiser's *mean* output `x̂_0`, not to its *step*. The diffusion step (the DDIM / probability-flow update) is a state transition along the noise-level axis, deterministic given `x_t`. That makes the sampler itself a "world model of the noise axis" whose dynamics are the probability-flow ODE. Diffusion Forcing makes this concrete by mixing the two axes (Q6).
- **JEPA is x-prediction.** It predicts the clean target embedding, never a noise-like quantity. JiT's argument then suggests that this, rather than ε, is the natural target when embedding dimension is high.

### Gaps
- No source found that measures the "single Euler step = JEPA prediction" equivalence empirically (e.g., comparing a one-step flow on a JEPA latent to the JEPA predictor). The equivalence is algebraic and is my derivation from Lipman's Eq. 8 with the CondOT path.

## Q6. Where the analogy breaks: point prediction at one corruption level vs a sampleable family over noise levels; multimodality and blur

### Takeaway
A JEPA predictor is a conditional mean (L2) or median (L1) at roughly one corruption level. With a multimodal future it outputs the average of the modes, which in pixel space is the familiar blur. JEPA addresses this only by (a) having encoders that drop unpredictable detail, or (b) introducing a latent variable z, which I-JEPA and V-JEPA do not. Diffusion learns the conditional mean at *every* noise level. Chaining those (or using the score family for Langevin / ODE sampling) turns means into samples. Recent hybrids fix JEPA's gap by swapping pointwise regression for conditional diffusion or flow matching in the latent.

### Cited Findings
- **MSE gives blurry predictions.** Video prediction with an MSE loss gives "inherently blurry predictions". Verified quote: "inherently blurry predictions" — [Mathieu, Couprie, LeCun 2015, arXiv 1511.05440, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05440#page=1)
- **LeCun 2022: multiplicity needs a latent variable**, and JEPA has two ways to represent the multiplicity of y. Verified quotes: "enabling the model to represent multiple predictions. This may require the use of a latent variable"; "Latent variables represent information about y that cannot be extracted from x" — [LeCun 2022, p.19](https://web.archive.org/web/20241231120746id_/https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf#page=19); "two ways a JEPA may represent the multiplicity of values of y" — [p.24](https://web.archive.org/web/20241231120746id_/https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf#page=24)
- **I-JEPA's z is deterministic**: mask and position tokens, not a latent variable. Verified quote: "The conditioning variable z then corresponds to a set of (possibly learnable) mask and position tokens" — [I-JEPA, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08243#page=3)
- **Sampling needs a denoiser across noise levels.** Kadkhodaie & Simoncelli sample by coarse-to-fine stochastic ascent with a *blind* denoiser valid over a range of noise levels. The residual at each level supplies the gradient of that level's blurred density. Verified quote: "residual of the denoising function, which is proportional to the gradient of" — [Kadkhodaie & Simoncelli 2021, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.13640#page=4)
- **One noise level is fine for representation**, as l-DAE shows (61.5%, about 3% below multi-level). The noise schedule is a generative necessity, not a representational one. Verified quote: "Using this single-level noise achieves decent accuracy of 61.5%" — [l-DAE, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.14404#page=7)
- **Diffusion Forcing interpolates between next-token prediction and full-sequence diffusion** via independent per-token noise levels. Past tokens clean and the next token fully noised recovers next-token (next-latent) prediction. Verified quotes: "independent per-token noise levels" — [Chen et al. 2024 (Diffusion Forcing), arXiv 2407.01392, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01392#page=1); "training a causal next-token prediction model to generate one or several future tokens without fully diffusing past ones" — [p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01392#page=1)
- **Hybrids that make the JEPA predictor stochastic**: JEDI ("multi-step stochastic JEPA predictor") — [arXiv 2605.13013, p.21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13013#page=21); F-JEPA ("switching from pointwise transition regression to stochastic trajectory-level prediction") — [arXiv 2608.29029, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29029#page=1); D-JEPA / MAR per-token diffusion heads — [D-JEPA p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.03755#page=1), [MAR p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11838#page=1)
- **Production JEPA world models plan by optimizing, not sampling** (energy / CEM search). RoboJEPA's diffusion decoder exists only to visualize. Verified quote: "The decoder serves purely as a visualization tool" — [RoboJEPA, p.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10515#page=13)

### Inferences
- **Sharp form of the break.** A diffusion model trains `D(·, t) ≈ E[x_0 | x_t]` for *all* `t ∈ (0, 1]`. Because the corruption is Gaussian and nested, those means determine the scores of every blurred density `p_t`, and hence (through the reverse SDE/ODE) the data distribution itself. A JEPA trains one conditional moment at one corruption type and level. A single first moment does not determine a distribution, and with no family of levels there is no path from a simple prior to the data. So the JEPA alone cannot be sampled, and this is a mathematical limit, not an engineering one.
- **Why JEPA's averaging is less harmful.** The JEPA encoder can learn to discard the unpredictable components (LeCun's first way), so averaging in that space is much less destructive than averaging pixels. That is why JEPA mean prediction works for *representation* and *planning* (choosing actions only needs a good conditional mean or energy), while it fails for *generation*.
- **The two axes are complementary.** World models step along physical time with a one-shot conditional-mean jump. Diffusion steps along noise level with many small conditional-mean-guided moves. Diffusion Forcing, JEDI and F-JEPA are the explicit products of the two axes.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled study isolating "mode-averaging in learned JEPA space" (e.g., a measure of blur or multimodal collapse of V-JEPA 2-AC predictions under stochastic dynamics). JEDI's stochastic-Atari test is the nearest, and I only verified that the test exists, not its numbers.

## Q7. Theory of what denoisers learn: generalization vs memorization, and what that implies for JEPA

### Takeaway
The *exact* empirical-optimal L2 denoiser has a closed form: a softmax-weighted average of training points. Sampling with it reproduces training examples (memorization). Real networks generalize because of architectural inductive biases, namely geometry-adaptive harmonic bases (Kadkhodaie et al.) and locality/equivariance (Kamb & Ganguli). The same logic applies to any L2 conditional-mean regressor, including JEPA predictors. The empirical optimum is a lookup over training targets, so anything useful comes from the inductive bias.

### Cited Findings
- **EDM gives the closed-form ideal denoiser for a finite dataset** (its Eq. 57): a Gaussian-kernel-weighted average of training images. Verified quote: "closed-form solution for the ideal denoiser" — [EDM, p.23](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364#page=23)
- **Learning the ideal score means memorizing.** A diffusion model that learns the ideal (empirical) score can only reproduce training examples, so any creativity has to come from deviations from it (locality, equivariance). Verified quote: "diffusion models that learn the ideal score function can only memorize" — [Kamb & Ganguli 2024, arXiv 2412.20292, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.20292#page=2)
- **Memorization gives way to generalization with enough data.** Denoisers trained on non-overlapping subsets converge to nearly the same score once data is large enough, and the inductive bias is a geometry-adaptive harmonic basis. Verified quotes: "two DNNs trained on non-overlapping subsets of a dataset learn nearly the same score function" — [Kadkhodaie, Guth, Simoncelli, Mallat 2023, arXiv 2310.02557, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02557#page=1); "geometry-adaptive harmonic" — [same, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02557#page=1)
- **Diffusion as coarse-to-fine autoregression over frequency** (secondary commentary). Each noise level resolves roughly the next band of spatial frequencies, so a diffusion sampler is "autoregression in the frequency domain", which parallels a world model's autoregression in time. Verified quote: "diffusion models of images perform approximate autoregression in the frequency domain" — [Dieleman 2024 blog](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html#main)
- **Diffusion models are denoising autoencoders** trained at many noise levels (secondary commentary). Verified quote: "I highlight a connection to another type of model: the venerable autoencoder" — [Dieleman 2022 blog](https://sander.ai/2022/01/31/diffusion.html#main)
- **JEPA's anti-collapse term carries density information** (JEPA-SCORE via the encoder Jacobian). So a JEPA's learned geometry encodes `p_data`, much as a denoiser family encodes it through scores. Verified quote: "provably estimates the data density" — [arXiv 2510.05949, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05949#page=1)

### Inferences
- **JEPA predictors have the same memorizing optimum.** The empirical-risk minimizer of the L2 JEPA loss (for fixed encoders) is also a kernel-like average of training target embeddings, conditioned on the context. So JEPA and diffusion share both the "conditional-mean regressor" form and the "generalization comes from inductive bias, not from the objective" property. That property is shared because both are conditional-mean regressions, so for theory, results from the denoiser side (GAHB, locality) are the natural lens on what JEPA predictors learn.
- **Density lives in different places.** In diffusion the density is encoded in the *predictor* (scores at all levels). In a JEPA it is encoded in the *encoder* (via the anti-collapse/Gaussianity constraint and the Jacobian, per the Gaussian Embeddings paper), not in the predictor.

### Gaps
- No primary source found that applies Kadkhodaie-style generalization/memorization analysis directly to JEPA predictors.
- The "two ways density is stored" contrast is my synthesis. No source states it.

## Q8. The cleanest formal statement of the unification

### Takeaway
A single template covers diffusion, flow matching, masked diffusion, MAE, BERT, I-JEPA, V-JEPA and latent world models. Each regresses a target map `T(y)` from a corrupted view `ỹ ~ q_c(· | y)`, so its optimum is `E[T(y) | ỹ, c]` (or the median under L1). The methods differ only in the corruption family `q_c`, the set of corruption levels `c` trained on, the target map `T` (fixed vs learned), and the loss (L2 / L1 / cross-entropy). Generation becomes possible exactly when the levels span a path from a tractable prior to the data and the output is distributional or Tweedie-linked to a score. Read this way, the hypothesis is right: a JEPA predictor is one slice of a denoiser, in a learned space, with the rest of the slices missing.

### Cited Findings
- **Gaussian, all levels, fixed T (diffusion).** `E[x_0|x_t]` ↔ score for every level (EDM Eqs. 2–3) — [EDM, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364#page=3); all weightings equivalent up to `w(λ)` — [Kingma & Gao, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00848#page=1)
- **Interpolant velocity as a conditional expectation**: `b = E[ẋ_t | x_t]` — [Albergo et al., p.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.08797#page=13); marginal field = posterior average of conditional fields — [Lipman et al., p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.02747#page=4)
- **Masking, all levels, one-hot T (masked diffusion)**: ELBO = weighted integral of masked cross-entropies — [MD4, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.04329#page=1); score = clean conditional × analytic scalar — [RADD, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.03736#page=1)
- **Masking, one level, continuous fixed T (MAE)** = a single-step patch-conditioned diffusion — [DiffMAE, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03283#page=5)
- **Masking, ~one level, learned T (I-JEPA L2; V-JEPA L1 → median)** — [I-JEPA, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08243#page=1); [V-JEPA, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.08471#page=3); collapse possible because T is learned — [LeCun 2022, p.21](https://web.archive.org/web/20241231120746id_/https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf#page=21); diffusion with a learned T collapses too — [REPA-E, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10483#page=1)
- **Time shift + action, frozen T (latent world model)** — [DINO-WM, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.04983#page=1); [V-JEPA 2-AC, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09985#page=1)
- **Per-token levels mixing both axes (Diffusion Forcing)** — [p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01392#page=1); conditional latent diffusion = multi-step stochastic JEPA predictor (JEDI) — [p.21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13013#page=21)

### Inferences
- **The template.**
  ```
  min_g  E_{c ~ π(c)} E_{y ~ p_data} E_{ỹ ~ q_c(·|y)}  ℓ( g(ỹ, c), T(y) )
  ⇒ g*(ỹ, c) = E[ T(y) | ỹ, c ]        (ℓ = squared error)
             = median[ T(y) | ỹ, c ]   (ℓ = absolute error, coordinate-wise)
             = p( T(y) | ỹ, c )        (ℓ = cross-entropy, T one-hot)
  ```

  | method | q_c | π(c) | T | ℓ | g* gives | sampleable alone? |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Gaussian diffusion / flow matching | `α_c y + σ_c ε` | all of (0,1] | identity or frozen VAE | L2 (weighted) | mean ⇔ score ⇔ velocity at every level | yes (ODE/SDE) |
  | Masked diffusion (MDLM, MD4, MaskGIT) | absorbing mask, ratio c | all of (0,1] | one-hot tokens | CE | full clean conditional (RADD) | yes |
  | MAE | mask at c=0.75 | point mass | pixels | L2 | mean at one level | no (one step) |
  | BERT | mask at c=0.15 | point mass | one-hot | CE | conditional at one level | no (one level) |
  | I-JEPA | mask (multi-block) | ≈ point mass | EMA encoder (learned) | L2 | mean in learned space | no; needs anti-collapse |
  | V-JEPA / V-JEPA 2 | spatio-temporal mask | ≈ point mass | EMA encoder (learned) | L1 | median in learned space | no; needs anti-collapse |
  | JEPA world model (DINO-WM, V-JEPA 2-AC) | "observe past, hide future" + action | one horizon | frozen encoder | L2/L1 | one-step mean/median of next latent | no (planning by search) |
  | Diffusion Forcing / JEDI / F-JEPA | per-token noise on future latents | all levels × time | frozen or learned encoder | L2 (diffusion / CFM) | mean at every level, per time step | yes |

- **Answer to the hypothesis.** "A diffusion model is an encoder-predictor-decoder that predicts a denoising step on a latent" is right about the architecture (LDM / RAE) and right that the core operation is a conditional-expectation predictor, the same one JEPA trains. It is imprecise on two points:
  1. The network predicts a conditional mean (equivalently score or velocity), not a step. The step is the sampler's convex move toward that mean (Q5).
  2. Generation depends on the *family over corruption levels*, which JEPA does not have.
- **The most compact one-liner.** "A JEPA world model is the one-step, one-corruption-level, learned-target-space special case of a conditional denoiser. Diffusion is the all-levels, fixed-target-space case, and only the latter integrates into a sampler." Diffusion Forcing and the 2026 hybrids (JEDI, F-JEPA) are the explicit interpolations between the two.

### Gaps
