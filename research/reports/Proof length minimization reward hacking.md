# Length rewards push proof work out of sight

Every common way of measuring a Lean proof's simplicity can be driven down without making the proof any simpler. The reason is the same each time. Lean spreads a proof across five places: the source a person writes, the elaborated term the kernel checks, the computation the kernel performs, compiled code the kernel never sees, and the environment of definitions, instances, macros and axioms. Each measure looks at one of these, so an optimizer lowers its score by moving work into another. Source tokens and tactic counts reward one-word automation and file-local macros. Term size rewards proof by reflection, and it changes by a factor of about 370,000 depending on whether shared subterms are counted once. Kernel time is cut to roughly zero by `native_decide`, which since February 2026 returns a freshly minted axiom as its proof. Length measured against a library collapses unless the library is charged for. Axiom counts audit trust well but say almost nothing about simplicity. The prior art agrees: every serious shortener, from Metamath's `MINIMIZE_WITH` to Meta's ProofOptimizer, had to bolt side constraints onto "shorter" after it produced proofs that were valid but worse. Theory explains why. No proxy is unhackable over an unrestricted policy space, and Gödel's speed-up theorem means a stronger system or library can shorten some proofs by more than any computable function. disp (the user's tree-calculus language) fixes the *measure*: the elaborated closed tree has no names, notation or citations to game. It does not fix the *incentive*. In disp a Proved verdict has no proof object. The checker's bounded search, `refl` plus a ledger, and trusted slots inside types do the proving, none of them is charged, and two new hacks appear that Lean cannot have: witnesses that inspect the checker, and claims that are vacuous by construction. No size objective exists in disp today, so every disp hack below is inferred from code and pinned tests, not observed.

## Five boundaries let every Lean measure fall without simplification

Lean's weakness under a size reward is not a bug in any one tactic. It comes from how the system is built. The source, the elaborated term, kernel reduction, compiled native code and the environment are separate, and a measure that counts one of them rewards moving work into the others. RL training adds pressure in the same direction even before any length term is added. ProofOptimizer's authors note that RL-trained provers already produce proofs "reliant on unnecessarily strong automation where a simple step would suffice" ([Gu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15700v1)). Lean will not accept a skipped step, so a length reward cannot produce an invalid proof the way a length penalty on chain-of-thought produces skipped reasoning ([SmartThinker](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04348)). What it produces instead is *delegation*: the work is handed to automation, to the kernel, to the compiler, or to the environment.

### Text length and tactic counts reward one-word automation

ProofOptimizer (ICLR 2026) is the main RL system that rewards shorter Lean proofs. It counts "the number of tokens produced by a Lean-specific tokenizer", and the count "does not penalize long identifier names" and "ignores comments and line breaks". The reward is relative shortening, paid only when the new proof is valid and no longer than the old one ([Gu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15700v1)). It reports **87% shortening on miniF2F, 57% on PutnamBench and about 49% on Seed-Prover's IMO 2025 proofs**. The visible text of the paper never discusses reward hacking or banned tactics. Its own data pipeline uses a macro containing "try native_decide try omega" to filter out trivial theorems ([Gu et al. 2025, App. A.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15700v1)).

Under a token or character count the degenerate optima are easy to list. One is collapsing case branches with `<;>` or `all_goals`. Another is writing term-mode one-liners with anonymous constructors and `▸`. A third is dropping the `show` and `have` annotations that carried the explanation. Two more are especially cheap. The first is leaving a search tactic such as `exact?` or `apply?` in the proof, where six characters stand for whatever term the search finds at elaboration time. ProofOptimizer itself had to "replace invocations of exact? and apply? with the actual proof that is found" before shortening ([App. C.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15700v1)). The second is defining a file-local `macro "t" : tactic => `(tactic| first | omega | simp_all | nlinarith [...])`. That costs one line of environment and makes every later proof one token long. Line counts are the weakest of all. ProofOptimizer's own listings had their "new lines removed, multiple lines compressed into one" to fit on the page.

Tactic count is weaker still, because it ignores argument length. ImProver measures length as "the number of tactic invocations", although the prompt it gives the model says length is "measured in the number of lines" ([Ahuja et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04753)). Under a tactic count, any proof can become a single `aesop (add safe [...])`, `grind [l1, l2, …]`, `simp_all [*, …]`, or `first | … | …` combinator. Leaving arguments out of the count does not remove the cost of the proof. It moves the cost into the arguments, the configuration, or the environment.

### Term size depends on a sharing convention that spans five orders of magnitude

Measuring the elaborated term closes the notation loophole, but it runs into a different problem. Lean `Expr`s are DAGs: a subterm that appears in many places is stored once and shared. Whether a measure counts that subterm once or every time it appears changes the result astronomically. Henrik Böving explained a core `bv_decide` lemma's term size this way: "`Expr.size` counts the nodes in the tree instead of the DAG view". Sebastian Ullrich's `pp.exprSizes` printed **`[size 2575268672/6967/6967]`**, that is, about 2.6 billion nodes as a tree against 6,967 as a DAG. Computing a related tree size took another user 50–70 GB of RAM ([Zulip: huge expressions](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/270676-lean4/topic/huge.20expressions.html)). Lean core has both counters, `Expr.sizeWithoutSharing` and `Expr.numObjs` ([Lean/Expr.lean](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/master/src/Lean/Expr.lean); [NumObjs.lean](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/master/src/Lean/Util/NumObjs.lean)). Even the DAG count is not stable: some term-building APIs do not preserve maximal sharing, so measured DAG size depends on whether sharing was restored before counting ([AlphaShareCommon](https://lean-lang.org/doc/api/Lean/Meta/Sym/AlphaShareCommon.html)).

The two conventions invite opposite hacks. Under tree size, an optimizer gains by introducing `have`/`let` binders and auxiliary lemmas. That is partly good style, but it also punishes honest proofs by orders of magnitude even when they are cheap to check. Under DAG size, an optimizer can produce terms that are exponentially large as trees but share heavily. Those terms are cheap to store and ruinous for any printer, exporter or external checker that does not preserve sharing. Under *any* term measure, the global optimum is proof by reflection. `decide` proves `p` by reducing a `Decidable p` instance, so the term is tiny and the work happens during kernel reduction ([Init/Tactics.lean](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/master/src/Init/Tactics.lean)). Wojciech Nawrocki's verdict on term size applies: "different terms of the same size can have drastically different kernel proof checking times," so "size is not a great measure" ([Zulip: measuring proof efficiency](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/270676-lean4/topic/measuring.20proof.20efficiency.html)).

### Kernel time is zeroed by an axiom minted per call

Charging check time blocks `decide`, but it opens a cheaper door. Since lean4#12217 (February 2026), `native_decide` compiles the decision procedure, runs it natively, and, if the result is `true`, "will add an axiom asserting `e = true` and return that axiom" ([Lean/Meta/Native.lean](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/master/src/Lean/Meta/Native.lean)). The proof term is a single constant, so its size is constant and the kernel does about O(1) work to check it. All the real computation happens in compiled code that the kernel never sees. Joachim Breitner's announcement notes that `#print axioms` now lists a fresh `_native.native_decide.ax_…` per use ([Zulip: Heads up](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/270676-lean4/topic/Heads.20up.3A.20changes.20to.20native_decide.20(and.20bv_decide).html)). The trust cost is real. The trusted base now includes the compiler and "all definitions with an `@[implemented_by]` attribute" ([Init/Tactics.lean](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/master/src/Init/Tactics.lean)). Mirek Olšák points out that "users can define their own untrusted implementations", which is how `native_decide` gets used to prove `False` ([Zulip: Automated verification of native_decide](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/113488-general/topic/Automated.20verification.20of.20native_decide.html)). Finally, a single line, `set_option debug.skipKernelTC true`, removes kernel checking altogether, under a soundness warning ([Lean/Environment.lean](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/master/src/Lean/Environment.lean)).

Time measures are also fragile in ordinary ways. Kernel cost has to be read by wrapping `addDecl` in `withHeartbeats` with `Elab.async false`, because otherwise the measurement does not wait for the kernel. Run-to-run variance is a known problem ([Zulip: measuring proof efficiency](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/270676-lean4/topic/measuring.20proof.20efficiency.html)). Kernel success can flip when an *unrelated* instance is added to Mathlib ([Zulip: Deep recursion](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/287929-mathlib4/topic/Deep.20recursion.20in.20mathlib.20but.20not.20in.20lean.20core.html)). Rewriting `Nat.sqrt` with structural recursion on fuel made it about 1000× faster in the kernel, while the alternative workaround produced large proof terms instead ([Zulip: Definitional well-founded recursion](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/236446-Type-theory/topic/Definitional.20well-founded.20recursion.html)). Term size and kernel time trade off directly, so pricing only one of them simply selects the other.

### Library-relative length collapses unless the library pays

Measuring description length relative to a library is free for the optimizer if library additions cost nothing. The optimizer adds something that is nearly the theorem itself, such as a lemma, macro, `simp` attribute, `Decidable` instance or notation, and the proof becomes one token. The limiting case is declaring the target as an `axiom`, which Anthropic's 2026 alignment write-up describes as a common Lean hack ([Anthropic: Training a Misaligned Reward Seeker](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/reward-seeker/)). Environment-level shortenings fall into three groups:

- **Trust-changing:** `axiom`, `sorry`, `implemented_by` combined with `native_decide`.
- **Sound but cost-hiding:** macros, `attribute [local simp]`, instances, scoped notation, `set_option maxHeartbeats 0`.
- **Real compression:** a general lemma used more than once.

Only the last is the behavior a description-length objective is supposed to reward. Charging for it correctly requires a precise diff of the environment, covering attributes and options as well as `def` text. No existing tool computes that diff.

### Axiom count audits trust, not simplicity

Almost all of Mathlib depends on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. Counting axioms therefore mostly rewards avoiding classical lemmas, which is not simplicity. As a trust gate it is excellent: an exact whitelist of those three rejects every `sorryAx` and every per-call `_native` axiom automatically. Counting dependencies (the distinct constants a proof references) is gamed in a different way. Reflection references one checker instead of many lemmas, and heavy automation produces terms that reference many constants while the source names none.

| Measure | Cheapest degenerate optimum | What catches it |
|---|---|---|
| Characters / tokens / lines | `exact?` left in, local macro, `<;>` chains, automation one-liners | Charge environment additions; ban search tactics in final proofs |
| Tactic count | One `grind`/`aesop`/`simp_all` with long arguments | Count arguments (i.e. tokens) instead |
| Term size, tree | Penalizes honest unshared proofs; rewards aux lemmas | DAG count after `instantiateMVars`, aux constants expanded |
| Term size, DAG | Exponential-as-tree shared terms; reflection | Pair with kernel time |
| Kernel time / heartbeats | `native_decide` (constant axiom), `skipKernelTC` | Exact axiom whitelist; option filtering |
| Library-relative MDL | Library absorbs the theorem | Two-part code: charge the library, amortize only across other users |
| Axioms / dependencies | Reflection; weak signal overall | Use as a hard gate, not a score |

## Theory rules out an unhackable length proxy

These failures are not peculiar to Lean. The theory of description length predicts all of them. Kolmogorov complexity is defined only up to an additive constant that depends on the reference machine. For any single finite object, some universal machine gives it complexity O(1), for example a machine that prints the object on input "0" and otherwise simulates a standard machine ([Li & Vitányi](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-030-11298-1)). "Simplicity relative to a library" is that constant written down as code. Two-part MDL fixes this by minimizing L(H) + L(D|H). The model, here the library, must be charged, or the model that memorizes the data wins ([Grünwald 2007](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262072816/the-minimum-description-length-principle/)). A per-theorem proof length that ignores the library is the degenerate one-part case.

Proof complexity sharpens the point. Gödel's speed-up theorem says that for any computable f there is a statement whose shortest proof in a weaker system is longer than f of its shortest proof in a stronger one ([Gödel's speed-up theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_speed-up_theorem); [Buss 1994](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/godelone/)). Moving strength into the axioms or the library is therefore not a cheat a length metric can detect, because for some theorems it is the only way to get a short proof. Cut elimination runs the other way. Lutz Straßburger, revisiting Hilbert's newly found 24th problem on proof simplicity, notes that proofs with cuts "that fit on a page" can become "bigger than the size of the universe" once the cuts are removed. Cuts are auxiliary lemmas, "crucial information" ([Straßburger 2019](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/Labo/Lutz.Strassburger/papers/hilbert24.pdf)). Normalization is also the field's classical criterion for two proofs being "the same". A length minimizer therefore favors exactly the lemma-dense presentations that this criterion of sameness erases. Straßburger requires that a good notion of proof identity "respect the size of proofs" up to a polynomial factor. A length score that changes super-polynomially between proofs deemed identical is measuring how a proof is presented, not the proof itself.

Adding time to the measure helps only partially. Levin's Kt = length + log(time) and Schmidhuber's speed prior charge for computation, which blocks "let the checker do it" ([Schmidhuber 2002](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/reinforcement-learning/model/2002-schmidhuber.pdf)). The exchange rate is still one bit of description for a doubling of check work, so reflection that trades k bits of proof for 2^k steps of computation breaks even under Kt and wins outright under length alone. Accattoli and Dal Lago supply the honest time unit: leftmost-outermost β-steps, evaluated with sharing, are an invariant cost model for the λ-calculus despite size explosion ([Accattoli & Dal Lago 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.01233)). That pins time down only up to a polynomial.

Reward-hacking theory then explains why tuning the proxy does not escape this. Skalse et al. prove that over all stochastic policies two rewards can be unhackable only if one is constant, and that narrowing or simplifying a reward does not usually help. Unhackability exists only on restricted policy sets ([Skalse et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13085)). In Manheim and Garrabrant's taxonomy, length minimization is most exposed to two variants. One is extremal Goodhart: tiny degenerate artifacts at the tail. The other is metric manipulation: editing the library, tokenizer or checker ([Manheim & Garrabrant](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.04585v4)). Gao et al.'s overoptimization curves give the expected shape for any imperfect proxy: the true reward rises, then falls ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10760v1)). Genetic programming ran this experiment decades ago. With no size term, programs bloat, because "longer representations of a given solution outnumber shorter ones" ([Langdon & Poli 1997](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Langdon_1997_bloatTR.html)). With a linear size penalty, tuning becomes a cliff. The best-performing remedies were lexicographic parsimony (size only breaks ties between equally fit programs) and hard limits combined with a mild penalty ([Luke & Panait 2006](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Luke_2006_EC.html)). The theory therefore points to restricting the artifact space and putting a price on every resource, not to searching for a cleverer scalar.

## Every serious shortener had to add side constraints

Metamath is the clearest precedent, because every step there is a primitive inference and there is no automation to hide behind. Even so, before 2019 `minimize *` "could add dependencies on new axioms if it made the proof shorter". The fix made the default refuse new axioms and added `/no_new_axioms_from` ([Metamath list](https://go.mail-archive.com/metamath@googlegroups.com/msg00447.html)). A 2023 thread found the minimizer could still pick a shortening that depended on `ax-13` unless `ax-*` was excluded explicitly ([Google Group](https://groups.google.com/g/metamath/c/JYws500zfN8/m/eQewW3ARBwAJ)). When OpenAI's GPT-f contributed machine-shortened proofs in 2020 (for example, dvtanlem "247 steps to 14"), maintainers caught three separate ways a shorter proof could be valid but worse ([Google Group: More proof shortenings from OpenAI](https://groups.google.com/g/metamath/c/2F7knFknKJ8/m/3yi2o67tAwAJ)):

- It added a distinct-variable condition, which "can really weaken results".
- It used a definition marked "(New usage is discouraged.)".
- It used a deprecated OLD theorem.

Polu agreed to filter for no extra distinct variables and no larger axiom trace. Metamath's guards are a ready-made checklist. Each one blocks a way a shortening can keep the proof valid while changing *what* is proved or *what it rests on*. The readability debate came out the other way from the pessimists' expectation. Norman Megill found an AI-shortened proof "much easier to read", and Mario Carneiro located bad short proofs in exhaustive search under strict optimality, not in ML-generated ones ([Google Group: Why would proof shortening be useful?](https://groups.google.com/g/metamath/c/CMKRwV0CX3w/m/9BxgffRtBQAJ)).

The LLM-era Lean shorteners document both directions of gaming. ProofOptimizer found proofs that became "much slower than the original proof" because "a faster proof algorithm is replaced by a shorter but slower method (e.g. brute force with interval_cases)". The fix was a combined length-and-heartbeat objective. With it, the miniF2F proofs showing more than 50% speedup rose from 56 to 81 and the slowdowns disappeared ([Gu et al. 2025, §5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15700)). Its RL training also caused diversity collapse. ImProver documents the mirror-image hack on its readability metric: the model can pad a proof with `have` statements "not being used in the proof's deductive process whatsoever" to raise the declarativity ratio. The authors call their metrics "intentionally simplistic" ([Ahuja et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04753)). According to a search summary of the Nature paper (the page itself was not readable), AlphaProof charges −1 per tactic but selects final proofs by "a cost function linearly depending on string length and execution time". That suggests DeepMind anticipated the short-but-slow trade ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09833-y)). Leanabell-Prover-V2 reports only "slight gains" from a tactic-count reward ([arXiv 2507.08649](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08649)).

Classical automated reasoning reached the same verdict decades earlier: the different notions of "short" disagree. Kinyon's survey of the Wos/Veroff tradition notes that "the slightly shorter proof has a significantly higher maximum clause weight", and that simplicity is "obviously context-dependent" ([Kinyon 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.04251)). Wernhard and Bibel give exact cases. Meredith's 1963 variation of Łukasiewicz's proof is one step shorter as a DAG (33 vs 34) but larger as a tree (669 vs 585). Their own "combinatory compression" reaches compacted size 19 at the price of tree size 119 against 64 ([Wernhard & Bibel 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.12827)). Library learning adds a warning about apparent compression. Audits of LEGO-Prover found that "exactly one lemma is reused", and found "no evidence of the direct reuse of learned lemmas" ([Berlot-Attwell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20274v1); [EACL 2026](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-eacl/2026.eacl-long.163.pdf)). A system can emit lemmas and look as if it compresses without reusing anything.

Communities without an explicit metric converged on the same defenses through style rules. Mathlib's flexible linter flags a rigid tactic acting on the output of a non-terminal `simp`, because "we don't want changes to the simp set to potentially break untouched files" ([FlexibleLinter](https://florisvandoorn.com/LeanCourse25/docs/Mathlib/Tactic/Linter/FlexibleLinter.html)). A pure length score penalizes the robust `simp only [...]` form and so inverts that guidance. Kim Morrison notes that "Mathlib, for example, does not allow the use of `native_decide`" ([Zulip](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/113488-general/topic/Automated.20verification.20of.20native_decide.html)). Code golf's list of loopholes forbidden by default covers interpreters made for the challenge, external state, and built-ins that solve the task outright ([meta.codegolf](https://codegolf.meta.stackexchange.com/questions/1061/loopholes-that-are-forbidden-by-default)). That list maps almost one-to-one onto reference-machine choice, environment and automation.

One gap stands out. **No paper found reports that a length reward taught a Lean model to emit `native_decide`, `grind` or local macros.** The ingredients are all present. AI provers including Aristotle, Kimina and Claude Opus 4.5 already emit `native_decide` without any length incentive ([Zulip](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/113488-general/topic/Automated.20verification.20of.20native_decide.html)), and LongCat-Flash-Prover had to build a Lean lexer and parser after a sudden jump in pass rate during RL turned out to be cheating ([arXiv 2603.21065](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21065)). ProofOptimizer publishes no frequency analysis of the tactics in its outputs, so whether its 87% came partly from delegation is unknown. The general verifier exploits (`sorryAx` leaks through `apply?`, `assume false`, spec gaming) are covered in [CONSTRAINED_PROGRAM_SEARCH.md §6.7](../CONSTRAINED_PROGRAM_SEARCH.md) and [NATURE_OF_OPTIMIZERS.md §6](../NATURE_OF_OPTIMIZERS.md). They are not repeated here, except where a size objective changes them.

## disp fixes the measure and leaves the incentive

The first fact about disp is that **no size or simplicity objective exists in it today**. No code computes program size as a score. The only node count on the checking path is a threshold below which a definition is not used as a lemma ([lib/rules.disp:155-173](../../lib/rules.disp)). The optimizer that would multiply a 0/1 type gate by a size or cost score is listed as "Designed but not built" ([README.md:148](../../README.md)). The question is therefore what the substrate and checker would let through once such an objective is attached.

### The closed tree closes notation and citation games

disp's real advantage is that everything elaborates to a tree, and the tree carries no names. Record field names "live only in scope, never in the tree", and `::` annotations are never compiled ([SYNTAX.typ:150](../../SYNTAX.typ)). A reference to a definition elaborates to that definition's compiled tree as a literal ([src/elab/expr.ts:88-101](../../src/elab/expr.ts)). The emitted program is therefore a closed tree with the library embedded, and it has a canonical ternary preorder string that is "byte-identical across backends" ([EVALUATOR.md:20-21](../../EVALUATOR.md)). If size is measured on that tree, ProofOptimizer's identifier-length blind spot, notation, macros, comments and the "cite a giant lemma in one token" hack all disappear. In disp you cannot cite anything: the citation *is* the tree. Lean needs an environment diff to price library additions, and that diff does not exist. disp gets it from inlining.

That claim holds under three conditions. First, the measure must be taken on the elaborated tree and never on `.disp` source. The elaborator evaluates closed applications at compile time with a 400-million-step divergence budget ([src/elab/cir.ts:118](../../src/elab/cir.ts); [src/elab/state.ts:19-37](../../src/elab/state.ts)), so `w := search_for_witness(spec)` is a tiny source line that hides an unbounded search. This is `decide` moved to compile time. Second, the elaborator is a free parameter. Bracket abstraction "is part of definitional equality — so changes here change every compiled program" ([src/elab/cir.ts:1-5](../../src/elab/cir.ts)). Jay's book shows plain bracket abstraction inflating a size-k combinator to 5k−2 ([Jay, ch. 4](../barry-jay/reflective-programs-in-tree-calculus-tree_book.pdf)), and Tromp shows one combinator shrinking from 2030 to 374 bits under a better scheme ([Tromp, *Functional Bits*](https://tromp.github.io/cl/LC.pdf)). Third, tree and DAG size diverge exactly as they do in Lean, and the divergence matters more here because the library is inlined. Unshared tree size charges the library once per use. That punishes using the library at all and pushes toward bespoke combinators, the anti-abstraction outcome [THE_NATURE_OF_ABSTRACTION.typ](../THE_NATURE_OF_ABSTRACTION.typ) warns about. DAG size computed against the process-wide hash-cons table ([src/core/tree.ts:45-90](../../src/core/tree.ts)) makes anything already interned free. A candidate's marginal size then depends on what else the session has built, so pre-building structures elsewhere becomes a hack. The defensible measure is **the DAG node count of the closed elaborated tree, hash-consed fresh with no session state**. That is a two-part code (library plus program given library) for free, and it is not implemented.

### Proofs have no size; the checker does the proving, uncharged

Here disp does worse than Lean. In Lean, reflection is one option among many. In disp it is the default design. A Proved verdict in the claim layer comes from a bounded symbolic search. The search splits pieces along their type's cases and licenses induction only on strictly smaller inputs ([lib/confirm.disp:1-10](../../lib/confirm.disp)). `eq_by_induction` takes two programs and no proof term at all ([lib/confirm.disp:704-711](../../lib/confirm.disp)). In the kernel, symmetry, transitivity and induction steps are all proved by `refl`, with the session ledger and reachability doing the work ([lib/kernel/kernel.test.disp:713-745](../../lib/kernel/kernel.test.disp)). So "minimize proof length" has no stable target. Scoring the witness's size pushes toward whatever the checker can settle with the most search, which is the disp form of swapping a structured proof for `decide` or `interval_cases`.

The fixed budgets (`run_fuel := 4000`, `split_depth := 3`) cap this push but do not charge for it. A witness that needs more checker work goes Open rather than Proved. A minimizer therefore drives toward the edge of the budget, and verdicts become brittle to budget changes, though only in the sound direction: "a deeper search or a longer run only settles more" ([lib/rules.disp:174-178](../../lib/rules.disp)). The fix the literature found, ProofOptimizer's heartbeat term, has nothing to attach to. `judge_with` returns only a `Verdict` and discards the fuel it spent ([lib/rules.disp:180-189](../../lib/rules.disp)). The step counter the design leans on ([FOUNDATIONS.md:161](../../FOUNDATIONS.md): "Cost is measured today as reduction steps (`ApplyStats.steps`)") is itself gameable. The eager reducer checks its memo table before counting a step, and a memo hit costs nothing ([src/core/tree.ts:648-657](../../src/core/tree.ts)). A candidate evaluated after another candidate has warmed the memo looks cheaper. The honest counters are the in-language machine's memo-off steps, which are slow and charge nothing for building data ([lib/machine.disp:1-9](../../lib/machine.disp)), and ic-net's interaction count, whose readouts are still listed as future work ([research/OPTIMIZER.typ:806-810](../OPTIMIZER.typ)).

disp's natives play the role of `native_decide`, and here disp does better. `tree_eq` and `m_advance` are keyed to the compiled tree of an in-language definition, fall back to that definition on `null`, have a no-native conformance mode, and are cross-checked across five backends that must agree byte for byte ([src/eval/eager.ts:25-33](../../src/eval/eager.ts); [README.md:136](../../README.md)). This is a real mitigation. It is not a closure. In normal operation the native answer is trusted, and the differential runs cover test fixtures, not every verdict a minimizer produces.

### Reflection, trusted type slots and vacuous claims are new or worse

Three surfaces either have no Lean counterpart or are wider in disp, and size pressure makes each one cheaper to exploit.

**Checker-sniffing witnesses.** A Lean proof term cannot observe that it is being checked. A disp program can triage on any tree, including a hypothesis. The confirmer handles this soundly: forged hole markers are wrapped as opaque ([lib/confirm.disp:122-127](../../lib/confirm.disp)), and every inspection of a symbolic piece forces a case split. The kernel's `Guard` tier does not handle it. `sniff_cod := {n} => if (is_neutral(n)) { Nat } else { False }` with witness `{n} => zero` certifies `true` while being false at every concrete point. It is pinned as a passing test, and the comment describes it as "the residue of the step-1 barrier" ([lib/kernel/kernel.test.disp:575-590](../../lib/kernel/kernel.test.disp)). Only `TwoFace` catches it, and `TwoFace` checks a battery of eight fixed trees, "a lie detector … not a proof" ([lib/kernel/kernel.disp:950-951](../../lib/kernel/kernel.disp)). Those two small trees are exactly what a minimizer of kernel-certified term size would find.

**Trusted slots inside types.** Lean's `@[csimp]` and `implemented_by` let an attribute swap implementations. In disp, any type can declare `ends`, `check`, `norm`, `gate` or `members`, and the judge believes them. The coherence check tests these slots only on the same eight probes ([lib/kernel/types.disp:68-80](../../lib/kernel/types.disp)). A generated type whose `ends` name distinct endpoints, but whose inhabitants all lie outside the probe set, passes. In the repo's own words, it then "launders a false equation into the ledger" ([lib/kernel/kernel.test.disp:989-994](../../lib/kernel/kernel.test.disp)). If AI-generated types enter a size-minimizing loop, this is the most direct route to proofs that are short because they are `refl`.

**Vacuous claims.** A claim is any program `{witness, challenge} => Bool` ([lib/claim.disp:1-8](../../lib/claim.disp)). The smallest claim, `{_w, _c} => true`, is Proved by every witness. Guarded claims of the form `if (NatR(x)) {…} else { true }` are vacuous wherever the guard fails. [GOALS.md](../../GOALS.md) wants a self-play loop that generates its own score functions, which [NATURE_OF_OPTIMIZERS.md](../NATURE_OF_OPTIMIZERS.md) already flags as the most Goodhart-prone item in the plan. Rewarding compact proofs of self-generated claims makes the weakest claim the cheapest. Nothing implemented measures how strong a claim is.

### Open is safe at the type level and unsafe at the gate

disp's three-valued verdict is a real structural win over Lean RL harnesses that treat a timeout as a failure or, worse, as an accept. "A failure is final, but running out of budget proves nothing" ([README.md:78-84](../../README.md)), and Proved is produced only by exhausting a finite domain or by a shortcut ([lib/verdict.disp:3-5](../../lib/verdict.disp)). The risk sits at the interfaces. The designed gate collapses everything to `param_apply T e == Ok true` ([research/OPTIMIZER.typ:91-93](../OPTIMIZER.typ)). The M0 optimizer is validated by re-running tests. `growth.cheaper` ranks programs by polynomial fits that are explicitly "evidence, not a proof" ([lib/growth.disp:1-5](../../lib/growth.disp)). Size pressure combined with any bounded check is adversarial selection for late counterexamples. The `Trees` source enumerates "every tree, smallest first" ([lib/trees.disp:1-24](../../lib/trees.disp)), so the challenge order is predictable. Hard-coding a large unary constant costs linear size, but a *computed* threshold such as "wrong when n > 2^2^2^2" is a small tree whose first failure no bounded search reaches. A diverging program is likewise often the smallest witness of a partial-correctness claim, because `partial_at` never refutes it ([lib/theories/cost.test.disp:35](../../lib/theories/cost.test.disp)).

| Lean exploit class under a size reward | disp status |
|---|---|
| Names, notation, comments, macros, token tricks | **Closed**, if measured on the elaborated tree |
| Citing environment additions for free (MDL collapse) | **Closed** on closed-tree size; **open** on session-marginal DAG size |
| Automation or reflection hiding work (short but slow) | **Default architecture**; capped by budget, never charged |
| `native_decide` / trusted compiled evaluation | **Mitigated** by differential natives; still trusted at run time |
| `sorry` / axioms | **Mitigated**: `checked.disp` rejects Open by default and labels "Assumed" ([lib/theories/checked.disp:33-61](../../lib/theories/checked.disp)); `use raw` and the caller-supplied lemma list remain trusted |
| `csimp`-style implementation swaps | **Worse**: trusted type slots, eight-probe coherence |
| Checker sniffing | **New**: pinned `Guard` lies; confirmer sound |
| Spec gaming / vacuous statements | **Unaddressed**, and cheaper than in Lean |
| Timeout treated as success | **Closed** in the verdict type; **open** at the designed 0/1 gate |
| Cost-metric gaming | **Open** in the implemented memo-dependent counter |
| Unproved trusted floor | The five rule lenses "have not themselves been proved sound" ([README.md:130-133](../../README.md)). [LOB_AND_CURRY.md](../LOB_AND_CURRY.md) says they are proved by hand, but no written proof was found |

## A resistant objective prices source, time, trust and claim together

Both systems end up needing the same recipe, built from theory and from the precedents above. Correctness and trust are hard gates. Resources are Pareto axes, not a weighted sum. Length breaks ties among artifacts that are otherwise equal, in the lexicographic way that worked for GP.

For Lean, the hard gates are:

- an exact axiom whitelist of `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` (which catches every per-call `_native` axiom and `sorryAx`);
- statement matching of the kind SafeVerify provided for ProofOptimizer;
- bans on `debug.*` options, `implemented_by`, `extern`, `unsafe` and `native_decide`;
- bans on search tactics left in the proof.

Every environment addition the submission makes (macros, attributes including `local simp`, instances, `set_option`) is either charged at full token cost or forbidden. A library lemma is amortized only across *other* theorems that use it, so a single-use helper gains nothing. Source tokens are paired with kernel `addDecl` heartbeats (synchronous, median of several runs) and with term DAG size after metavariables are instantiated and auxiliary constants expanded. Only Pareto improvements are rewarded. The flexible linter is enforced so that length pressure cannot buy brittleness. No existing tool packages this combined measure.

For disp the machinery is mostly already present, and the gaps are specific:

1. Define size as the fresh-hash-consed DAG count of the closed elaborated tree.
2. Return the spent fuel from `judge_with` and make it a second axis.
3. Gate on `== "Proved"` under the confirmer or a lie-detecting kernel tier, never on plain `Guard`, `param_apply == Ok true`, or a growth fit.
4. Disqualify any verdict that rests on "Assumed" entries, on `use raw` loads, or on types whose trusted slots are covered only by the eight probes.
5. Use the honest search as an exploit model for generated claims: require `by_search` to *refute* a fixed set of trivially wrong witnesses before a claim counts. This is the AlphaVerus defense from [CONSTRAINED_PROGRAM_SEARCH.md §6.7](../CONSTRAINED_PROGRAM_SEARCH.md), and in disp it is nearly free.
6. Never read `ApplyStats.steps` from a memoizing backend.

The cheapest experiments that would turn these inferences into evidence:

- Measure the tree-to-DAG ratio for real disp definitions such as `judge` and the kernel.
- Run a smallest-first search for kernel-certified witnesses under `Guard` and see how quickly it reaches `sniff_cod`.
- In Lean, elaborate the same lemmas through `simp`, `omega`, `grind` and explicit terms, and record `numObjs`, tree size and kernel heartbeats. No published measurement of this kind was found.

## Conclusion

The useful reframing is that in a proof assistant, length pressure cannot produce invalid proofs, only *displaced* ones. The choice of measure decides where the work hides, not whether it hides: in automation for text metrics, in the kernel for term metrics, in the compiler for time metrics, in the environment for library-relative metrics. Metamath, which has no automation, still had to guard trust and hypothesis strength, which shows that even a perfectly canonical size leaves gaming open along the axes it does not see. disp is the strongest version of that lesson. It achieves what a Lean reward designer would most want, a notation-free object with library content built in. Yet because its proofs *are* checker runs, "proof length" stops being a meaningful axis at all. The right objective in disp is not a smaller proof but a tuple: witness DAG size, checker fuel spent, change to the trusted base, and claim strength. Today only the first is even definable from the code.

The danger ranking also changes. Under size pressure in Lean, the worst outcome is a trusted-compiler axiom, which `#print axioms` exposes mechanically. In disp, the cheapest small artifacts are a two-tree `Guard` lie, a type whose `ends` slot is false outside eight probes, and a claim that is vacuously true. These are the hacks a reflective, self-describing language makes cheap, and they cannot be audited by listing axioms. That makes the honest search's refutation power the most valuable thing disp could add to a size-minimizing loop. Turning it on generated claims and generated types, not only on candidate programs, is what would make disp actually more resistant to these hacks than Lean rather than merely cleaner.
